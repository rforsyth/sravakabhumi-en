# Translator's notes: sbh2.2 (Aśubhā — The Unattractive)

The conduct-purifying object for the person of lust-conduct (9.3–9.4), the first of the five such objects (sbh2.2–2.6). The unattractive has six modes (1.4–1.9): putrid unattractiveness (*pratyaśubhatā*), taken inwardly as the parts of the body and outwardly as the corpse in decay (¶2–4), and the suffering, inferior, relative, defilement, and perishability modes (¶5–9). ¶10 distinguishes five kinds of lust, and ¶11–14 match each mode to the lust it purifies. 14.7–14.8 say that putrid unattractiveness is what is meant here, the other modes serving other conducts as well.
