# Translator's notes: sbh1.1 (Gotrabhūmi)

The stage of lineage (*gotra*): what makes a person fit for the disciple's path. The Sanskrit begins at 1.2, in the middle of the list of the five factors of endowment-from-self; the opening of the stage survives in Xuanzang's Chinese translation (T1579 juan 21). What survives gives the conditions under which the lineage is perfected (1.2–10.24), the marks of a person established in the lineage, stated as the opposites of six marks of one who is not (11.1–13.5), and the kinds of persons established in the lineage (14.1–26.1). The text breaks off at 26.1, at the definition of the person who is near.

## Structure

- 1.2–2.2. Endowment-from-self (*ātmasampat*), five factors: *manuṣyatva* (1.3–6; kept in Sanskrit because the text defines it as rebirth among humans with the male faculty), rebirth in a noble region (1.7–8), intactness of faculties (1.9–12), clarity directed to its sphere (1.13–18), unobstructed action (1.18–21). Endowment-from-others (*parasampat*), five: the arising of Buddhas, the teaching of the true dharma, its establishment, its further transmission, and the responsive support of others (listed 1.25–26, defined 1.27–2.2).
- 3.1–10.11. The course these make possible, each stage defined in turn: wholesome dharma-desire (3), going forth (4), restraint of virtue (5), restraint of the senses (6.1–7.8), knowing the right measure in food (7.9–14), application to wakefulness (8.1–3), dwelling with full awareness (8.4–6), seclusion (9), purification from hindrances (10.1–4), dependence on concentration, the four dhyānas (10.5–11).
- 10.12–10.24. The perfecting of the lineage and its supporting conditions; "the delineation of lineage" (10.24).
- 11.1–13.5. Six marks of one not of the nature for final nirvāṇa (11.12–13, 12.1–4, 12.5–9, 12.10–12, 12.13–17, 13.1–3); the marks of one established in the lineage are their opposites (11.3–6).
- 14.1–26.1. Persons established in the lineage, listed at 14.4–7 and defined in turn: only established, descended, gone out (15.1–18.2); of mild, middle, and sharp faculty (19.1–21.8); of lust-, hatred-, and delusion-conduct (21.9–23.2); born in an unfavorable moment, heedless, wrongly practiced, obscured, and their opposites (24); far (25); near (26.1, where the text breaks off; the definitions of the near, matured, and purified persons survive in Xuanzang, T1579 juan 21).
