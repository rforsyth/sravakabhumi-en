# Translator's notes: sbh1.1 (Gotrabhūmi)

The first of three sub-bhūmis within Yogasthāna I. The *gotrabhūmi* describes what makes a person *fit* for the disciple-vehicle path — the conditions that must be in place before descent into practice can begin. It moves from prerequisites (a viable rebirth, faculties, faith, no immediate-retribution karma, support from the dharma and the community) through the path that lineage makes possible (going forth, restraint, dhyāna, the four noble truths, liberation), and finally to identifying the person-types that have the lineage versus those who do not.

## Structural overview

The gotrabhūmi is the first of three sub-bhūmis within Yogasthāna I, framing what makes a person *fit* for the disciple-vehicle path before the descent (avatāra) begins. It moves through three concentric rings:

1. **Conditions for being a viable practitioner** (1.1–1.26): the eight factors of *ātmasampat* (endowment-from-self) and the four factors of *parasampat* (endowment-from-others). These together constitute the prerequisite ground — a human birth in a noble locale, intact faculties, faith in the dharma-vinaya, no immediate-retribution karma; plus the arising of Buddhas, established teachings, transmission, and material support.
2. **The path that lineage makes possible** (3.1–10.24): wholesome dharma-desire → going forth → restraint → senses → measure in food → wakefulness → full awareness → seclusion → hindrance-purification → samādhi → the four dhyānas → the noble truths → liberation → final nirvāṇa. This is the *gotra-samudāgama* — what the lineage matures into when the conditions hold.
3. **Identifying who has the lineage** (11.1–25.1): six marks of one *not* in the lineage; person-types within the lineage (mild/middle/sharp faculty, lust/hatred/delusion conduct, far/near, matured/not), with definitions for most categories.

The bhūmi ends mid-list — see "Abrupt ending" below.

## Pattern of textual issues

Shukla 1973 reflects manuscript variants and PDF-extraction artifacts that show up across sbh1.1 in three patterns:

- **Scribal duplications.** sbh1.1:18.2 has `nātyarthaṁ ... atyarthaṁ dhandhavāhīni` where only `atyarthaṁ dhandhavāhīni` makes sense — the leading `nātyarthaṁ` is almost certainly copied from the *madhyendriya* entry in 19.2. Read here without the *na*; otherwise mild and middle faculty become indistinguishable.
- **Lacunae.** sbh1.1:12.5 has a marked gap (`..............`) in the source. Translated through the gap as `…`; the surrounding context (`anekaparyāyeṇa … gāmī-duḥkha-apatrāpyeṇa samanvāgataḥ`) suggests a missing modifier like *durgati-* before *gāmī*.
- **Compound boundary errors.** The migration from monolithic `sbh1` collapsed the long fault-list at paragraphs 12.10–12.11 into one segment, leaving 12.11 a fragment ("Action — taking up the past, future, or present"). Restored at the source's `|` boundary in the quality pass.

## Major terminology decisions

A few choices warrant cross-cutting explanation:

- **`ātmasampat` / `parasampat` → "endowment-from-self" / "endowment-from-others"** (1.22–1.26). Earlier draft used "fullness of self / of the other," which reads strangely in English (the "other" sounds like a single counterpart rather than a category of contributing factors). The adopted hyphenated form makes the structural pairing audible and clarifies that *parasampat* covers Buddhas, teachings, transmission, and lay support collectively.
- **`ātmabhāva` → "individual existence"** (1.22, 1.28), kept distinct from *ātmasampat*. *ātmabhāva* names the body-and-mind continuum that gets brought to fulfillment; *ātmasampat* names the eight-factor profile that makes that continuum a viable practice ground. Conflating the two (some translations do) loses the structural argument that one's continuum *is the substrate for* the eight factors, not identical to them.
- **`parataḥ pratyanukampā` → "the responsive support of others"** (1.26, 2.1–2.2). "Compassion from another" reads coldly and abstractly; the source clearly means concrete material support (robes, alms-food, lodging, medicine) given by donors and patrons in response to a renunciant's situation. *Pratyanukampā* (`prati-anu-kampā`) carries both the "responsive" and "supportive" senses; "responsive support" preserves both.
- **`(a)parivṛttakarmāntatā` → "(un)obstructed action"** (1.18, 1.21). Kept the conventional rendering despite the looser fit. Literally "having (not) had the karma-end turned-around/sealed" — referring specifically to the five immediate-retribution actions whose karmic consequences are held to be irreversible in this very life. "Sealed" or "irreversible" would be more literal but less recognizable in the broader Buddhist-translation literature.
- **`indriyāvaikalya` → "intactness of the faculties"** (1.12), harmonized with `indriyairavikalatā` ("having faculties intact") at 1.2 and 1.9. The source uses two related forms (one a noun, one an adverbial); collapsed to a single English idiom for readability.

## Abrupt ending at 25.1

The file ends with the bare phrase "The person who is near," — the last item in the list at 14.4–14.7 that gets *named* but not given its own *tatra X katamaḥ* definition block. This isn't an editorial truncation: the source itself transitions directly from `āsanne pudgalaḥ |` to `II. avatārabhūmiḥ — yaiḥ ṣaḍbhir bhavyo…` (now [sbh1.2:1.1](../../docs/sbh1.2.html#sbh1.2:1.1)). The "near" person is defined indirectly by everything that follows: avatārabhūmi *is* the description of how a lineage-bearer becomes near to nirvāṇa.

The "matured / not matured" pair (also enumerated at 14.7 but not separately defined) is treated the same way — picked up by the six-factors framework at the start of avatārabhūmi.

A clean re-read of sbh1.1 should therefore continue into sbh1.2 without pause; the bhūmi headings mark a doctrinal pivot, not a content discontinuity.
