# Translator's notes: sbh1.2 (Avatārabhūmi)

The second sub-bhūmi of Yogasthāna I. *Avatārabhūmi* describes the *descent* (*avatāra*) into the path — the transition from being merely *fit* (the gotrabhūmi end-state) into actively *practicing*. Six person-types map the journey from latent fitness to arhatship; eight marks identify what a descended practitioner looks like in life.

## Structural overview

The avatārabhūmi is the second of three sub-bhūmis within Yogasthāna I. Its job is to describe what *descent* (*avatāra*) into the path looks like — the transition from being merely *fit* for the path (the gotrabhūmi end-state) into actively *practicing* it. The chapter unfolds in four blocks:

1. **Six person-types** (1.1–1.27): *bhavya* (merely fit), three grades of wholesome roots (mild / middle / strong), *niṣṭhā-prāyogika* (applied to the ultimate), *niṣṭhā-gata* (arrived at the ultimate). Each gets its own `tatra X katamaḥ` block. These six are the canonical map of the disciple-vehicle path from latent fitness through arhatship.
2. **Path summary through the six person-types** (2.1–2.13, 3.1–4.13): how a single practitioner moves through all six categories sequentially — gotra → mild → middle → strong → applied → arrived. The "three births" minimum and "seven births" maximum framework appears here.
3. **Eight marks of the descended person** (4.14–10.4): identification criteria. Marks 1 (response to dharma) and 4 (shame and dread) are the longest; the rest are short.
4. **Closing material** (11.1–19.1): summary remarks, gradient by wholesome-root grade, observation that only Buddhas and protector-disciples directly perceive lineage and descent, types of descended persons, cross-reference to the sub-categories from the gotrabhūmi, the *uddāna* summary verse, and the colophon.

## Pattern of textual issues

Avatārabhūmi has fewer manuscript pathologies than gotrabhūmi but more *interpretive* difficulties — places where the Sanskrit is well-attested but the meaning depends on which referent the pronouns and similes attach to.

- **The honey-creature simile at 4.20** is the largest interpretive crux in this chapter. The Sanskrit explicitly says the simile concerns the *well-stated* dharma-vinaya — which is counterintuitive in a passage about the descended person's marks (the descended person *is* drawn to the well-stated dharma, per 4.21+). The translation reads 4.20 as describing the *un-descended state* against which the descended person is contrasted: the un-descended person, like a honey-creature thrown into vinegar, cannot connect with the well-stated dharma until descent has occurred. The descended person, by contrast (4.21+), is like the honey-creature in honey. This reading preserves the literal Sanskrit and makes the contrast structure work; an alternate reading that emends *svākhyātasya* to *durākhyātasya* is unnecessary.
- **The *yāvat* shorthand at 1.9, 1.11** ('and so on up through ...') is rendered explicitly rather than left as 'Up to:'. Standard Buddhist-text convention: *yāvat* before a milestone means 'all the elaboration above through to this point.'
- **The three-clause question at 4.1–4.3** about whether final-nirvāṇa attainment is on a fixed timetable was rendered as three separate questions in the original draft, obscuring that they are one inquiry with three angles. Punctuation revised so the question structure is visible: 4.1 asks about fixed measure, 4.2 about not-fixed, 4.3 about certainty across persons.

## Major terminology decisions

- **`ātmabhāva` → 'individual existence'** (1.14, 1.18, 2.6) — kept distinct from *ātmasampat* in [sbh1.1](sbh1.1.html), per the convention established there. The 'final individual existence' (*caramam ātmabhāvam*) in 1.14 and 1.18 is the last birth in the saṁsāric series for the practitioner reaching arhatship — *carama* is positional in the rebirth-series, not biographical.
- **`paramaśītī-bhāva-prāpta` → 'attained supreme calming'** (1.26). Earlier draft 'attained supreme cool-becoming' is a literal calque on the Sanskrit-compound shape but reads strangely in English. *Śīta* (cool) is the standard Buddhist metaphor for nirvāṇa as the cessation of the kleśas' burning; English 'calming' and 'peace' both reach for the same conceptual space. 'Cooling' would be more literal but loses the religious register.
- **`niṣṭhā-prāyogika` / `niṣṭhā-gata` → 'applied to the ultimate' / 'arrived at the ultimate'** (1.17, 1.22). *Niṣṭhā* is the consummation, the final endpoint — *prāyogika* names the one in active application toward it; *gata* the one who has arrived. 'Ultimate' is preferred over 'completion' or 'culmination' because it preserves the goal-orientation in the noun. The extracted Sanskrit at 1.20 lacks the expected negator before *āsrava-kṣayam anuprāpnoti*; because 1.21 and 2.9–2.13 explicitly say this person has not reached arhatship, the English restores the negative sense and marks the segment [Damaged/Conjectural].
- **`āsrava-kṣaya` → 'destruction of the fluxes'** (1.18, 1.20, 1.24, 1.25, 2.12). *Āsrava* (Pali *āsava*) is the canonical 'influxes' / 'taints' / 'cankers' — the four (sense-desire, becoming, view, ignorance) whose extinction marks arhatship. 'Fluxes' is the Sanskrit-leaning rendering; 'taints' or 'cankers' would be the Pali-derived alternative. Either is defensible.
- **`niṣkrānta` → 'gone out'** (14.3–15.2; cf. sbh1.1:14.4–18.2) — kept distinct from *pravrajita/pravrajyā* ('gone forth') because the taxonomy is about gaining the noble path and fruit, not ordination. This is intentionally literal English for a path-status category.
- **The eight 'marks' / *liṅgāni*** (sections 3–9) are observational identifiers, not requirements — they describe how a descended person typically behaves. Translation uses 'marks' consistently rather than mixing with 'signs' or 'characteristics.'

## Path-structure note: the bhavya–niṣṭhāgata sequence

The six person-types in section 1 trace the disciple's progression. They aren't six independent categories — they're six *stages* the same person passes through:

| Stage | Person-type | Marker |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-descent | *bhavya* (merely fit) | First gains faith and makes view upright |
| Descent stage 1 | *mṛdu-kuśalamūla* (mild roots) | Same as *bhavya* but established |
| Descent stage 2 | *madhya-kuśalamūla* (middle roots) | Produces 1, 2, or several further births toward distinction |
| Descent stage 3 | *adhimātra-kuśalamūla* (strong roots) | Attains the final individual existence and reaches nirvāṇa from there |
| Practice | *niṣṭhā-prāyogika* (applied) | Realizes 1st–3rd path-fruits but not yet arhatship |
| Arrival | *niṣṭhā-gata* (arrived) | Realizes arhatship — task done, supreme calming attained |

Section 2.1–2.13 makes the sequence explicit. Section 3.10–3.16 adds the temporal framework: minimum three births (one for descent, one for ripening, one for release), maximum seven births. The rest of the chapter (3.17–9.4) addresses the practical question: *how do we recognize who has descended?*
