# Translator's notes: sbh3.0 (Preamble — Approaching the Teacher)

The opening preamble of Yogasthāna III — the practitioner-teacher relationship that must be in place before the nine-stage *śamatha* of [sbh3.1](sbh3.1.html) can begin. Covers the qualifications of a *kalyāṇamitra* who actually knows the yoga (*yoga-jña*) and the four roles such a teacher plays; the teacher's examination of the practitioner by lineage / faculty / conduct; the daily routine the disciple takes up under instruction; and the *kāya-viveka* / *citta-viveka* (bodily and mental withdrawal) that prepare the ground for the dhyānas. Closes with a fourfold definition of *samādhi* that grounds the *cittasthiti* analysis at sbh3.1.

The preamble has two source paragraphs:

1. **Paragraph 1 (large):** approaching a knower-of-yoga, the qualifications such a teacher should have, the manner of examination (lineage / faculty / conduct), the daily routine of the practitioner who has come under instruction, and bodily withdrawal (*kāya-viveka*).
2. **Paragraph 2:** mental withdrawal (*citta-viveka*) and a fourfold definition of *samādhi* (anugata-saṁjñā, kuśala-saṁjñā, saṁyoga-saṁjñā, anavadya-saṁjñā).

After this preamble the doctrinal body of Yog III continues in sbh3.1 (nine stages of mental abiding, śamatha framework) and sbh3.2 (vipaśyanā framework).

## Structural overview

The preamble is gate-keeping: the practitioner needs a teacher before the nine-stage practice of sbh3.1 can begin, and the teacher needs the right qualifications. The Yogācāra criteria are extensive:

- **The teacher's qualifications** (1.5–1.40): *kalyāṇamitra* characteristics (much-learned, an attainer, compassionate, possessed of antidotes), four roles (*sandarśaka, samādāpaka, samuttejaka, saṁpraharṣaka*), and the prerequisite of being a *yoga-jña* — one who actually knows the yoga and not merely the doctrine.
- **Examination of the practitioner** (1.40–1.100): the teacher determines lineage, faculty (mild / middle / sharp), conduct (lust / hatred / delusion / pride / vitarka / balanced / slight-dust), and applies the appropriate *carita-viśodhana* practice from sbh2.x.
- **Daily routine after instruction** (1.100–1.180): how the disciple structures the day — first watch of the night walking and sitting to purify mind from obscuring dharmas; middle watch lying down on the right side as the Tathāgata does, *āloka-saṁjñā* and *utthāna-saṁjñā* established; third watch returning to walking and sitting; *jāgarikā-yoga* throughout.
- **Bodily withdrawal** (1.180–1.214): *kāya-viveka* — not dwelling with householders or other renunciants; the right kind of dwelling-place; restraint of body and senses on the alms-round.
- **Mental withdrawal and concentration** (2.1–2.31): *citta-viveka* — avoiding defiled and indeterminate mental engagement; the fourfold definition of *samādhi* under the *prātimokṣa-saṁvara* heading: by conformable object, by wholesome object, by yoke-to-continuity, by yoke-to-blameless-delight.

## Why this is a preamble and not a "topic"

The Yogācāra structure of Yog III is body-content first (śamatha at sbh3.1, vipaśyanā at sbh3.2), with the closing uddāna and colophon coming after sbh3.2's body. The preamble is a setup that orients the practitioner toward the body content rather than being itself a doctrinal topic. The teacher-and-method content here is a precondition for the practice — once one has located a teacher and stabilized the daily routine, the nine stages of mental abiding at sbh3.1 become available.

Compare sbh2.0's preamble, which similarly serves as a framework rather than a topic: sbh2.0 prepares the seventeen-question architecture of Yog II, sbh3.0 prepares the practitioner-teacher relationship of Yog III. Yog I has no preamble (the text begins with `I. gotrabhūmiḥ`); Yog IV has no preamble (sbh4.1 opens directly with `IV. caturthaṁ yogasthānam` followed immediately by the seven-attentions content).

## Cross-references

- The lineage / faculty / conduct examination at 1.40–1.100 inherits the typology from sbh1.1 (lineage faculties), sbh2.0:2 (faculty / conduct classifications of the 28-person typology), and sbh2.1–2.7 (the five carita-viśodhana practices).
- The *kalyāṇamitra* characteristics at 1.5–1.40 echo the *kalyāṇamitratā* requisite at sbh1.3:48–55, but framed here as criteria for the teacher rather than as a quality of the practitioner.
- The fourfold *samādhi* definition at 2.20–2.31 is the same definition that grounds the nine-stage *cittasthiti* analysis at sbh3.1.

## Major terminology decisions

- **`kalyāṇamitra` → 'good friend'** throughout, consistent with sbh1.3. The four roles (*sandarśaka, samādāpaka, samuttejaka, saṁpraharṣaka*) are rendered functionally: 'demonstrator, exhorter, arouser, gladdener.'
- **`yoga-jña` → 'knower of yoga'** rather than 'yogi' — the term emphasizes the teacher's *knowledge* of the practice, paired with the practitioner who is the *yoga-prayukta* ('engaged in yoga'). This is a deliberate non-trivialization of *yoga* as a relationship between knowledge and engagement.
- **`kāya-viveka` / `citta-viveka` → 'bodily withdrawal' / 'mental withdrawal'** — preserving the parallel and matching the standard convention. The pair functions as a single twofold *viveka* preparing the ground for *prāvivekya* (the state of seclusion that supports the dhyānas).
- **`pratimukha smṛti`** in 1.150ff. (the standard sitting-formula posture) is rendered 'mindfulness to the fore' — same as the sbh1.3 convention.

## A note on the daily routine description

The description of the practitioner's daily routine (1.100–1.180) is one of the more practically detailed sections in the entire Śrāvakabhūmi. It pairs phrasally with the *jāgarikānuyoga* requisite at sbh1.3:42 (which gives the same routine as one of the twelve requisites). Here the same routine appears in the *pragmatic* frame — what the disciple actually does day by day — rather than the *qualitative* frame of sbh1.3 (what *jāgarikā* means as a requisite).
