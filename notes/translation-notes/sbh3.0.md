# Translator's notes: sbh3.0 (Approaching the Teacher)

The opening of the third yogasthāna. A beginner approaches a knower of yoga (*yogajña*) and asks for instruction (1.2–8); the teacher encourages him (1.9–31), puts four questions (1.32–33), and examines him as to vow, lineage, faculty, and conduct by four means: question, talk, behavior, and knowledge of others' minds (1.34–103). He then trains him in five matters (1.104–105), of which this section treats the first two, protection and accumulation of the requisites of concentration (1.106–114) and seclusion (1.115–2.6), and begins the third, one-pointedness of mind, defined as concentration (2.7–31). The sentence that begins at 2.31 continues at [sbh3.1](sbh3.1.html) 1.1.

## Structure

- 1.1. The frame: the establishments of the second yogasthāna, from persons to the fruit of cultivation, are complete.
- 1.2–1.8. The approach, with mindfulness in four respects (1.2–3), and the request (1.7–8).
- 1.9–1.31. Encouragement, and praise of abandonment (1.12–30).
- 1.32–1.33. Four questions: refuge; purity of the holy life, of virtue, and of view; the noble truths; going forth for nirvāṇa.
- 1.34–1.103. Examination by question (1.38–60: vow, 1.38–44; lineage, faculty, and conduct, 1.45–60), by talk (1.61–96: lineage, 1.62–69; faculty, 1.70–76; conduct — lust, hatred, delusion, conceit, applied thought — 1.77–94), by behavior (1.97–100), by knowledge of others' minds (1.101–103).
- 1.104–1.105. The five matters of training: protection and accumulation of the requisites of concentration, seclusion, one-pointedness of mind, purification of hindrances, cultivation of attention.
- 1.106–1.114. Protection and accumulation of the requisites, which are those listed at sbh1.3:2.3 (1.110).
- 1.115–2.6. Seclusion (*prāvivekya*): fullness of place (1.116–147; fivefold from 1.123), fullness of posture (1.148–175), withdrawal, bodily (1.176–181) and mental (2.1–3); the four summed as seclusion (2.4–6).
- 2.7–2.31. One-pointedness is concentration: a mental continuum of recurrent mindfulness with a conformable object, yoked to continuity and blameless delight (2.9–10); the elements glossed in turn — recurrent recollection (2.12–16), conformable object (2.17–25), continuity (2.26–27), blameless delight (2.28–29); the definition restated (2.30–31).
