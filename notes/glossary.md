# Translation glossary

Controlled English renderings of recurring Sanskrit technical terms. Each entry gives the rendering, the trigger for each rendering where there is more than one, worked examples from the current translation, and adjacent terms not to be conflated. Section-wide renderings settled in `editorial-conventions.md` §9 are repeated first.

## *aśubha* / *aśubhatā* — "unattractive" / "unattractiveness"

Rendered **"unattractive"** / **"unattractiveness"**. *Aśubhā* as the name of the practice may be kept in Sanskrit (sbh3.2:34.13, 54.1). In the sixfold list of sbh2.2, once 1.4 has named the quality, **"mode"** stands in for *-aśubhatā* in the remaining members (1.5–1.9, "the suffering mode …"). The first member, *pratyaśubhatā*, is **"putrid unattractiveness"** (sbh2.2:1.4, with its note). Not used: "foulness", "impurity", "repulsiveness", "inherent".

## *ālambana* — "object"

The object of cognition or meditation, what the mind takes as its referent. Rendered **"object"**; not "support". *Ālambana-vastu* is **"object"** (sbh2.1:1.3 "four objects (*ālambana-vastu*)"), or "the thing taken as object" where the *vastu* is in focus. *Vastu* on its own is "entity" in its technical sense and "thing" for an ordinary item (see the *vastu* entry).

|---|---|

| *ālambana-vastu* (sbh2.1:1.3) | object |
| *ālambana-pariśuddhi* (sbh2.1:15.11) | purity-of-object |
| *bāhya ālambana* (sbh3.1:3.2) | outer object |
| *ālambana-nimitta* (sbh3.2:32.1) | object-sign |

## *abhinirhāra* — "bringing forth" / "manifestation"

Rendered **"bringing forth"** where the verbal force is dominant (sbh4.2:13.1 "the bringing forth of the five higher knowledges"; sbh4.1:3.8) and **"manifestation"** as a noun (sbh4.3:1.1). Not "projection".

## *vastu* — "entity" / "the six investigation-subjects" / "thing"

Three senses, three renderings. Revised 2026-10-23; before that "thing" throughout.

1. **The technical referent: "entity".** The real item a teaching points to, as against its name or meaning (*artha*) and before anything is said about its characteristics. This is the second of the six investigation-subjects (sbh3.2:4.4 "meaning, entity (*vastu*), characteristic, side, time, and reasoning"; 5.4 "The entity is twofold: inward and outward"), and the same sense in *jñeya-vastu* **"knowable entity"** (sbh2.1:4.1; sbh3.2:31.9 "the image conformable to the knowable entity"), *vastu-paryantatā* **"the full extent of the entity"** (sbh2.1:7.1; sbh3.2:6.8), *vastu-mātra* **"mere entity"** (sbh2.4:1.2; sbh2.7:11.3 "only the entity"), and the inward and outward *vastu* of the impermanence contemplation (sbh4.3:6.7 "the inward entity is the six sense-bases; the outward entity is sixteenfold", with the sixteen as "earth-entity", "park-entity", and so on). One word is used for all of these so the reader can see that the pervasive object of sbh2.1, the second investigation-subject of sbh3.2, and the inward/outward sweep of sbh4.3 are one idea.
2. **The list-word: "the six investigation-subjects".** *ṣaḍ-vastu*, *ṣaḍ-vastu-prabheda* (sbh3.2:3.1, 4.1, 4.3, 6.1, 7.1, 17.21, 29.6, 31.14; sbh4.1:5.7, 13.1; sbh4.2:1.5). The Sanskrit uses *vastu* both for the list and for its second member; rendering the two alike ("six kinds of things" / "the thing") read as confusion in English, so the list-word takes the descriptive phrase.
3. **An ordinary item: "thing".** A lust-arousing thing (sbh2.0:3.2), the things one gives (sbh1.3:69–72), the tools of a craft (sbh3.2:45.2), the six things possessed (sbh4.3:18.36).

Why "entity" and not "thing" or "substance". Deleanu has "thing" for the second investigation-subject and "[real] thing" / "thing-in-itself" for *vastu* and *vastu-mātra* (2006, 3.28.2.1.2 and n. 106–107, after Frauwallner's *Ding an sich*); Xuanzang has 事 (義、事、相、品、時、理, T1579 juan 30). "Thing" is faithful but too weak to register as a term in a practice manual, and it collides with the list-word. "Substance" fits the contrast with meaning (substance against wording) but in the demonstrations the word is a locatable count noun ("an outward *vastu*", sbh3.2:19.2; "lovingkindness in the *vastu*", 19.3), where "substance" slides to underlying stuff, which this school denies. "Entity" is the usual English for the Tibetan translators' *dngos po*, carries a technical register, works in every sentence the text needs, and says nothing about permanence. Glossed once, at sbh3.2:4.4.

### Adjacent terms (not to be conflated)

- *ālambana* — "object" (see entry above)
- *artha* — "meaning"; the first investigation-subject, paired against *vastu* as description against referent
- *dravya* — not used in this text; would be "substance"

## *audārika* / *audārikatā* — "gross" / "grossness"

Rendered **"gross"** (adjective) and **"grossness"** (noun); not "coarse". The paired term *śānta* is **"calm"** (sbh4.1:3.5 "the grossness of sense-pleasures and the calm of the first *dhyāna*").

### Worked examples

| Sanskrit | English |
|---|---|
| *audārika* | gross |
| *audārikatā* | grossness |
| *audārika-lakṣaṇa* (sbh4.2:1.1) | gross characteristic |
| *audārika-* / *śānta-lakṣaṇa* (sbh4.1:13.1) | gross-characteristic / calm-characteristic |

### Adjacent terms (not to be conflated)

- *śānta* — "calm"
- *dauṣṭhulya* — "encumbrance" (see entry below); a different opposition from gross / calm

## *bhūtatā* / *tathatā* / *tathābhūtatā*

- ***bhūtatā*** — **"actuality"** (sbh2.1:7.8)
- ***tathatā*** — **"suchness"** (sbh2.1:7.9); not "thusness"
- ***tathābhūtatā*** — **"such-actuality"** (sbh3.2:5.37)

sbh2.1:7.8–7.10 defines *yathāvad-bhāvikatā* as the object's *bhūtatā* and its *tathatā* with the four reasonings.

### Adjacent terms (not to be conflated)

- *yathābhūta* — "as it actually is" (conventions §9); modifies verbs of knowing and seeing
- *dharmatā* — "nature of things" (*dharmatā-yukti* "nature-of-things reasoning", sbh3.2:5.26)

## *dauṣṭhulya* — "encumbrance"

Rendered **"encumbrance"**. Its opposite is *praśrabdhi* "pliancy": as encumbrance departs, pliancy arises (sbh3.2:56.6–56.8).

## *manuṣyatva* — retained untranslated

Rendered ***manuṣyatva***, with the gloss "(human-male birth)" at its first occurrence (sbh1.1:1.3). The text defines it at 1.4–1.5 as rebirth in the community of humans together with the male faculty; "being human" would make that definition tautologous, so the Sanskrit is kept.

### Worked examples

| Sanskrit | English |
|---|---|
| *manuṣyatva* (sbh1.1:1.3) | *manuṣyatva* (human-male birth) |
| *manuṣyāṇāṁ sabhāgatāyāṁ pratyājāta* (sbh1.1:1.4) | reborn in the community of humans |
| *puruṣaḥ puruṣendriyeṇa samanvāgataḥ* (sbh1.1:1.5) | is male, endowed with the male faculty |

### Adjacent terms (not to be conflated)

- *manuṣya* — "human"
- *puruṣa* — "male"; *puruṣendriya* — "male faculty"
- *ātmabhāva* — "individual existence" (conventions §9)
- *ātmasampat* — "endowment-from-self" (sbh1.1:1.23)

## *saṁskāra* — "formation(s)"; *kāya-saṁskāra* "body-conditioner"

Default rendering **"formation"** / **"formations"**. In the breath practice, *kāya-saṁskāra* is **"body-conditioner"** and *citta-saṁskāra* **"mind-conditioner"**; *abhisaṁskāra* in the nine-stage and path contexts is **"effort"**. Not "volitional formation", "fabrication".

| Compound / sense | English |
|---|---|
| *saṁskāra-skandha* (sbh2.7:6.1) | formations-aggregate |
| *saṁskāra*, second link of dependent origination (sbh2.7:16.3) | formations |
| *saṁskāra-duḥkhatā* | suffering of conditioning |
| *kāya-saṁskāra* (sbh2.6:5.3; the breath tetrad, fourth step, 14.8) | body-conditioner |
| *citta-saṁskāra* (sbh2.6:14.11) | mind-conditioner |
| *abhisaṁskāra* / *anabhisaṁskāra* (sbh3.1:9.2, 10.5) | with effort / without effort |

## Apparatus markers

Departures from Shukla's printed text are marked inline in the English, one marker per departure, with a one-line note naming the witness. The convention is in `editorial-conventions.md` §2; in brief:

| Marker | Use |
|---|---|
| `[Lacuna]` | text missing from the printed edition; nothing supplied |
| `[Damaged]` | text present but unreadable; nothing supplied |
| `[Conjectural: …]` | supplied by the translator, supported by the context or by Xuanzang |
| `[Restored: …]` | a Sanskrit reading attested in a critical edition and absent from, or misprinted in, Shukla |

Markers contain English only. A `[Lacuna]` that falls between segments is placed at the end of the segment before the gap. An obvious misprint gets the note but no marker.
