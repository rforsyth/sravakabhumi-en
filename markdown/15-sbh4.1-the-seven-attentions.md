# The Seven Attentions

*Śrāvakabhūmi · sbh4.1*

## IV. The Fourth Yogasthāna

Of the practitioner who has obtained attention, who has thus entered into limited delight in abandonment, beyond that, two courses lie open — none other. What two? Namely: the mundane and the supramundane. This beginning practitioner, who possesses attention, thinks: 'Shall I go by the mundane course, or by the supramundane?' — and cultivates that very attention. Inasmuch as he cultivates, the pliancy and one-pointedness of mind, by the passing of those days and nights, go to expansion, growth, abundance. When his attention has become [Conjectural: firm], steady, and keen, and his [Restored: purified] resolve on the object proceeds, and the *śamatha-vipaśyanā* signs have been grasped — then, wishing to go by the mundane path, he begins the practice there; or by the supramundane path.

How many persons, in this very life, go by the mundane path alone,

not by the supramundane?

It is said: Four. Namely: the wholly outsider; even within the dharma, one who is dull and has previously practised *śamatha*; likewise one who is [Restored: keen] but of unripe wholesome roots; a bodhisattva wishing to attain *bodhi* in the future, not in this very life. These four persons go by the mundane-path-vehicle in this very life.

The mundane-path travel itself is twofold: that of the completely-bound ordinary persons, and that of the partially-bound, the trainees.

What is it?

Seeing the grossness of sense-pleasures and [Conjectural: the calm] of the first *dhyāna*, with its attainment and rebirth — that is the going to dispassion toward sense-pleasures. Thus, up through — dispassion toward the sphere of nothingness is to be known. Likewise the unconscious attainment, and, supported by the *dhyāna* attainments, the bringing forth of the five [Restored: higher knowledges].

The practitioner engaged in dispassion toward sense-pleasure attains *kāma-vairāgya* through seven attentions. He attains *kāma-vairāgya*.

What seven attentions?

It is said: The characteristic-knowing, the resolve-born, the seclusion, the delight-gathering; the examination attention; the practice-culminating, and the fruit-of-practice-culmination.

What is the characteristic-knowing attention?

It is said: The attention by which the practitioner comprehends the gross characteristic of sense-pleasures, and the calm characteristic of the first *dhyāna*.

How does he comprehend the gross characteristic?

It is said: Investigating the six investigation-subjects (*vastu*) of sense-pleasures: [Restored: meaning, entity, characteristic,] side, time, and reasoning.

Investigating the gross meaning: 'These sense-pleasures are with-fault, with-many-troubles, with-many-afflictions, with-many-disturbances.' The much-fault-character of these sense-pleasures, up to much-disturbance-character — this is the gross meaning.

Investigating the entity: 'There is, inwardly, sense-desire toward sense-pleasures; [Restored: there is, outwardly, sense-desire toward sense-pleasures].'

Investigating the specific characteristic: 'These are the defilement-sense-pleasures (*kleśa-kāma*); these are the thing-sense-pleasures (*vastu-kāma*). They are in turn bases for pleasant feeling, bases for painful feeling, or bases for neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling. The bases for pleasant feeling are the basis of *kāma-rāga*, the basis of perception-and-mind-perversion. The bases for painful feeling are the basis of hatred, the basis of anger and resentment. The bases for neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling are the basis of concealment, spite, deceit, fraud, shamelessness, and lack of dread of blame, and the basis of view-perversion. Thus these sense-pleasures are accompanied by depraved feelings and by [Conjectural: depraved] defilements.' Thus he investigates the specific characteristic of sense-pleasures.

How does he investigate the general characteristic?

'All these sense-pleasures, together with the suffering-of-birth, the suffering-of-aging, up through the suffering-of-thwarted-desire, are equally bound and bound-together. Even those sense-pleasure-enjoyers who proceed in great sense-pleasure-abundance — they too are not free from the *dharmatā* of birth-and-the-rest. Their abundance is short-lived.' Thus he investigates the general characteristic.

How does he investigate the side?

'These sense-pleasures fall on the dark side. They are like a skeleton, like a piece of meat, like a grass-torch; like a coal-pit; like a venomous snake; like a dream; like borrowed ornaments; like [Restored: fruit on a tree]. Even beings seeking sense-pleasures experience the suffering of the seeking. They experience the suffering of guarding, of loss-of-affection, of dissatisfaction, of non-autonomy, of misconduct. All this is to be understood as before. So also for one indulging in sense-pleasures. Five faults have been spoken of by the Blessed One: 'Sense-pleasures give little gratification, much suffering, much fault.' 'For one indulging in sense-pleasures, there is no sufficiency, satisfaction, or satiation. By this enumeration, sense-pleasures are reproached by the Buddhas, by the Buddha's disciples, by the good, the rightly accomplished, the true persons.' 'To one indulging in sense-pleasures, the fetters go to accumulation.' 'I say that there is no evil, unwholesome deed whatever that such a one would not do.' 'These sense-pleasures are dissatisfaction-makers, [Restored: common to many], causes of unrighteous and wicked conduct; increasers of sense-craving, to be avoided by the good, [Conjectural: swiftly perishing], [Restored: dependent on conditions], grounds of heedlessness, hollow, impermanent, vain, of a false and deceptive nature, illusion-like, deceiving fools.' Whether sense-pleasures of this very life, or of the next life, whether divine or human — [Restored: this is the domain of] Māra, this is Māra's bait, where these manifold evil, unwholesome mental dharmas arise: namely covetousness, ill-will, violence — or whatever else is obstructive to a noble disciple in training. In many ways these sense-pleasures fall, for the most part, on the dark side. Thus he investigates the side.

[Conjectural: How does he investigate time?] 'Across past, future, and present times, these sense-pleasures are [Conjectural: constantly], at all times, [Restored: perpetually], always thus with-many-troubles, with-many-afflictions, with-many-faults.' Thus he investigates time.

How does he investigate reasoning?

'With great endeavor, with great seeking, with great labor, by various assorted craftsmanship-and-occupations, sense-pleasures are gathered. They are generated, accumulated. Even when well accumulated and well generated, they serve only — outwardly, for [the suffering] of the things one possesses: mother, father, son, wife, female slave, male slave, worker, servant, friend, minister, kinsman, and blood-relation; or of this inward body — of form, gross, made of the four great elements, built up on boiled rice and gruel, subject to constant anointing, washing, rubbing, breaking, cutting, scattering, and destruction — merely to counteract suffering as it arises. Food is for counteracting the suffering of hunger. For warding off the suffering of cold and heat, and for covering the private parts, clothing. And for warding off the suffering of drowsiness and fatigue, bedding and seat, and for warding off the suffering of walking and standing. And for warding off the suffering of illness, medicine for the sick. Thus these sense-pleasures are counteractions to suffering.' 'These are not to be enjoyed with attachment, nor with clinging — but only [Restored: as] a sick man afflicted with illness [takes] medicine merely to calm the illness. This is also trustworthy tradition: [Restored: that] these sense-pleasures are gross thus and thus.' 'Direct knowing-and-seeing also arises in me.' 'There is also this inferential rule.' It is also the nature of sense-pleasures — beginningless, established-dharma, unthinkable-dharma. It is not to be thought. It is not to be discriminated. Thus he investigates reasoning.

Having thus comprehended the gross-characteristic of sense-pleasures, namely by the six investigation-subjects, he comprehends the calm-characteristic in the first *dhyāna*: 'There is not at all this grossness in the first *dhyāna* that there is in the desire-realm.' [Restored: Being free from this grossness, it is calm. Thus he comprehends the calm-characteristic in it. The attention of the concentrated plane by which he comprehends the gross-characteristic in sense-pleasures] and the calm-characteristic in the first *dhyāna* — this is called the characteristic-knowing attention. This attention is to be known as mixed with hearing-and-thinking.

Having thus fully understood sense-pleasures and the first *dhyāna* by the correctly investigated gross-and-calm characteristic, he transcends hearing and thinking and resolves purely by the cultivation-aspect alone. He cultivates *śamatha-vipaśyanā* with that very sign as object. And as, while cultivating, he resolves again and again on that grossness-and-calm [Restored: as investigated] — [Restored: this is called] the resolve-born attention.

Through familiarization, cultivation, and frequent practice of that, for the first time the path arises for the abandoning of defilements. When the path for the abandoning of defilements has arisen, the attention [Restored: that accompanies it] is called the seclusion attention.

For the first time, by the abandoning of those desire-realm defilements that are to be abandoned first, and by the dissipation of the encumbrance on their side — beyond that, he becomes one delighting-in-abandonment, delighting-in-seclusion. There, seeing the benefit of abandonment, touched by limited seclusion-born rapture-and-happiness, from time to time by the gladdening attention he gladdens [the mind]; by the urgency-stirring attention he stirs it to urgency — all for the dissipation of sloth-and-torpor and agitation. This is called the delight-gathering attention.

Of him thus delighting-in-abandonment, delighting-in-cultivation, properly engaged, with the support of the practice of the wholesome side, the [Restored: outburst of defilement] bound to sense-pleasures does not occur, whether walking or dwelling. It occurs to him: 'Is it that I [Restored: do not] feel sense-desire toward sense-pleasures though it is present, or that it is absent?' Wishing to examine this, he attends to one or another gladdening, attractive sign. Because the latent tendency has not been entirely abandoned, as he attends to that sign his mind becomes inclined-toward-indulgence, prone-toward-indulgence, leaning-toward-indulgence. [Restored: Equanimity does not stand firm; there is no disenchantment, disgust, aversion, or repugnance.] It occurs to him: 'My mind is not properly dispassionate, not liberated from sense-pleasures. My mind is restrained by formations, held like water [by a dam], [Conjectural: not] restrained by its nature. Let me, by greater measure, abide more delighting-in-abandonment for the complete abandoning of that latent tendency, and delighting-in-cultivation.' This is called the examination attention.

By a greater measure, he abides delighting-in-abandonment, delighting-in-cultivation, joined with *śamatha-vipaśyanā*. He examines again and again. Of him cultivating the antidote, time after time examining what is abandoned and not abandoned, the mind disengages from all desire-realm defilements — for the time being. But the seed is not yet completely uprooted. At that time, having reached the end of the path of preparatory practice for the [Conjectural: first] *dhyāna*, the attention that is the antidote to all defilements arises. This is called the practice-culminating attention.

And, [Restored: with that as its immediate condition] and as its cause, he attains the first *dhyāna*. The attention conjoined with the basic-first-*dhyāna* is called the fruit-of-practice-culmination attention.

Abiding in the seclusion attention and the delight-gathering attention, he gladdens the body with seclusion-born rapture-and-happiness manifesting faintly [Restored: from time to time]; at the time of the practice-culminating attention he [Restored: suffuses it with them] manifesting [Restored: densely] and amply from time to time. And when he is abiding in the fruit-of-practice-culmination attention, there is no part [Restored: that is unsuffused], [Restored: that could be suffused], in his whole body, by seclusion-born rapture-and-happiness. At that time, secluded from sense-pleasures, secluded from evil unwholesome dharmas, with *vitarka* and *vicāra*, with seclusion-born rapture-and-happiness, he attains and abides in the five-factored first *dhyāna*. He is called one who stands in the fruit of cultivating the [Restored: path] of the antidote to the desire-realm, and who has attained *kāma-vairāgya*.

By the characteristic-knowing attention, he properly understands what is to be abandoned; [Restored: he also properly knows what is to be attained,] and his mind sets the determination toward the abandoning of what is to be abandoned and the attaining of what is to be attained. By the resolve-born attention, he properly begins the practice for abandoning and attaining. By the seclusion attention, he abandons [Restored: the strong defilements]. By the delight-gathering attention, he abandons the [Restored: middling] grade of defilement. By the examination attention, he establishes the mind in non-pride-of-attainment. By the practice-culminating attention, he abandons the mild grade of defilement. By the fruit-of-practice-culmination attention, he experiences the fruit of cultivating [Conjectural: these kinds of attention], cultivated and well cultivated.

Furthermore: the characteristic-knowing attention and the resolve-born attention — this is called the conforming attention, accompanied by the [Restored: antidote of denunciation]. The seclusion attention and the practice-culminating attention — this is the antidote attention, accompanied by the antidote of abandonment. The delight-gathering attention is both antidote and gladdening. The examination attention is the review attention. Thus it is said. This being so, among the six attentions four attentions are to be known as [Restored: included]: namely, conforming, antidote, gladdening, and review.

As is the first-*dhyāna* attainment with the seven attentions, so the second, third, fourth *dhyāna* attainments, and the attainments of the spheres of infinite space, infinite consciousness, nothingness, and neither-perception-nor-non-perception — with the seven attentions.

Among them, [the attention] by which he comprehends the gross-characteristic in *vitarka*s, and the calm-characteristic in the second *dhyāna*, which is without *vitarka* — that is the characteristic-knowing attention for second-*dhyāna* attainment. There, the *dhyāna*-attainer — the first-*dhyāna*-obtainer — sees grossness in *vitarka*s: 'That mental talk which, though belonging to the concentrated plane, [Restored: moves unsteadily on the object] and, because it first alights on the object, is gross — this is *vitarka*. Following along after it, likewise moving unsteadily on the object, the subtler mental talk is [Conjectural: examination]. These *vitarka* and *vicāra* are mental factors; arising in the mind, they arise co-existent [with it], conjoined [with it], functioning on a single object. Thus they arise inwardly, and are included in the external sense-base. All of them are past, future, and present; arisen from causes, dependently arisen, [Conjectural: subject to arising and decay], temporary, present but briefly, agitators of the mind, perturbers — they proceed in an uncalm mode. Compared with the plane above, because they go with dwelling in suffering, they are of the dark side, accompanied by the benefit of [Conjectural: only a little] seclusion-born rapture-and-happiness. And this plane is by nature such that, for one dwelling in it, mental activity with *vitarka* and *vicāra* proceeds constantly, at all times, perpetually, always, not calm, not fully calm' — by such modes he comprehends the gross-characteristic in *vitarka*s. Not at all does this gross-characteristic exist in the second *dhyāna*, which is without *vitarka*. Therefore calm is the second *dhyāna*, because this grossness has departed. The remaining attentions for the attainment of the second *dhyāna* are to be known as appropriate, as before. Thus plane by plane, up through the attainment of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, seven attentions are to be known as appropriate.
