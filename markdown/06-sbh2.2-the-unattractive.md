# The Unattractive

*Śrāvakabhūmi · sbh2.2*

What is the unattractive?

It is said: The unattractive has six modes. Namely: putrid unattractiveness, the suffering mode, the inferior mode, the relative mode, the defilement mode, and the perishability mode.

What is putrid unattractiveness?

It is said: Putrid unattractiveness is to be understood as taken inwardly and taken outwardly.

Taken inwardly: hair of the head, body hair, nails, teeth, dust, dirt, skin, flesh, bone, sinew, vein, kidneys, heart, spleen, lungs, intestines, mesentery, stomach, bowel, urine, feces, tears, sweat, saliva, mucus, fat, lymph, marrow, grease, bile, phlegm, pus, blood, brain, brain-membrane, urine.

What is unattractiveness taken outwardly?

The discolored, the festering, [Conjectural: the decomposed], the swollen, the gnawed; the bloodied; the dispersed; the bone; the skeleton. Or: things made of excrement, urine, saliva, or mucus; things smeared with blood or pus; dung-heaps, gutters. And anything of this kind is to be understood as outward putrid unattractiveness. Unattractiveness taken inwardly and taken outwardly — this is called putrid unattractiveness.

What is the suffering mode?

What arises in dependence on a contact to be felt as painful — bodily and mental, the disagreeable felt experience, comprised in feeling. This is called the suffering mode.

What is the inferior mode?

Whatever is the most inferior thing, the most inferior realm — namely, the desire-realm. There is no realm more inferior, lower, or more rejected than this. This is called the inferior mode.

What is the relative mode?

A certain thing, although in itself fair, appears unattractive in relation to something fairer. For example: in relation to the formless realm, the form-realm appears unattractive. And: in relation to *nirvāṇa*, the cessation of *satkāya*, existence up to the peak of existence is reckoned unattractive existence. This is the relative mode of this kind.

[Conjectural: What is the defilement mode?] All the fetters, bondages, latent tendencies, and secondary defilements operating in the three realms [Lacuna] — this is called the defilement mode.

What is the perishability mode?

The impermanence, unsteadiness, unreliability, and nature of change of the five aggregates of clinging. Thus this unattractiveness is, for the purification of the lust-conduct person, the object.

Concerning that lust, namely: inwardly toward sense-pleasures, sense-desire and sense-lust; outwardly toward sense-pleasures, sexual desire and sexual lust; sense-object desire and sense-object lust; form-desire and form-lust; *satkāya*-desire and *satkāya*-lust; this is fivefold lust. For the abandoning, dispelling, and non-occurrence of this fivefold lust, the six modes of the unattractive are the [appropriate] object.

Of these: taken inwardly — by the inward putrid-unattractiveness object he purifies the mind, inwardly, from sense-desire and sense-lust toward sense-pleasures. By the outward putrid-unattractiveness object he purifies the mind from the four kinds of lust outwardly conjoined — colour-lust, shape-lust, touch-lust, and attendance-lust.

When he attends to the discolored, the festering, [Conjectural: the decomposed], or the swollen, then he purifies the mind from colour-lust. When he attends to the bloodied, then he purifies the mind from shape-lust. When he attends to the bone or the skeleton, then he purifies the mind from touch-lust. When he attends to the dispersed, he purifies the mind from attendance-lust.

Therefore the outward putrid unattractiveness was laid down by the Blessed One in the four charnel-ground [passages]. Whatever [corpse in a] charnel-ground he has seen — one day dead, or seven days dead, being eaten by crows, hawks, vultures, dogs, jackals — in each case he applies it to this very body: ‘This body of mine too will become thus, is of such a kind, has not gone beyond this nature of things.’ By this, [the aspects] from the discolored up to the gnawed are declared. And where it further says: ‘Whatever [corpse in a] charnel-ground he has seen — skin, flesh, and blood gone, well-bound’ — by this, the bloodied is declared. [Lacuna] And where it further says: ‘Those very charnel-ground [Conjectural: bones] — the spine, the jaw, the row of teeth, the skull — scattered apart, of one year, two years, up to seven years, white, conch-colored, dove-colored, mixed with dust-color, are seen’ — by this, the dispersed is declared.

Thus, by the outward-conjoined putrid-unattractiveness object, he purifies the mind from [Conjectural: sexual] lust. By the suffering-mode object and the inferior-mode object, he purifies the mind from sense-object-conjoined sense-lust. By the relative-mode object, he purifies the mind from form-lust. By the defilement-mode object and the perishability-mode object — covering the desire-realm up to the peak of existence — he purifies the mind from all [Conjectural: *satkāya*-lust]. This much is said [Conjectural: regarding the conduct-purifying object of the lust-conduct person]. The whole unattractive object, in all its aspects, is included here; but what is intended in this context is putrid unattractiveness. The other unattractiveness is an object for the purification of other conducts too.
