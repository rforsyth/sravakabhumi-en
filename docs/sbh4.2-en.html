Higher Jhāna and Formless Transitions

Śrāvakabhūmi · English working translation
← All topicsView side-by-side ↔

The seven manaskāras of sbh4.1 generalized through the higher transitions, governed by the audārika-lakṣaṇa (gross-characteristic) pattern: each higher attainment is reached by recognizing the grossness of the lower and the peace of the higher. Seven transitions: 1st→2nd, 2nd→3rd, 3rd→4th jhāna; 4th jhāna→ākāśānantyāyatana, ākāśa→vijñāna, vijñāna→ākiñcanya, ākiñcanya→naivasaṁjñā-nāsaṁjñāyatana. Plus the two non-conscious attainments (asaṁjñā- and nirodha-samāpatti) and the five abhijñā (super-knowledges) with their twelve perceptions.

The gross-characteristic in all these lower planes — up through the *ākiñcanyāyatana* — is, in summary, twofold: greater suffering-character of the lower-and-lower planes, and non-calm-abiding-character, and lesser-life-span-character. Thus this twofold gross-characteristic. He investigates by the six investigation-subjects, as appropriate. For whichever plane he wishes to make dispassion toward, the calm-characteristic is the plane above it, as appropriate — up through the practice-culminating-fruit attention. Then he is 'secluded from sense-pleasures,' or at higher levels, secluded from the analogous gross-content.

[At the 1st→2nd transition:] Turning the mind away from the *savitarka-savicāra* objects, he binds the mind to *avitarka-avicāra-samādhi-nimittas*. Withdrawing from the distractedly-moving object, in the non-distractedly-moving object, by single-dharma-character, calm, settled, the mind proceeds. He establishes it. Therefore: 'By the calming of *vitarka* and *vicāra*, by inward-confidence' — by the practice of *avitarka-avicāra-samādhi*, transcending the with-cleft, with-interval state, he attains the without-cleft, without-interval state. Therefore: 'By one-pointedness of mind' — by completely abandoning *vitarka-vicāra* in every way, the *avitarka-avicāra* practice-culminating attention is the *samādhi*. Immediately following, conditioned by it, the next arises. Therefore: 'Born from concentration' — from the attainment of the wished-for, undeplored object, or from seeing the fault in *prīti* coming next. Pacifying gone-to-elation and gone-to-dejection, by the disappearance of all the obstructions belonging to the *vitarka-vicāra*-first-*dhyāna*-defilement side, the counter-action of which is *praśrabdhi*, the workableness of mind-and-body — 'Rapture-and-happiness' — by the same understanding as before. By progression, the second comes to be. Everything else as before.

He sees the fault in the rapture-signs. Therefore: 'From dispassion-toward-rapture.' At that time, he has attained two mind-disturbance-makers, two obstructions: in the rapture-less third *dhyāna*, settling the mind, and in the second *dhyāna* there were *vitarka-vicāra*; here it is rapture. Therefore: 'He abides in equanimity.' Both these dharmas are mind-disturbance-makers, obstructors of without-interval equanimity. In the first *dhyāna* there are *vitarka-vicāra*, by which without-interval equanimity does not proceed. In the second *dhyāna* there is rapture, by which here too without-interval equanimity does not proceed. Therefore in this contemplator, in the first and second *dhyāna*s, equanimity is not yet; for which reason in the third *dhyāna* 'he abides in equanimity' is said. He, abiding in equanimity, abides thus with mindfulness established. As perception-and-attention conjoined-with-rapture occur — if, due to non-cultivation of the third *dhyāna*, by lapse-of-mindfulness, occasionally perception-and-attention conjoined-with-rapture occur in the mind — those, those, lightly, lightly indeed, by wisdom he penetrates. He properly understands. As they arise, arise, he does not endorse them. He abandons, dispels; pushes out; equanimously regards the mind. Therefore: 'Mindful and clearly-knowing.'

By that equanimity, by frequent practice, the rapture-conjoined factors are abandoned. The mind-agitating-maker — the rapture-less, calm, fully-calmed equanimity — arises in the heart, as known. By counter-action to rapture, at that time, in the form-body and the mind-body, happiness and *praśrabdhi*-happiness are experienced. From the third *dhyāna* below, that form-happiness does not exist; nor does without-interval equanimity. From the third *dhyāna* upward, although equanimity is found, happiness is not. Below, sense-pleasure-equanimity is absent; above, happiness is absent. This is that base — the third *dhyāna* — which the noble ones declare. In dependence on this, the attaining-and-abiding person who is mindful and the happy-abider attains and abides in the third *dhyāna* — so the noble ones, the elders, the elder disciples say.

Among the noble, there is no equality of category. Of the antidote to *sukha*, the abandonment-antidote is unannounced. What is, by counter-action to that, *sukha-prahāṇa* — that is announced. What further is that antidote? It is equanimity-mindfulness-clear-knowing. By practicing this, dropping out from the third *dhyāna*, where there is third-*dhyāna*-plane *sukha*, that he abandons. Therefore: 'By abandoning *sukha*, and previously by abandoning *duḥkha*, by the disappearance of *saumanasya* and *daurmanasya*.' In the fourth-*dhyāna*-attainment-time, that contemplator attains the transcendence of pleasure-and-pain. Of what was previously abandoned, of what is now abandoned, making the summation, he says: 'From the abandoning of pleasure, from the abandoning of pain, from the previous disappearance of joy and dejection.' Not in the fourth-*dhyāna*-attainment-time alone is *sukha* abandoned; in second-*dhyāna*-attainment-time *duḥkha* is; in third-*dhyāna*-attainment-time *saumanasya* sets; in first-*dhyāna*-attainment-time *daurmanasya* is set. There is at least, by the abandoning of pleasure-and-pain, neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling for him; not specially-marked. Therefore: 'Neither-painful-nor-pleasant' — at that time, taking the first *dhyāna* as the starting reference, all the lower-plane defilements are abandoned. Namely: *vitarka-vicāra*, *prīti*, in-and-out-breaths. By the abandoning of these, the equanimity there arising, and the mindfulness — that becomes purified, fully clarified. By which his mind, having attained the fourth *dhyāna*, stands without movement — sphere of all-non-perturbation. Therefore: 'By the purity of equanimity-and-mindfulness.'

Of one whose mind is resolved on space, the form-perception — connected with blue-yellow-red-white etc. — is transcended by indifference-toward and dispassion-toward. Therefore: 'From the transcendence of form-perceptions' — for the cause of the mind proceeding to the limit. The obstruction-perceptions of various kinds, many-and-varied, caused by the accumulation-of-color, those go away. Therefore: 'From the disappearance of obstruction-perceptions.' Or, by the cause-of-disappearance of those, the abundance-perception proceeding in the remaining superior aggregations — namely, perceptions of food, drink, clothing, ornaments, house, garden, forest, army, mountain, etc. — in those, all and every directed-effort no longer proceeds. Therefore: 'From non-attention to manifold-perceptions.' Having thus cultivated form-obstruction-manifold-perception, he becomes resolved on space by the infinity-aspect. Therefore: 'Infinite space' — taking the immediate-vicinity, transcending — from the practice-culminating attention to the high-practice-culminating-fruit, he attains the basic attainment. Therefore: 'Having attained, he abides in the infinity-of-space-base.'

Until the basic attainment is not attained, for the attainer of the space-as-object stage, that and the other aggregates are own-plane; the immediate-vicinity ones are also lower-plane aggregates. By a moment of consciousness, infinite space is resolved-on. That very consciousness, with the infinite-aspect of space-resolve — wishing to attain the *vijñānānantyāyatana* — having turned away the *ākāśānantyāyatana*-perception, that very consciousness is resolved-on with the infinite-aspect. With its immediate-vicinity, the basic *ākāśānantyāyatana* is transcended. Therefore: 'Transcending the *ākāśānantyāyatana* completely, infinite consciousness.' He, transcending the immediate-vicinity of *vijñānānantyāyatana*, from the practice-culminating attention to the basic-practice-culminating-fruit, attains. Therefore: 'Having attained, he abides in the *vijñānānantyāyatana*.'

He, dropping out from the *vijñānānantyāyatana*, searching for an object beyond consciousness, does not find one — neither connected with form, nor formless. Not finding that object, transcending the *vijñānānantyāyatana*-with-its-immediate-vicinity, he resolves: 'There is nothing else as an object.' He becomes resolved on the perception of nothingness. By the abundance of this perception-resolve, transcending the *ākiñcanyāyatana*-immediate-vicinity, from the practice-culminating attention, he attains the basic practice-culminating-fruit. Therefore: 'Transcending the *vijñānānantyāyatana* completely, there is nothing, the *ākiñcanyāyatana* — having attained, he abides.'

Therefore: 'Perceiving' — namely, dropping out from the *ākiñcanyāyatana*, gross-perceiving, fault-perceiving the perception-of-nothingness, he turns away the *ākiñcanyāyatana*-perception. By that previous *ākiñcanyāyatana*-attainment-time, the perception-of-nothingness was transcended; now the *non*-perception-of-nothingness has come to be transcended. Therefore: 'Perceiving — namely, perception-of-nothingness or non-perception-of-nothingness or non-perception-of-nothingness.' Not, however, that all and every perception of his is ceased. As in the non-perception-attainment or the cessation-attainment — but here, a subtle perception proceeds, with sign-as-object. 'Neither perception nor non-perception.'

There are two non-conscious attainments: the non-perception attainment and the cessation attainment. The non-perception attainment is entered by an ordinary person using perception-turned-away attention; the cessation attainment, however, is entered only by a noble one. By two attentions, entry into these two attainments occurs: namely, into the non-perception attainment by perception-turned-away attention; into the cessation attainment by attention dropping-out from *naivasaṁjñā-nāsaṁjñā* and resolving-on-cessation-of-object.

By the basis of *dhyāna*, the projection of the five higher knowledges occurs. How does it occur? Just as the contemplator becomes an attainer of pure *dhyāna*. Relying on that pure *dhyāna*: whatever dharma he has heard, grasped, fully obtained — bearing on the higher-knowledge-predominant, namely the range-of-supernormal-power, recollection-of-past-abodes, divine-ear, divine-eye, knowledge-of-others'-minds — that very dharma he attends to with concentrated-plane-attention. He becomes meaning-knower and dharma-knower. Of that meaning-knowing, dharma-knowing practitioner, making the mind so-and-so, by the practice of frequent-cultivation — the time, the moment, becomes when his fruits-of-projection, the five higher knowledges, arise.
