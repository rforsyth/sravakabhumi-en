The Pervasive Object

Śrāvakabhūmi · Sanskrit (Shukla 1973) ↔ AI-assisted English
← All topicsView English only →

The first of the four objects of meditation listed at 1.5. The pervasive object is itself fourfold (2.4–2.5): the image with conceptual construction (¶3–5), the image without conceptual construction (¶6), the full extent of the entity (vastu-paryantatā, ¶7), and the consummation of the work (¶8). ¶7 defines the full extent of the entity through yāvad-bhāvikatā and yathāvad-bhāvikatā, kept in Sanskrit and glossed at 7.2. The text calls the object pervasive because its four kinds are present in every object, are taught by all Buddhas, and belong to both the śamatha and the vipaśyanā side (9.3–9.6). ¶11–19 support the account from scripture: a discourse to Revata (¶11–15) and two verses with their exegesis (¶16–19).
