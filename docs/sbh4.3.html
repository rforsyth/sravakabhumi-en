Closing Material: Rebirth, Lokottara Path, Arhatship

The closing chunk of Yogasthāna IV — covering the lokottara-mārga (supramundane path) proper, complementing the laukika-mārga (mundane path) treated in sbh4.1 and sbh4.2. Treats the four-noble-truths-based supramundane progression, the sapta-manaskāra applied to the supramundane, bhāvanā-svabhāva, karma, prakāra-bheda, the attainment of arhatship, and parinirvāṇa. Ends with the colophon of the Śrāvakabhūmi as a whole.

sbh4.3:1.1
guṇānāmapṛthagjanasandhāraṇānāṁ yathāyogamabhinirhāro veditavyaḥ |
The corresponding rebirth of these qualities among ordinary persons should be known as appropriate.
Paragraph 1 maps each meditative attainment to its corresponding rebirth: each of the four dhyānas and the four ārūpya attainments has a three-tier (mild/middling/strong) cultivation that yields rebirth in a specific tier of the corresponding deva-realm. This is the bhāvanā-phala mapping that completes the laukika-mārga analysis of [sbh4.1](sbh4.1.html)–[sbh4.2](sbh4.2.html). The five Pure Abode gods (Avṛha through Akaniṣṭha) are the realms of anāgāmin rebirth — the supramundane fourth-dhyāna mixing with-flux and fluxless cultivation produces a fivefold result (1.6–1.8). The standard Sarvāstivāda Abhidharmakośa III scheme is identical in structure though with different deva-list orderings.

sbh4.3:3.1
atha lokottareṇa mārgeṇa gantukāmo bhavati tasya catvāryāryasatyānyārabhya sapta manaskārā anupūrveṇotpadyante |
Now, [if the practitioner] wishes to go by the supramundane path, the seven attentions [sapta-manaskāra] arise sequentially for him in connection with the four noble truths.
The sapta-manaskāra (seven attentions) — lakṣaṇa-pratisaṃvedī (characteristic-knowing), ādhimokṣika (resolve-born), prāvivekya (seclusion), rati-saṃgrāhaka (delight-collecting), mīmāṃsā (investigation), prayoga-niṣṭhā (practice-culmination), prayoga-niṣṭhā-phala (fruit-of-practice-culmination) — were treated in detail under the laukika-mārga at [sbh4.1](sbh4.1.html). Here they are reused for the lokottara-mārga: the same seven applied to the different object (four noble truths instead of grossness/calmness). This is the chapter's central architectural argument: the seven-attention progression is the general form of meditative progression, applicable equally to laukika and lokottara. Compare [sbh3.1:15.2](sbh3.1.html#sbh3.1:15.2) for the same reusability claim about the four manaskāra across śamatha and vipaśyanā.

sbh4.3:3.5
tadyathānityākāreṇa, duḥkhākāreṇa, anātmākāreṇa ca |
namely, the aspect of impermanence [anityatā], the aspect of suffering [duḥkhatā], [the aspect of emptiness (śūnyatā),] and the aspect of not-self [anātmatā].
The ṣoḍaśa-ākāra ārya-satya — sixteen aspects of the four noble truths — is the canonical Sarvāstivāda-Yogācāra analytic framework. Four truths × four aspects = sixteen aspects. Of suffering: anitya, duḥkha, śūnya, anātma (impermanence, suffering, emptiness, not-self). Of arising: hetu, samudaya, prabhava, pratyaya (cause, arising, origination, condition). Of cessation: nirodha, śānta, praṇīta, niḥsaraṇa (cessation, calm, sublime, escape). Of path: mārga, nyāya, pratipatti, nairyāṇika (path, method, practice, deliverance). The original at 3.5 dropped śūnyatā; restored in brackets. The same sixteen are taken as the object of the darśana-mārga sixteen-moment penetration in Abhidharmakośa VI.27.

sbh4.3:4.1
tatra daśabhirākārairduḥkhasatyaṁ parīkṣamāṇaścatura ākārānanupraviśati |
Examining the truth of suffering through ten aspects, he enters into [its] four aspects.
sbh4.3:4.2
katamairdaśabhistadyathā |
Through what ten? Namely:
sbh4.3:4.3
vipariṇāmākāreṇa, avināśākāreṇa, viyogākāreṇa, sannihitā |
by the aspect of transformation [vipariṇāma], by the aspect of destruction [vināśa], by the aspect of separation [viyoga], by the aspect of being-at-hand [sannihita],
The ten aspects of suffering at 4.3–4.5 unfold the four aspects of duḥkha-satya in finer grain. Mapping (made explicit at 23.1–5): anityatā is comprised by five aspects — vipariṇāma (transformation), vināśa (destruction), viyoga (separation), sannihita (at-hand), dharmatā (nature-of-things). duḥkhatā is comprised by three aspects — saṃyojana-bandhana (fetter-and-bondage), aniṣṭa (unwished-for), anāśvāsika (insecurity). śūnyatā is comprised by one aspect — anupalabdhi (non-apprehension). anātmatā is comprised by one aspect — asvatantra (non-autonomy). The 5+3+1+1 partition is canonical and gives a doctrinal reason for the unequal weighting: impermanence is multi-modal (five distinct ways things change), suffering is tri-modal (three distinct ways things hurt), and emptiness/no-self are each grounded in a single decisive epistemological insight.
sbh4.3:4.4
kāreṇa, dharmatākāreṇa |
by the aspect of nature-of-things [dharmatā],
sbh4.3:4.5

by the aspect of fetter-and-bondage [saṃyojana-bandhana], by the aspect of unwished-for [aniṣṭa], by the aspect of insecurity [anāśvāsika], by the aspect of non-apprehension [anupalabdhi], and by the aspect of non-autonomy [asvatantra].
Note: the original at 4.5 named the ninth aspect anugraha (non-assistance/non-helpfulness), but the actual analysis at 22.34 clearly identifies it as anupalabdhi (non-apprehension) — the aspect by which the practitioner enters śūnyatā. Corrected to non-apprehension to match the analysis-passage. anupalabdhi is the standard Yogācāra epistemological term for what the meditator fails to find when looking for a self among the aggregates: 'mere aggregate, no self.'
