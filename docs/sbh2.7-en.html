The Skill-Object

The skill-object, third of the four objects of meditation (sbh2.1:1.5), is fivefold (1.2): skill in the aggregates (¶2–11), in the elements (¶12–13), in the sense-bases (¶14–15), in dependent origination (¶16–17), and in the possible and impossible (¶18). For the first four, the text lists the dharmas and then defines the skill in them; the fifth is dependent origination itself, with the difference that it concerns the fruits of wholesome and unwholesome actions (18.1–18.6). The five are summed up as skill in the specific characteristic, which is skill in the aggregates, and skill in the general characteristic, the other four (19.1–19.4). The fourth object, the defilement-purifying, has no section of its own; it is defined at sbh2.0:35.1.

What is the skill-object? Namely: skill-in-aggregates, skill-in-elements, skill-in-sense-bases, skill-in-dependent-origination, skill-in-the-possible-and-impossible.

What are the aggregates, and what is skill-in-aggregates?

It is said: There are five aggregates. Form-aggregate, feeling-aggregate, [Conjectural: perception-aggregate,] formations-aggregate, and consciousness-aggregate.

The form-aggregate: whatever form there is, all of it is the four great elements, and what is derived from the four great elements. Form is also past, future, or present; inward or outward; gross or subtle; inferior or superior; far or near.

What is the feeling-aggregate?

Feeling arisen in dependence on contact to be felt as pleasant, painful, or neither-painful-nor-pleasant. The six feeling-bodies: feeling born from eye-contact, and feeling born from ear-, nose-, tongue-, body-, and mind-contact.

What is the perception-aggregate?

Namely: perception-with-sign, perception-without-sign, limited perception, expansive perception, immeasurable perception, and the perception 'there is nothing' — perception of the base of nothingness. The six perception-bodies: perception born from eye-contact, and perception born from ear-, nose-, tongue-, body-, and mind-contact.

What is the formations-aggregate?

The six volition-bodies: volition born from eye-contact, and from ear-, nose-, tongue-, body-, and mind-contact. Setting aside [Conjectural: feeling] and perception — the other mental factors are also formations.

What is the consciousness-aggregate?

Whatever is mind, [Conjectural: mentality, and consciousness]. Again, the six consciousness-bodies: eye-consciousness; ear-, nose-, tongue-, body-, and mind-consciousness. And this feeling, perception, formations, and this consciousness — Past, future, present; inward or outward — and so on in detail, as before. These are called the aggregates.

What is skill-in-aggregates?

One who knows these dharmas as taught — by their distinct nature, by their manifold nature, and who does not apprehend anything beyond that, nor conceptually constructs anything beyond that. This, in summary, is called skill-in-aggregates.

What is the distinct nature of the aggregates? The form-aggregate is one thing; the feeling-aggregate is another; and so on, each distinct, up through the consciousness-aggregate. This is distinct nature.

What is manifold nature?

The form-aggregate has many kinds, and is manifold in nature. By the division into great elements and [Conjectural: derived form]; by past, future, present, and so on; by kind-distinctions — this is called manifold nature for the form-aggregate. The same applies, mutatis mutandis, to the remaining aggregates.

And how does he not apprehend or conceptually construct anything beyond that?

He apprehends only the aggregate, only the thing — and does not, apart from the aggregates, apprehend a self permanent, steady, of unchanging nature; nor does he apprehend anything as ‘mine.’ He does not conceptually construct anything beyond that.

What are the elements?

What is skill-in-elements?

It is said: There are eighteen elements. Eye-element, form-element, eye-consciousness-element; ear-element, sound-element, ear-consciousness-element; nose-element, smell-element, nose-consciousness-element; tongue-element, taste-element, tongue-consciousness-element; body-element, tangible-element, body-consciousness-element, [Lacuna] dharma-element, mind-consciousness-element — these are called the elements.

Furthermore, [that] these eighteen dharmas arise from their own respective element, from their own respective seed, from their own respective lineage — are born, come forth, and manifest; when one knows, accepts, and considers this — this is called skill-in-elements. When one knows the proceeding of the eighteen dharmas from their own respective element, this is precisely skill-in-cause-and-condition — this is what skill-in-elements amounts to.

What are the sense-bases?

What is skill-in-sense-bases? It is said: There are twelve sense-bases. Eye sense-base, form sense-base; ear sense-base, sound sense-base; nose sense-base, smell sense-base; tongue sense-base, taste sense-base; body sense-base, tangible sense-base, mind sense-base, and dharma sense-base. These are called the sense-bases.

What is skill-in-sense-bases?

The eye is predominant; forms are the object; for the arising of eye-consciousness with its associates. and the mind that has just ceased is the immediate-condition. Likewise, the ear is predominant, sound is the object, and the mind that has just ceased is the immediate-condition, for the arising of ear-consciousness with its associates. And so on, up through: mind as the immediate [condition], the attention born from it as the predominant-condition, the dharma as object — for the arising of mind-consciousness with its associates. Thus by three conditions — immediate-condition, object-condition, and predominant-condition — the proceeding of the six consciousness-bodies happens, with their associates. Skill in conditions with respect to the inward and outward sense-bases — this is called skill-in-sense-bases.

What is dependent origination? What is skill-in-dependent-origination?

It is said: Ignorance-conditioned formations; formations-conditioned consciousness; consciousness-conditioned name-and-form; [continuing in detail through the twelve links] until: thus is the arising of this whole great mass of suffering. This is called dependent origination.

Further, [that] dharmas alone [Conjectural: nourish] dharmas, dharmas alone [Conjectural: suffuse] dharmas; formations alone bring forth dharmas — because of cause-arisenness, because of dependent-arisenness — not having been, they come into being. Having come into being, they pass away again. Therefore these formations are impermanent; and what is impermanent has the nature of birth, aging, disease, death, grief, lamentation, suffering, dejection, and distress. By their nature-of-aging up through their nature-of-distress, they are suffering. What is suffering — being not-self-determining, weak — is not-self. When by these aspects there is, with respect to dependently-arisen dharmas, knowing-of-impermanence, knowing-of-suffering, and knowing-of-not-self — this is called skill-in-dependent-origination.

Skill-in-the-possible-and-impossible is also to be understood as just dependent-origination. The distinction is this: By skill-in-the-possible-and-impossible, he knows that causation is [Conjectural: not disparate] — that for wholesome and unwholesome actions there is a fruit-of-result; [Lacuna] and that for unwholesome actions the fruit is unwished. When the knowing is thus, this is called skill-in-the-possible-and-impossible.

These five skill-domains, in summary, are skill-in-specific-characteristic, and skill-in-general-characteristic. Skill-in-aggregates declares skill-in-specific-characteristic. The remaining four declare skill-in-general-characteristic. This is called the skill-object.
