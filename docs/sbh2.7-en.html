The Five Skill-Objects

Śrāvakabhūmi · English working translation
← All topicsView side-by-side ↔

The third of the four primary ālambana categories (alongside vyāpī, carita-viśodhana, and kleśa-viśodhana). The kauśalya-ālambana (skill-object) names five domains in which the practitioner cultivates skill: skandha-kauśalya (skill in the aggregates), dhātu-kauśalya (in the elements), āyatana-kauśalya (in the spheres), pratītyasamutpāda-kauśalya (in dependent origination), and sthānāsthāna-kauśalya (in the possible-and-impossible). Each is treated through both svalakṣaṇa (specific characteristic) and sāmānya-lakṣaṇa (general characteristic) framing.

What is the skill-object? It is said: skill-in-aggregates, skill-in-elements, skill-in-bases, skill-in-dependent-origination, skill-in-place-and-not-place.

What are the aggregates, and what is skill-in-aggregates? It is said: There are five aggregates. Form-aggregate, perception-aggregate, feeling-aggregate, formations-aggregate, and consciousness-aggregate.

The form-aggregate: whatever form there is, all of it is the four great elements, and what is derived from the four great elements. Form is also past, future, or present; inward or outward; gross or subtle; inferior or superior; far or near.

What is the feeling-aggregate? Pleasant feeling, painful feeling, and neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling — arisen in dependence on contact conditioned by the corresponding feeling. The six feeling-bodies: feeling born from eye-contact, and feeling born from ear-, nose-, tongue-, body-, and mind-contact.

What is the perception-aggregate? Namely: perception-with-sign, perception-without-sign, limited perception, expansive perception, immeasurable perception, and the perception 'there is nothing' — perception of the base of nothingness. The six perception-bodies: perception born from eye-contact, and perception born from ear-, nose-, tongue-, body-, and mind-contact.

What is the formations-aggregate? The six volition-bodies: volition born from eye-contact, and from ear-, nose-, tongue-, body-, and mind-contact. Setting aside volition and perception — the other mental factors are also formations.

What is the consciousness-aggregate? Whatever is mind, mental cognition. Again, the six consciousness-bodies: eye-consciousness; ear-, nose-, tongue-, body-, and mind-consciousness. That feeling, perception, formation — is that very consciousness. Past, future, present; inward or outward — the elevenfold mode-analysis applies as before. These are called the aggregates.

What is skill-in-aggregates? One who knows these dharmas as taught — by their distinct-self-nature, by their multiple-self-nature, and who does not apprehend anything beyond that, nor conceptually constructs anything beyond that. This, in summary, is called skill-in-aggregates.

What is the distinct-self-nature of the aggregates? The form-aggregate is one thing; the feeling-aggregate is another; and so on, each distinct, up through the consciousness-aggregate. This is distinct-self-nature.

What is multiple-self-nature? The form-aggregate has many kinds, and is multiple-natured. By the division of element-and-world; by past, future, present and so on; by kind-distinctions — this is called multiple-self-nature for the form-aggregate. The same applies, mutatis mutandis, to the remaining aggregates.

And he does not apprehend or conceptually construct anything beyond that. He apprehends only the aggregate, only the substance — and does not, apart from the aggregates, apprehend a self. He does not apprehend anything as permanent, steady, of unchanging nature, nor anything as mine. He does not conceptually construct anything beyond that.

What are the elements? What is skill-in-elements? It is said: There are eighteen elements. Eye-element, form-element, eye-consciousness-element; ear-element, sound-element, ear-consciousness-element; nose-element, smell-element, nose-consciousness-element; tongue-element, taste-element, tongue-consciousness-element; body-element, tangible-element, body-consciousness-element, dharma-element, mind-consciousness-element — these are called the elements.

Furthermore, when one knows that these eighteen dharmas arise from their own respective element, from their own respective seed, from their own respective lineage — that they are born, come forth, and manifest [from these]; and when one knows, accepts, and contemplates this — this is called skill-in-elements. When one knows the proceeding of the eighteen dharmas from their own respective element, this is precisely skill-in-cause-and-condition — this is what skill-in-elements amounts to.

What are the bases? What is skill-in-bases? It is said: There are twelve bases. Eye-base, form-base; ear-base, sound-base; nose-base, smell-base; tongue-base, taste-base; body-base, tangible-base, mind-base, and dharma-base. These are called the bases.

What is skill-in-bases? The eye is predominant; forms are the object; for the arising of eye-consciousness with its associates. The immediately-preceding mind is the immediate-condition. Likewise, the ear is predominant, sound is the object, the immediately-preceding mind is the immediate-condition, for the arising of ear-consciousness with its associates. And so on, up through: the immediately-preceding mind, the attention born from it as the predominant-condition, the dharma as object — for the arising of mind-consciousness with its associates. Thus by three conditions — immediate-condition, object-condition, and predominant-condition — the proceeding of the six consciousness-bodies happens, with their associates. Skill-in-cause-and-condition with respect to the inward and outward bases — this is called skill-in-bases.

What is dependent origination? What is skill-in-dependent-origination? It is said: Ignorance-conditioned formations; formations-conditioned consciousness; consciousness-conditioned name-and-form; [continuing in detail through the twelve links] until: thus is the arising of this whole great mass of suffering. This is called dependent origination.

What follows: dharmas alone produce dharmas; dharmas alone carry dharmas. Formations alone are the carriers of dharmas — because of cause-arisenness, because of dependent-arisenness — having been, they come into being. Having come into being, they pass away again. Therefore these formations are impermanent; and what is impermanent has the nature of birth, aging, disease, death, grief, lamentation, suffering, dissatisfaction, and despair. By their nature-of-aging up through their nature-of-despair, they are suffering. What is suffering — being not-self-determining, weak — is not-self. When by these aspects there is, with respect to dependently-arisen dharmas, knowing-of-impermanence, knowing-of-suffering, and knowing-of-not-self — this is called skill-in-dependent-origination.

Skill-in-place-and-not-place is also to be understood as just dependent-origination. The distinction is this: By skill-in-place-and-not-place, he knows the variability-of-cause-condition — that for wholesome and unwholesome actions there is a fruit-of-result; and that for unwholesome actions the fruit is unwished. When the knowing is thus, this is called skill-in-place-and-not-place.

These five place-skills, in summary, are skill-in-specific-characteristic, and skill-in-general-characteristic. Skill-in-aggregates declares skill-in-specific-characteristic. The remaining four declare skill-in-general-characteristic. This is called the skill-object.
