The opening of the third yogasthāna. A beginner approaches a knower of yoga (yogajña) and asks for instruction (1.2–8); the teacher encourages him (1.9–31), puts four questions (1.32–33), and examines him as to vow, lineage, faculty, and conduct by four means: question, talk, behavior, and knowledge of others' minds (1.34–103). He then trains him in five matters (1.104–105), of which this section treats the first two, protection and accumulation of the requisites of concentration (1.106–114) and seclusion (1.115–2.6), and begins the third, one-pointedness of mind, defined as concentration (2.7–31). The sentence that begins at 2.31 continues at sbh3.1 1.1.

III. The Third Yogasthāna

Thus, when the establishment of persons, of objects, on up to the establishment of the fruit of cultivation has been made — a person desiring his own benefit, wishing to attain his own goal, a beginner, a first-time worker, should approach a knower of yoga — a preceptor, an instructor, a person occupying the place of a teacher — establishing mindfulness in four respects. With the intention of understanding, not with a fault-finding mind, with reverence, not with the haughtiness of equality. Seeking what is wholesome. Not for self-exaltation. ‘I will yoke myself and others to wholesome roots,’ thinking thus. Not for the sake of gain and honor. And having so approached, in due time making an opportunity, baring one shoulder, placing the right knee-circle upon the earth, sitting on a yet lower seat, with all respect, he should make request for yoga: ‘I am one in need of yoga; teach me yoga, out of compassion.’ And, thus requested, by the yogin who knows yoga that beginner, first-time worker, desirous of applying himself to attention in yoga, [Conjectural: should be encouraged] with very gentle words, gladdened, and the benefit of abandonment should be praised: ‘Good, good, [Conjectural: dear one], that you, in a populace [Conjectural: fallen into] heedlessness, inclined toward sense-objects, attached to sense-objects, wish to apply yourself to heedfulness; you who, in a populace that has entered the [Conjectural: prison] of the lower realms, wish to get out of that [Conjectural: prison] of the lower realms; you who, in a populace [Conjectural: bound in the fetters] of lust, hatred, and delusion, wish to throw off the bonds; you who, in a populace that has entered the great forest and difficult path of saṁsāra, wish to cross over it; you who, in a populace fallen into a great famine of wholesome roots through defilement, wish to attain plenty of wholesome roots; you who, in a populace overtaken by the great fear of the defilement-thieves, wish to attain the safety of nirvāṇa; you who, in a populace gripped by the great illness of defilement, wish to attain [Conjectural: the supreme health] that is nirvāṇa; you who, in a populace carried away by the current of the four floods, wish to cross over the floods; you who, in a populace that has entered [Conjectural: the great darkness of ignorance], wish to attain the great light of knowledge. Moreover, friend, applying yourself thus, and you will consume the country’s alms [Conjectural: not in vain]. You will become a doer of the Teacher’s word, a meditator who does not neglect dhyāna, endowed with vipaśyanā, one who frequents empty dwellings, devoted to the yoga of his own [Conjectural: task], uncensured by wise fellow brahma-farers, practising for the welfare of [self and others] alike, for the welfare of others, for the welfare of many, out of compassion for the world, for the benefit, welfare, and happiness of gods and humans’ — thus by such gentle words having gladdened, showing the benefit of abandonment, he should be inquired of with respect to four matters of inquiry. ‘Has the friend single-mindedly gone for refuge to the Buddha, the dharma, the saṅgha, and does he not acknowledge any other teacher or one worthy of offerings outside? Is your brahma-faring purified from the start for cultivation, and is your virtue thoroughly pure, your view upright? Have you heard and retained the dharma concerning the summary and analysis of the noble truths, whether little or much? Is your mind resolved on nirvāṇa, and have you gone forth intent on nirvāṇa?’ If, when asked, he answers ‘yes,’ beyond this, in four respects he should be examined by four means. He should be examined by way of vow. By way of lineage, faculty, and conduct he should be examined; by [Conjectural: questioning], talk, behavior, and the [Conjectural: knowledge] of others’ minds he should be inquired into.

There, how, by question, is he examined as to vow?

Thus he should be asked: ‘Where, friend, is your vow made?’ In the disciple-vehicle, the solitary-buddha-vehicle, the great-vehicle. Wherever he has made the vow, there he will declare himself. Thus by question he is examined as to vow. How by question are lineage, faculty, and conduct examined? He should be asked thus: ‘Friend, your own lineage, faculty, conduct — what is my lineage?

What kind are my faculties — mild, middling, sharp? Am I of lust-conduct?

Or of hatred-conduct?

and so on, [Conjectural: up to]: am I of applied-thought-conduct?’ If he is intelligent, and from earlier and later [behavior] his lineage, faculty, and conduct have been observed, noted, he declares it. If, however, he is [Conjectural: not] capable, and from earlier and later [behavior] his [lineage and faculty] have not been observed or noted, nor thereby his conduct observed — when asked, he does not declare. For him, after that, the three are examined by talk. Before him a talk connected with the disciple-vehicle is to be made. With varied, intelligible, [Conjectural: sweet] ways of speech — when that talk is being spoken, if he is of disciple-lineage, he is exceedingly delighted by that talk. He rejoices, gladdened, becomes joyful. [Conjectural: One of solitary-buddha or great-vehicle lineage] is not pleased, does not resolve. Or again, in the talk connected with the great-vehicle, when spoken, the one of great-vehicle-lineage is exceedingly delighted. Rejoices. Up to: he is pleased, resolves. The one of disciple or solitary-buddha lineage is not so. If, again, he is of mild faculties, he [Conjectural: exerts himself] greatly for noting the dharma and the meaning, for grasping and penetrating [them]; the middle-faculty person not [so]; the sharp-faculty person, however, quickly notes the dharma and grasps it, and penetrates it, even when a deep talk is being spoken. If, again, he is of lust-conduct, when a gladdening talk is spoken, he is exceedingly pleased, delights; [Conjectural: he enters into its import, and his hair stands on end], and he displays tears, a smooth continuity, softness of mind, fluidity of mind. If he is of hatred-conduct, [Lacuna] [Conjectural: If he is of delusion-conduct,] when a penetrative talk is spoken, connected with nirvāṇa, free from worldly attachment, he is alarmed, falls into alarm. As stated for mild faculty, so it is to be understood here too. If he is of [Conjectural: conceit-conduct], [Conjectural: he does not want to listen in order to know]. He does not lend ear. He does not likewise set his mind on understanding. Even when won over, he does not likewise voice his [Conjectural: assent]. If, again, he is of [Conjectural: applied-thought-conduct], even when attentive, his mind is distracted. He grasps ill-grasped. He does not grasp firmly, not stably, and destroys what was grasped. and he asks about the talk [Conjectural: again and again]. Thus by talk lineage, faculty, and conduct are examined.

How by behavior?

Those marks previously stated of the disciple-lineage and of lust-conduct persons — those are called ‘behavior.’ By that behavior, as appropriate, lineage, faculty, conduct should be examined.

There, how by the knowledge of others’ minds are lineage, faculty, and conduct examined?

As when the yogin who knows yoga is a possessor of the knowledge of others’ minds. By that knowledge of others’ minds, he knows lineage, faculty, and conduct as they actually are. Having examined these four matters by these four means, in five matters he disciplines him. Namely, in the protection-and-accumulation of the concentration-requisites, in seclusion, in mental one-pointedness, in purification of hindrances, in cultivation of attention. There, protection-and-accumulation of the concentration-requisites: as long as he is endowed with restraint of virtue. And there he dwells heedfully [Conjectural: so as not to decline]; he does not slacken his energy in practising the training-precepts of the virtue-aggregate [Damaged] as taught and allowed by the Buddha, thus he does not fall away from the training-path of the virtue-restraint he has [Conjectural: attained]. And he attains the not-yet-attained training-path. As with restraint of virtue, so restraint of senses, knowing right measure in food, application to wakefulness in the early and late night, dwelling with full awareness, on up to the ornaments of a recluse. Whatever requisite-comprised dharma-section he gains, he protects it and further, for the fullness of the section, [Conjectural: desirous of practice neither less nor more than stated, he dwells eager] and with energy aroused — this is called protection-and-accumulation of the concentration-requisites. Thus, becoming dispassionate toward dharmas on the side of decline and cultivating the dharmas [Conjectural: on the side of distinction], he becomes a dweller-in-seclusion.

What is seclusion?

That which is fullness of place, fullness of posture. There, fullness of place: namely, the forest, the foot of a tree, the empty hut — there the mountain glen, the mountain cave, the straw-heaps are called ‘empty hut.’ There, woodland is called ‘foot of a tree.’ There, the open air, the cemetery, remote dwellings are called ‘forest.’ All this summed up is the ‘place’ to be understood. Namely: forest, foot of tree, empty hut, mountain glen, mountain cave, straw-heap, open air, cemetery, woodland — remote dwellings. Fullness of place, again, is fivefold. Here, the place is from the beginning handsome. Pleasant to behold, agreeable, well-supplied with park, forest, lotus-pond, beautiful, charming, not uneven, not full of stumps and thorns, not full of stones, gravel, and potsherds; where, seeing it, his mind becomes clear for dwelling, for abandonment, for application. With gladdened mind, joyful mind, he undertakes abandonment — this is the first fullness of place. Again, this is [Conjectural: not crowded by day]. At night, of little sound, with little forest-clamor, little gnats, mosquitoes, wind, sun, creeping creature contact — this is the second fullness of place. Again further, free from lions, tigers, leopards, robbers, enemy armies, fear of humans and non-humans, where he dwells trusting, with mind free of suspicion, dwells at ease — this is the third fullness of place. Again further, the life-requisites conforming, robes etc., are there obtained with little difficulty. so that he does not become exhausted over alms. [Conjectural: Where provisions are not lacking] — this is the fourth fullness of place. Again further, it is supported by good friends. Such wise fellow brahma-farers dwell there [Conjectural: who make clear what has not been made clear]. And having penetrated a deep statement of meaning with wisdom, they explain it well. For the purification of knowledge-and-vision. This is the fifth fullness of place.

There, what is fullness of posture?

By day he passes the time in walking, by sitting; in the first watch of the night thus, in the middle watch he lies down on the right side; in the last watch he rises very promptly. He passes the time in walking and sitting. In that complete lodging, as authorized by the Buddha, he sits on a cot, a stool, grass, or a mat. He folds the legs cross-wise. For what reason? Seeing five reasons: with the body gathered compactly, pliancy arises. This posture is favorable to the arising of pliancy. Likewise in long-time sitting, he is able to pass the time. And by this posture, no bodily fatigue arises in him. Likewise, this posture is not shared with other sectarians, with adherents of other doctrines. Likewise others, seeing one seated in this posture, are exceedingly cleared in mind; this posture is resorted to and authorized by the Buddhas and the Buddha-disciples. These five reasons he sees and sits. Having folded the legs, he sets the body upright.

There, what is [Conjectural: bodily uprightness]?

The body being clearly raised and held straight. [Conjectural: And what is ‘setting’?] With a mind [Conjectural: free of guile], free of pretense, upright. There, with the body upright and held alert, sloth-and-torpor does not seize the mind and stay. With [the mind] free of pretense, outward distraction does not seize the mind and stay. Having established mindfulness to the fore.

There, what is mindfulness ‘to the fore’?

The mindfulness which he establishes, associated with wise attention, is called ‘to the fore,’ because it is turned against the whole dark side, contrary to it. Further, mindfulness whose object is the concentration-sign, an image-object, is called ‘to the fore,’ because it is turned away from all objects of the [Conjectural: non-concentrated] planes. This is called fullness of posture.

What is withdrawal?

Answer: twofold: bodily withdrawal, and mental withdrawal. There, bodily withdrawal: not dwelling together with householders or renunciants.

There, mental withdrawal: avoiding defiled and indeterminate attention, he cultivates an attention belonging to the concentration-plane or preparatory for the requisites of concentration. Wholesome, connected with the goal — this is called mental withdrawal. There, the fullness of place, this fullness of posture, this bodily withdrawal, this mental withdrawal — these gathered into one are called ‘seclusion.’

There, what is one-pointedness?

Answer: the mental continuum of recurrent mindfulness with a conformable object, yoked to continuity and blameless delight, that is called concentration. Wholesome mental one-pointedness too.

What does he recollect again and again?

Answer: the dharmas grasped and heard, and the admonition and instruction received from teachers — with that as the governing [condition], bringing the sign belonging to the concentration-plane into presence, he keeps mindfulness going with that as its object, yoked to continuity, and binds it [there].

There, what is the conformable-object?

Whatever concentration-plane object — manifold, of many kinds — by which the mind is supremely concentrated on the object, this is called conformable-object. To what is this conformable? Answer: it is a likeness of the [Conjectural: knowable entity]; therefore it is called conformable. The continuous, gapless, uninterrupted mindfulness that proceeds with that object, constantly and respectfully — this is being yoked to continuity. As for dwelling undefiled while delighting in that very object, the path-conductive quality (vāhimārgatā) of mindfulness — this is being yoked to [Conjectural: blameless] delight.
