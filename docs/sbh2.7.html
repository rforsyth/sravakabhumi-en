The Five Skill-Objects

Śrāvakabhūmi · Sanskrit (Shukla 1973) ↔ English (1 rendering)
← All topicsView English only →

The last dedicated ālambana chapter in Yogasthāna II. Kauśalya is third in the canonical four-fold order (vyāpī, carita-viśodhana, kauśalya, kleśa-viśodhana); the fourth, kleśa-viśodhana, has no separate chapter — it is treated within sbh2.0 itself, through the four-noble-truths discussion that grounds the supramundane path. The kauśalya-ālambana (skill-object) names five domains in which the practitioner cultivates skill: skandha-kauśalya (skill in the aggregates), dhātu-kauśalya (in the elements), āyatana-kauśalya (in the spheres), pratītyasamutpāda-kauśalya (in dependent origination), and sthānāsthāna-kauśalya (in the possible-and-impossible). Each is treated through both svalakṣaṇa (specific characteristic) and sāmānya-lakṣaṇa (general characteristic) framing.

The opening list gives the standard five aggregates in order: form, feeling, perception, formations, and consciousness. The local extraction omits saṁjñā-skandhaḥ, listing only four; saṁjñā-skandhaḥ is supplied editorially in the root because the per-aggregate definitions immediately following (sections 3–7) cover all five and the five-aggregate doctrine is universally attested. English flags the supplement as [perception-aggregate,]. See the Supplements entry in [notes/sanskrit-emendation-policy.md](../../../../../notes/sanskrit-emendation-policy.md).

sbh2.7:3.3

The elevenfold mode-analysis: three temporal (past/future/present), two locational (inward/outward), two qualitative (gross/subtle), two valuational (inferior/superior), two distance (far/near). Each form-instance falls under some configuration of these modes.

sbh2.7:8.1

What is skill-in-aggregates?
sbh2.7:8.2
ya etānyathoddiṣṭāndharmān nānātmakatayā ca jānāti |
One who knows these dharmas as taught — by their distinct nature,
sbh2.7:8.3
bahvātmakatayā ca, na tataḥ paramupalabhate |
by their manifold nature, and who does not apprehend anything beyond that,
sbh2.7:8.4

nor conceptually constructs anything beyond that.
sbh2.7:8.5
idamucyate samāsataḥ skandhakauśalyam |
This, in summary, is called skill-in-aggregates.
The threefold definition: knowing the aggregates as distinct from one another (nānātmakatā), as manifold within themselves (bahvātmakatā), and not apprehending or conceptually constructing anything beyond them. The English uses 'nature' rather than 'self-nature' here so the ātmakatā suffix does not get mistaken for a claim about self or svabhāva. The third condition is the soteriological hinge — skill-in-aggregates is the cognitive operation that finds nothing beyond the aggregates.

hetu-pratyaya-kauśalya — 'skill-in-cause-and-condition.' Knowing that each element has its own specific causal-substrate; the eye arises from the eye-element, eye-consciousness from its own seed and lineage. The Buddha's instruction to Revata identifies this as the antidote for the cause-confused practitioner.

sbh2.7:18.1
sthānāsthānakauśalyaṁ punaḥ pratītyasamutpāda eva veditavyam |
Skill-in-the-possible-and-impossible is also to be understood as just dependent-origination.
sthāna-asthāna — literally 'place and not-place'; translates Pali ṭhāna-aṭhāna. The phrase from MN 115 (Bahudhātuka Sutta): 'It is a place / not a place that...' — i.e., 'it is possible / not possible that...' The skill is the cognitive ability to recognize what is causally possible and what is not.
sbh2.7:18.2
tatrāyaṁ viśeṣaḥ |
The distinction is this:
sbh2.7:18.3
sthānāsthānakauśalyena viṣamahetukatāṁ jānāti |
By skill-in-the-possible-and-impossible, he knows differentiated causation —
viṣama-hetukatā — literally 'uneven causation,' i.e., differentiated causation. Causes have specific, not arbitrary, effects: wholesome actions yield wholesome fruits, unwholesome unwished ones. This names the karmic structure of cause-and-effect.
sbh2.7:18.4

that for wholesome and unwholesome actions there is a fruit-of-result;
sbh2.7:18.5
akuśalānāmaniṣṭa iti |
and that for unwholesome actions the fruit is unwished.
sbh2.7:18.6
yadevaṁ jñānamidamucyate sthānāsthānakauśalyam |
When the knowing is thus, this is called skill-in-the-possible-and-impossible.

Closing synthesis: the five skills reduce to two — svalakṣaṇa-kauśalya (skandha-kauśalya) and sāmānya-lakṣaṇa-kauśalya (the remaining four). This maps onto the third and fourth investigation-subjects of the vipaśyanā method.
