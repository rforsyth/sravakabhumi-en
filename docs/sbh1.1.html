Gotrabhūmi: The Stage of Lineage

Śrāvakabhūmi · Sanskrit (Shukla 1973) ↔ English (1 rendering)
← All topicsView English only →

The first of three sub-bhūmis within Yogasthāna I. The gotrabhūmi describes what makes a person fit for the disciple-vehicle path — the conditions that must be in place before descent into practice can begin. It moves from prerequisites (a viable rebirth, faculties, faith, no immediate-retribution karma, support from the dharma and the community) through the path that lineage makes possible (going forth, restraint, dhyāna, the four noble truths, liberation), and finally to identifying the person-types that have the lineage versus those who do not.

sbh1.1:1.2
indriyairavikalatā, āyatanagataḥ prasādaḥ, aparivṛttakarmāntatā |
Intactness of faculties, clarity directed to its sphere, unobstructed action.
sbh1.1:1.3
tatra manuṣyatvaṁ katamat |
What is manuṣyatva (human-male birth)?
Manuṣyatva is the abstract noun manuṣya + -tva, literally 'human-ness.' Asaṅga defines it here as a compound condition: rebirth among humans (1.4) and male body with the male sex-faculty (1.5). Translating manuṣyatva as bare 'being human' produces a jarring tautology at 1.5 — the male-faculty clause would read as restricting humanness rather than as a sub-condition of a technical category. Manuṣyatva is therefore retained in the running translation. The category is the first of the five ātmasampat (endowments-from-self) listed at 1.2 and parallels Abhidharmakośa usage; the Śrāvakabhūmi is a bhikṣu manual, and the bhikṣuṇī lineage has its own treatment elsewhere in the Yogācāra corpus. Puruṣendriya at 1.5 is the male sex-faculty as one of the twenty-two indriyas, distinct from puruṣa (the social-biological category). See glossary entry manuṣyatva.

When someone here has been reborn in the community of humans,

puruṣaśca puruṣendriyeṇa samanvāgato bhavati |

sbh1.1:1.7
āryā ya tane pratyājātiḥ katamā |
What is rebirth in a noble region?
Ārya-āyatana here means 'noble locus' in a geographical sense — paralleling madhya-janapada (Middle Country) in 1.8, the heartland where dharma transmission concentrates, contrasted with pratyanta-janapada (peripheral countries) where renunciant communities and teachings are sparse. The same word āyatana recurs at 1.13 (āyatana-gata prasāda) with a doctrinal referent — the dharma-vinaya as the proper locus of faith — so its two appearances in the ātmasampat list do not refer to the same thing. Both are rendered to keep the sense distinct: 'region' for the geographical āyatana here, 'sphere' for the doctrinal āyatana at 1.13. See glossary entry āyatana.
sbh1.1:1.8
yathāpīhai katyo madhyeṣu ja napadeṣu pratyājāto bhavati pūrvavadyāvadyatra gatiḥ satpuruṣāṇāmiyamucyate āryāyatane pratyājātiḥ |
When someone here has been reborn in the Middle Country, as before — namely wherever the resort of holy persons lies — this is called rebirth in a noble region.
sbh1.1:1.9
indriyairavikalatā katamā |
What is intactness of faculties?
sbh1.1:1.10
yathāpīhaikatyaḥ ajāto bhavatyaneḍaka iti vistaraḥ |
When someone here is born not blind, not deaf, [and so on as elaborated];
sbh1.1:1.11
aṁgapratyaṁgāvikalo vā yadrūpeṇāṁpratyaṁgāvaikalyena śrotrāvaikalyādikena bhavyaḥ kuśalapakṣasamudāgamāya |
with limbs and minor limbs intact, of such a kind that, through the intactness of his limbs and his hearing and so on, he is fit for the gathering of the wholesome.
sbh1.1:1.12
idamucyate indriyā vaikalyaṁ |
This is called intactness of faculties.
Shukla prints idamucyate indriyā vaikalyaṁ ('this is called the defectiveness of faculties') — but the definiendum at 1.9 is indriyair avikalatā (non-defectiveness), so the closing must read indriyāṇām avaikalyam (or indriyāvaikalyam) to match. Treated as a scribal/print error: the leading a- of avaikalyam has been lost, leaving vaikalyam in apparent contradiction with the opening. English silently corrects.
sbh1.1:1.13
āyatanagataḥ prasādaḥ katamaḥ |
What is clarity directed to its sphere?
Prasāda — rendered 'clarity' to keep it distinct from śraddhā 'faith,' which appears alongside it in 1.14 and 1.17. The source distinguishes the two: śraddhā is the active faith one gains, prasāda is the resulting serene-clarity-of-mind state; 1.17 says faith is the 'predominant forerunner' that produces prasāda. The standard Buddhist Studies rendering of prasāda is 'serene faith' / 'serene confidence' / 'lucid confidence,' which captures the faith-component bare 'clarity' loses; 'clarity' is kept here on consistency grounds and because śraddhā already carries the faith-rendering, but the connotation should be heard as faith-induced lucidity, not bare cognitive transparency. Āyatana-gata — 'gone-to/lodged-in [its proper] āyatana'; here the āyatana is the dharma-vinaya as object-locus of faith-clarity (1.16), distinct from the geographical ārya-āyatana at 1.7. Rendered 'directed to its sphere' with 'sphere' chosen over 'locus' to make the doctrinal-domain sense audible.

yathāpīhaikatyena tathāgatapravedite dharmavinaye śraddhā pratilabdhā bhavati |
When someone here, in the dharma-vinaya proclaimed by the Tathāgata, gains faith,

clarity of mind.

ayamucyate āyatanagataḥ prasādastadāyatanaṁ tathāgatapravedito dharmavinayaḥ sarveṣāṁ laukikalokottarāṇāṁ śukladharmāṇāmutpattaye |

sbh1.1:1.18
sarvakleśamalakāluṣyāpanayanā aparivṛttakarmāntatā yena paṁcānāmānantaryāṇāṁ karmaṇāṁ, tadyathā mātṛvadhāt pitṛvadhādarhadvadhātsaṁghabhedāttathāgatasyāntike duṣṭacittarucirotpādādanyatamānyatamadānantaryaṁ karma dṛṣṭa eva dharme na kṛtaṁ bhavati nādhyācaritamiyamucyate |
Unobstructed action: [the state in which] the five actions of immediate retribution — matricide, patricide, killing an arhat, schism in the saṅgha, or drawing blood from the Tathāgata with hostile intent — have not been done, have not been committed by him in this very life. This is called
aparivṛttakarmāntatā literally 'the state in which the karma-end has not been turned-around/sealed.' The five ānantarya (immediate-retribution) actions, once committed, are held to render the noble path unattainable in this very life — the karmic outcome is fixed. The contrast term in 1.21 (parivṛttakarmāntatā) is rendered 'obstructed action' for the same reason.
sbh1.1:1.19
aparivṛttakarmāntatā |
unobstructed action.
sbh1.1:1.20
itīmāni pañcānantaryāṇi karmāṇi kṛtopacitāni |
Conversely: when the five actions of immediate retribution have been done and accumulated,
sbh1.1:1.21
dṛṣṭa eva dharme parivartyābhavyo bhavati parinirvāṇāyāryamārgasyotpattaye tasmādetāni parivṛttakarmāntatetyucyate |
in this very life [the doer] becomes unfit for the arising of the noble path leading to nirvāṇa; that is why these are called obstructed action.
sbh1.1:1.22
svayamevānena sa ātmabhāva ebhiḥ pañcabhiraṅgaiḥ sampādito bhavati |
By himself, this individual existence has been brought to fulfillment by these five factors.
ātmabhāva ('individual existence' / 'this present body-and-mind') and ātmasampat ('endowment-from-self') are distinct in the source. The first names what is brought to fulfillment; the second names the resulting category — the eight factors that arise within one's own continuum, contrasted with parasampat (endowment-from-others) in 1.24.
sbh1.1:1.23
tasmādātmasampadityucyate |
That is why it is called endowment-from-self.
sbh1.1:1.24
parasampat katamā |
What is endowment-from-others?
sbh1.1:1.25
tadyathā buddhānāmutpādaḥ, saddharmadeśanādeśitānāṁ dharmāṇāṁ avasthānamavasthitānāṁ cānupravarttanaṁ |
Namely: the arising of Buddhas; the establishment of the proclaimed dharmas of the true teaching; the further transmission of those established dharmas;
sbh1.1:1.26
parataśca pratyanukampā |
and the responsive support of others.
parataḥ pratyanukampā — literally 'sympathy-in-response from another.' Rendered 'responsive support of others' to capture that this is concrete material support (food, robes, lodging, medicine) given in response to a renunciant's situation, not abstract benevolence.
sbh1.1:1.27
tatraḥ buddhānāmutpādaḥ katamaḥ |
What is the arising of Buddhas?
sbh1.1:1.28
yathāpīhaikatyaḥ sarvasarvasatve ttve ṣu kalyāṇaṁ hitādhyāśayamutpādya prabhūtairduṣkarasahasrairmahatā ca puṇyajñānasambhāreṇa ātmabhāvapratilambhe bodhimaṇḍe niṣadya, pañcanivaraṇāni prahāya, caturṣu smṛtyupasthāneṣu sūpasthitacittaḥ, saptatriṁśad bodhipakṣyān dharmān bhāvayitvā a nuttarāṁ samyaksaṁbodhimabhisaṁbudhyate |
When someone, having generated benevolent intention toward all beings, attains [a final] individual existence through thousands of difficult deeds and a great accumulation of merit and knowledge — and seated on the seat of awakening, having abandoned the five hindrances, with mind well-established in the four foundations of mindfulness, having cultivated the thirty-seven dharmas conducive to awakening, he awakens to supreme, perfect awakening.
sbh1.1:1.29
ayamucyate buddhānā mu tpādaḥ |
This is called the arising of Buddhas.
sbh1.1:1.30
atītānāgatapratyutpanneṣvadhvasu evamevā kleśa ā yā ta evaṁ buddhā bhagavanto loka utpadya tasyaiva ca śrāvakalokānukampāmupādāya catvāryāryasatyānyārabhya duḥkha sadbhirayañca dharmo niryāto, deśitaḥ, praśasto buddhaiśca buddhaśrāvakaiścā yamucyate buddhānāmutpādaḥ |
In past, future, and present times, just so [as described], blessed Buddhas arise in the world; and out of compassion for the world of disciples, taking up the four noble truths beginning with suffering, this dharma is brought forth, taught, and praised by the Buddhas and by the disciples of the Buddhas — this is called the arising of Buddhas.
sbh1.1:1.31
deśitānāṁ dharmāṇāṁ avasthānaṁ katamat |
What is the establishment of the taught dharmas?
sbh1.1:1.32
deśite saddharme, pravartite dharmacakre, yāvacca buddho bhagavāṁ vān jīvati, tiṣṭhati ca, parinirvṛte ca buddhe bhagavati, yāvatā kālena pratipattirna hīyate, saddharmaśca nāntardhīyate |
When the true dharma has been taught, the dharma-wheel set in motion, for as long as the blessed Buddha lives and remains, and after the blessed Buddha has attained final nirvāṇa, for as long as practice does not decline and the true dharma does not disappear,
sbh1.1:1.33
idamucyate saddharmasyāvasthānaṁ yāva vasthānaṁ veditavyaṁ |
this is called the establishment of the true dharma — up to that time the establishment is to be understood.
sbh1.1:1.34
avasthitānāṁ dharmāṇāmanupravarttanaṁ katamat |
What is the further transmission of the established dharmas?
sbh1.1:1.35
yatta evādhigantāra ḥ saddharmasya, saddharmasākṣātkriyāyai bhavyāṁ pratibalatāṁ jānatāṁ viditvā yathādhigatāmevānulomikīmavavādānuśāsanīmanupravarttayantīdamucyate avasthitānāṁ dharmāṇāmanupravarttanaṁ |
Those who are themselves attainers of the true dharma, knowing those fit and capable for the realization of the true dharma, transmit the conforming admonition and instruction in accord with their own attainment — this is called the further transmission of the established dharmas.
sbh1.1:2.1

What is the responsive support of others?
sbh1.1:2.2

What is wholesome dharma-desire?
sbh1.1:3.2
yathāpīhaikatyastathāgatasya vā, tathāgataśrāvakasya vā antikāddharmaṁ śrutvā śraddhāṁ pratilabhate |
When someone here, hearing the dharma from the Tathāgata or from a disciple of the Tathāgata, gains faith.

sa tāṁ pratilabhya idaṁ saṁśikṣate |
Having gained it, he trains thus:

What is going forth?
sbh1.1:4.2

[Damaged/Conjectural]: Making that wholesome dharma-desire predominant, [one goes forth] through the formal verbal act with motion as fourth, or through the undertaking of the training precepts from those skilled in full ordination.
The going-forth definition is textually unstable around upasampat-kuśalair aśīḥśīla-samādānaṁ vā. The first half clearly refers to ordination through the formal jñapti-caturtha verbal act; the second half is taken functionally as undertaking training precepts from those skilled in full ordination. The English is marked conjectural/corrupt rather than treating the extracted phrase as secure.

sbh1.1:8.1
jāgarikānuyogaḥ katamaḥ |
What is application to wakefulness?
sbh1.1:8.2
sadivā caṁkramaniṣadyābhyāmāvaraṇīyebhyo dharmebhyaścittaṁ pari śodhayati |
By day, by walking and sitting, he purifies the mind of obscuring dharmas.
sbh1.1:8.3

This is called application to wakefulness in the early and late watches of the night.

sbh1.1:8.6
iyamucyate samprajānadvihāritā |
This is called dwelling with full awareness.

yo jñeye vastuni tīvramohaśca bhavatyāyatamohaścāyamucyate mohacaritaḥ pudgalaḥ |
He who, with respect to a knowable object, has intense delusion and prolonged delusion — this is called the person of delusion-conduct.

What are the persons born in an unfavorable moment, heedless, wrongly practiced?
sbh1.1:24.2
akṣaṇo papannāḥ pramattāmithyāpratipannānāvṛtā pannā anāvṛtā veditavyā ḥ |
Persons born in an unfavorable moment, heedless, wrongly practiced, obscured, not obscured — these are to be understood accordingly.
sbh1.1:25.1
dūre pudgalaḥ katamaḥ |
What is the person who is far?
sbh1.1:25.2
asti pudgalaḥ kāladūratayā nirvāṇasya dūre |
There is a person far from nirvāṇa by remoteness in time.
sbh1.1:25.3
asti prayogadūratayā |
There is one far by remoteness in application.
sbh1.1:25.4
tatra katamaḥ kāladūratayā dūre |
What is one far by remoteness in time?
sbh1.1:25.5
anekairjātiśatairanekairjātiśatasahasraiḥ anekajātiśatasahasraiḥ tataḥ paścād bhavyo bhavatyāśupratyayalābhāya |
After many hundreds of births, many hundreds of thousands of births, after that he becomes fit for swift attainment of conditions,
sbh1.1:25.6
parinirvāṇāya |
for final nirvāṇa.
sbh1.1:25.7
tatra |
There,
sbh1.1:25.8
prayogadūratayā pu dgalo gotra eva kevale sthito bhavati |
by remoteness in application, the person is established only in the lineage,
sbh1.1:25.9
nāvatīrṇaḥ |
not yet descended.
sbh1.1:25.10
sa bhavyo bhavatyāśupratyayalābhāya |
He is fit for the swift attainment of conditions,
sbh1.1:25.11
parinirvāṇāya |
for final nirvāṇa.
sbh1.1:25.12
sa nirvāṇāyānārabdhaprayogadūratayā dūratvāt prayoga kāladūratayā ayamucyate dūre pudgalaḥ |
He, by remoteness through not having begun application toward nirvāṇa, by the remoteness in application — this is called the person who is far.
sbh1.1:26.1
āsanne pudgalaḥ |
The person who is near [is ready to descend into the path].
The gotrabhūmi ends mid-list. The categories enumerated at 14.4–14.7 ('matured / not matured', 'purified / not purified', 'far / near') are not all given their own definition blocks here: 'far' is defined at 25, but 'matured', 'purified', and 'near' transition directly into the avatārabhūmi. In the source, āsanne pudgalaḥ is followed immediately by II. avatārabhūmiḥ — yaiḥ ṣaḍbhir bhavyo…, so the next file ([sbh1.2](sbh1.2.html#sbh1.2:1.1)) effectively serves as the answer to 'what is the near person?' (becoming near to nirvāṇa is what the descent-stage describes).
