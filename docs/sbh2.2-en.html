Foulness

Śrāvakabhūmi · English working translation
← All topicsView side-by-side ↔

The first of the five carita-viśodhana ālambana — meditation objects matched to specific temperaments — corresponding to the rāga-carita (lust-temperament) practitioner. The Śrāvakabhūmi enumerates six kinds of foulness: pratyaśubhatā (foulness of becoming), duḥkhāśubhatā (foulness of suffering), vāsanāśubhatā, gantv-āśubhatā, prāptyāśubhatā, and sākalyāśubhatā — covering the foulness of inner aggregates, outer cemetery contemplations, and the five kinds of lust they each address.

What is foulness? It is said: Foulness is sixfold. Namely: inherent foulness, foulness of suffering, foulness of inferiority, relative foulness, foulness of defilement, and foulness of perishability.

What is inherent foulness? It is said: Inherent foulness has two aspects: taken inwardly, and taken outwardly.

Taken inwardly: hair of the head, body hair, nails, teeth, dust, dirt, skin, flesh, bone, sinew, vein, kidneys, heart, spleen, lungs, intestines, mesentery, stomach contents, bowel contents, urine, feces, tears, sweat, saliva, mucus, fat, lymph, marrow, grease, bile, phlegm, pus, blood, brain, cranial fluid, urine.

Taken outwardly, what is the inherent foulness? The discolored, the festering, the bursting, the swollen, the gnawed; the bloody-skeletal; the dispersed; the bone; the skeleton. Or: things made of excrement, urine, saliva, or mucus; things smeared with blood or pus; dung-heaps, gutters. And anything else of this kind — this is outward inherent foulness. Inward inherent foulness and outward inherent foulness together — this is called inherent foulness.

What is foulness of suffering? The painful feeling that arises in dependence on a contact conditioned by painful feeling — bodily and mental, the disagreeable felt experience. This is called foulness of suffering.

What is foulness of inferiority? Whatever is the most inferior substance, the most inferior realm — namely, the desire-realm. There is no realm more inferior, lower, or more rejected than this. This is called foulness of inferiority.

What is relative foulness? A certain object, although in itself fair, is reckoned foul in relation to something fairer. For example: in relation to the formless realm, the form-realm is reckoned foul. And: in relation to the cessation of *satkāya* — *nirvāṇa* — even the peak of existence is reckoned a foul existence. This is the relative foulness of this kind.

All the fetters, bondages, latent tendencies, and secondary defilements operating in the three realms — this is called foulness of defilement.

What is foulness of perishability? The impermanence, unsteadiness, unreliability, and property-of-being-subject-to-change of the five aggregates of clinging. This is the foulness for the purification of the lustful-temperament practitioner; it is the [meditative] object.

Concerning that lust, namely: inwardly toward sense-pleasures, sense-desire and sense-lust; outwardly toward sense-pleasures, sexual desire and sexual lust; object-desire and object-lust; form-desire and form-lust; *satkāya*-desire and *satkāya*-lust; this is fivefold lust. For the abandoning, dispelling, and non-occurrence of this fivefold lust, the sixfold foulness is the [appropriate] object.

Of these: taken inwardly — by the inward inherent-foulness object, he purifies the mind from sense-desire and sense-lust toward inward sense-pleasures. By the outward inherent-foulness object, he purifies the mind from the four kinds of lust outwardly conjoined — color-lust, form-lust, contact-lust, and approach-lust.

When he attends to the discolored, the festering, the bursting, or the swollen, then he purifies the mind from color-lust. When he attends to the bloody-skeletal, then he purifies the mind from form-lust. When he attends to the bone or the skeleton, then he purifies the mind from contact-lust. When he attends to the dispersed, he purifies the mind from approach-lust.

Therefore: the outward inherent-foulness was established by the Blessed One in the four charnel-ground reflections. Whatever charnel-ground he has seen — one day dead, or seven days dead, being eaten by crows, vultures, dogs, jackals — there, there, he draws the body-comparison to himself: "This body of mine is also of such a nature, of such a coming-to-be, has not gone beyond such a *dharmatā*." By this much, the cemetery progression from the discolored to the gnawed has been named. When he further says: "The charnel-ground he has seen — flesh, sinew, blood departed, well-bound" — by this, the bloody-skeletal has been named. When he further says: "Those very charnel-ground sites — the spine, the jaw with teeth, the skull — broken and rebroken, of one year, two years, up to seven years, white, conch-colored, dove-colored, mixed with dust-color, are seen" — by this, the dispersed has been named.

Thus, by the outward-conjoined inherent-foulness object, together with *maitrī*, he purifies the mind from lust. By the suffering-foulness object and the inferiority-foulness object, he purifies the mind from sense-object-conjoined sense-lust. By the relative-foulness object, he purifies the mind from form-lust. By the defilement-foulness object and the perishability-foulness object — covering the desire-realm up to the peak of existence — he purifies the mind from all body-lust. This is what is meant by [the practice] for the lustful-temperament practitioner: purifying-of-conduct, with object. The whole sixfold foulness-object is comprehended in this matter; what is properly intended here is inherent foulness. The other five foulnesses are objects also useful for the purification of *other* temperaments.
