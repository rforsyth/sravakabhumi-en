Śrāvakabhūmi

A working bilingual edition of Asaṅga's Śrāvakabhūmi, in Sanskrit (Shukla 1973) and English.

Introduction

Śrāvakabhūmi means "The Stage of the Disciple" — from śrāvaka, literally "hearer," the term for those who heard the Buddha's teaching directly and trained for liberation as arhats, and bhūmi, "ground" or "stage." It is a section of the Yogācārabhūmi-Śāstra, the encyclopedic treatise on yogic practice traditionally ascribed to Asaṅga (4th century CE). Organized into four yogasthānas ("yogic foundations"), it lays out a detailed program of contemplative training: the nine stages of mental abiding (śamatha), the method of insight (vipaśyanā), the five temperament-matched practices (unattractiveness, lovingkindness, dependent origination, elements, breath), and the seven attentions of the mundane path.

The Sanskrit original was long thought lost in India until 1938, when the explorer-scholar Rāhula Sāṅkṛtyāyana located and photographed a palm-leaf manuscript at Shalu Monastery in Tibet; those photographs are the basis for every modern Sanskrit edition, including the source text used here.

Textual status

The Sanskrit layer is based on Shukla 1973 (via SuttaCentral) but is not a fully diplomatic transcription. Obvious PDF/OCR extraction artifacts — chiefly intra-word whitespace breaks — have been normalized; a small number of documented restorations and supplements are recorded in the emendation policy. The English marks damaged or conjecturally reconstructed passages inline. For close philological argument, cite the segment ID and check the Sanskrit against Shukla and, where available, against the Tibetan and Chinese witnesses.
