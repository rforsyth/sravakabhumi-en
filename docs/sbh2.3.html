Lovingkindness

Śrāvakabhūmi · Sanskrit (Shukla 1973) ↔ English (1 rendering)
← All topicsView English only →

The second of the five carita-viśodhana practices, corresponding to the dveṣa-carita (hatred-temperament) practitioner. The Śrāvakabhūmi treats maitrī in its definitional aspect — three marks (mitra-pakṣa, friend-side; amitra-pakṣa, enemy-side; udāsīna-pakṣa, neutral-side), three intensities, the brahma-vihāra division of labor across the four immeasurables, and the temperament rationale for why hatred yields specifically to the cultivation of friendliness. Procedural development of the practice itself is given separately in the vipaśyanā illustration in sbh3.2.

sbh2.3:3.2
vipulena mahadgatena pramāṇenetyanena mṛdumadhyādhimātrasya sukhasyopasaṁhāra ākhyātaḥ |
'vast, exalted, immeasurable' — by this, the bringing of mild, middling, or strong happiness is named:
The intensity-gradation mapped onto cosmological structure: mild = kāmāvacara (desire-realm pleasure), middling = first-or-second-jhāna prīti-sukha (rapture-and-ease), strong = third-jhāna niṣprītika-sukha (ease without rapture). The root prints pramāṇena, but the standard brahmavihāra formula requires apramāṇena ('immeasurable'); translation follows the formula while leaving the Sanskrit unchanged. A practitioner can wish for others a happiness they themselves have access to.

should be understood as directed toward those who are neither-suffering-nor-happy, desiring happiness, on the friend-side, enemy-side, or neutral-side.
aduḥkhāsukhita — literally 'non-suffering-non-happy,' the neutral baseline. The brahmavihāra division of labor: this neutral being is the object of maitrī; the suffering one is the object of karuṇā; the happy one is the object of muditā.
sbh2.3:4.5
yastu duḥkhito vā, aduḥkhito vā punarmitrapakṣaḥ |
Whereas for the one who is suffering, or the non-suffering [that is, happy] one, on the friend-side,
The source has aduḥkhito (non-suffering) where context suggests sukhito (happy); preserved as-is. The classification distinguishes the suffering being (→ karuṇā) from the happy being (→ muditā); the Sanskrit reading is likely correct as 'non-suffering' = 'happy.'

sbh2.3:5.1
tatra vyāpādacaritaḥ pudgalaḥ maitrīṁ bhāvayan sattveṣu yo vyāpādastaṁ pratanu karoti |
The ill-will-conduct person, cultivating lovingkindness, attenuates the ill-will toward beings.
Vyāpāda-carita is kept as ill-will-conduct, distinct from the broader dveṣa-carita hatred-conduct label used in the five-practice summary. Pratanu karoti — 'attenuates,' literally 'makes thin.' The verb is precise: maitrī practice does not eliminate ill-will in a single move but progressively thins it. Same gradual-pacification model as the nine mental abidings.
sbh2.3:5.2
vyāpādāccittaṁ pariśodhayati |
He purifies the mind from ill-will.
