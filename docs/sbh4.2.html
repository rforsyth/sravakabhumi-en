Higher Jhāna and Formless Transitions

Śrāvakabhūmi · Sanskrit (Shukla 1973) ↔ English (1 rendering)
← All topicsView English only →

The seven manaskāras of sbh4.1 generalized through the higher transitions, governed by the audārika-lakṣaṇa (gross-characteristic) pattern: each higher attainment is reached by recognizing the grossness of the lower and the peace of the higher. Seven transitions: 1st→2nd, 2nd→3rd, 3rd→4th jhāna; 4th jhāna→ākāśānantyāyatana, ākāśa→vijñāna, vijñāna→ākiñcanya, ākiñcanya→naivasaṁjñā-nāsaṁjñāyatana. Plus the two non-conscious attainments (asaṁjñā- and nirodha-samāpatti) and the five abhijñā (super-knowledges) with their twelve perceptions.

Higher Jhāna and Formless Transitions
sbh4.2:1.1
audārikalakṣaṇaṁ punaḥ sarvāsvadharasu bhūmiṣu yāvadākiñcanyāyatanāt samāsena dvividhaṁ veditavyaṁ |
The gross-characteristic in all these lower planes — up through the *ākiñcanyāyatana* — is, in summary, twofold:
sbh4.2:1.2
duḥkhataraṁ vihāritā cādharādhobhūmīnāmapraśāntavihāritā ca |
greater suffering-character of the lower-and-lower planes, and non-calm-abiding-character,
sbh4.2:1.3
alpāyuṣkatarā ca |
and lesser-life-span-character.
Three criteria of grossness at every level: lower planes are (1) more suffering, (2) less calm-abiding, (3) shorter-lived. Alpāyuṣka-tarā refers to cosmological life-spans — beings reborn in higher planes live longer. For meditative purposes, this connects felt brevity-of-states at lower planes (states keep dropping away) with felt persistence-of-states at higher planes.
sbh4.2:1.4
ityetad dvividhamaudārikalakṣaṇaṁ |
Thus this twofold gross-characteristic.
sbh4.2:1.5
ṣaḍbhirvastubhiryathāyogaṁ paryeṣate |
He investigates by the six investigation-subjects, as appropriate.
sbh4.2:1.6
yasyā yasyā bhūmervairāgyaṁ kartukāmo bhavatyupariṣṭācca yathāyogaṁ śāntalakṣaṇaṁ |
For whichever plane he wishes to make dispassion toward, the calm-characteristic is the plane above it, as appropriate —
sbh4.2:1.7
yāvat prayoganiṣṭhāphalān manaskārāt tatra viviktaṁ kāmairiti |
up through the practice-culminating-fruit attention. Then he is 'secluded from sense-pleasures,' or at higher levels, secluded from the analogous gross-content.
sbh4.2:2.1
savitarkasavicāraviviktebhyaścittaṁ vyāvartayitvā tat vyāvartya |
[At the 1st→2nd transition:] Turning the mind away from the *savitarka-savicāra* objects,
sbh4.2:2.2
avitarkāvicārasamādhinimitteṣūpanibadhnāti |
he binds the mind to *avitarka-avicāra-samādhi-nimittas*.
sbh4.2:2.3
vyagracāriṇa ālambanād vivecya |
Withdrawing from the distractedly-moving object,
sbh4.2:2.4
avyagracāriṇyālambane ekadharmatayā śāntaṁ prasannaṁ cittaṁ pravartate |
in the non-distractedly-moving object, by single-dharma-character, calm, settled, the mind proceeds.
sbh4.2:2.5
vyavasthāpayati |
He establishes it.
sbh4.2:2.6
tenāha vitarkavicārāṇāṁ vyupaśamādadhyātmasaṁprasādanāt sabhāvanābhyāsāt tasyaivāvitarkāvicārasya samādheḥ, vitarkavicārasya samādheḥ, sacchidrasāntarāmavasthāmatikramya niśchidranirantarāmavasthāṁ prāpnoti |
Therefore: 'By the calming of *vitarka* and *vicāra*, by inward-confidence' — by the practice of *avitarka-avicāra-samādhi*, transcending the with-cleft, with-interval state, he attains the without-cleft, without-interval state.
Sac-chidra-sāntara (with-cleft, with-interval) describes first-jhāna attention as interrupted — the vitarka-vicāra create momentary clefts where attention falters before re-grasping the object. Niś-chidra-nirantara (without-cleft, without-interval) is the second jhāna's seamlessly continuous attention. A precise phenomenological description of the 1st→2nd transition.
sbh4.2:2.7
tenāha cetasa ekotībhāvāt sarveṇa sarvaṁ vitarkavicāraprahāṇādavitarkamavicāraṁ prayoganiṣṭho manaskāraḥ samādhistasyānantaraṁ taddhetukaṁ tatpratyayamutpadyate |
Therefore: 'By one-pointedness of mind' — by completely abandoning *vitarka-vicāra* in every way, the *avitarka-avicāra* practice-culminating attention is the *samādhi*. Immediately following, conditioned by it, the next arises.
sbh4.2:2.8
tenāha samādhijaṁ īpsitāninditārthaprāpteḥ prītau vā doṣadarśanāt |
Therefore: 'Born from concentration' — from the attainment of the wished-for, undeplored object, or from seeing the fault in *prīti* coming next.
sbh4.2:2.9
sa saṁpraharṣagataṁ daurmanasyagataṁ vitarkavicāraprathamadhyānakleśapakṣasarvadauṣṭhulyāpagamāt tatprātipakṣikapraśrabdhicittakāyakarmaṇyatāsuravānugatatvāt |
Pacifying gone-to-elation and gone-to-dejection, by the disappearance of all the obstructions belonging to the *vitarka-vicāra*-first-*dhyāna*-defilement side, the counter-action of which is *praśrabdhi*, the workableness of mind-and-body —
sbh4.2:2.10
prītisukhamanupūrveṇa gaṇayato dvitīyaṁ bhavatyevaṁ sarvaṁ pūrvavad veditavyam |
'Rapture-and-happiness' — by the same understanding as before. By progression, the second comes to be. Everything else as before.
sbh4.2:3.1
prītinimitteṣu doṣaṁ paśyati |
He sees the fault in the rapture-signs.
sbh4.2:3.2
tenāha prīter virāgāt |
Therefore: 'From dispassion-toward-rapture.'
sbh4.2:3.3
tasmiṁśca samaye dvividho 'sya cittakṣobhakaraḥ apakṣālo 'dhigato bhavati |
At that time, he has attained two mind-disturbance-makers, two obstructions:
sbh4.2:3.4
niḥprītike tṛtīyadhyāne cittaṁ pradadhataḥ |
in the rapture-less third *dhyāna*, settling the mind,
sbh4.2:3.5
dvitīye ca dhyāne vitarkavicārāḥ, etarhi ca prītiḥ, tenāha upekṣako viharati |
and in the second *dhyāna* there were *vitarka-vicāra*; here it is rapture. Therefore: 'He abides in equanimity.'
sbh4.2:3.6
etau hi dvau dharmau cittasaṁkṣobhakarau |
Both these dharmas are mind-disturbance-makers,
Prīti (rapture) is unambiguously good at first and second jhāna, yet for the practitioner moving to third jhāna it must be recognized as gross — as a citta-kṣobha-kara (mind-disturbance-maker) that obstructs nirantarā upekṣā. The engine of the mundane path: what was the achievement of the prior level becomes the obstruction at the next.
sbh4.2:3.7
nirantarāyā upekṣāyā vighnakārakau |
obstructors of without-interval equanimity.
sbh4.2:3.8
tatra prathame dhyāne vitarkavicārā bhavanti |
In the first *dhyāna* there are *vitarka-vicāra*,
sbh4.2:3.9
yena nirantaropekṣā na pravartate |
by which without-interval equanimity does not proceed.
sbh4.2:3.10
dvitīye dhyāne prītirbhavati |
In the second *dhyāna* there is rapture,
sbh4.2:3.11
yenātrāpi nirantaropekṣā na pravartate |
by which here too without-interval equanimity does not proceed.
sbh4.2:3.12
tenāyaṁ dhyāyī prathamadvitīyeṣu dhyāneṣu nāsti, tena tṛtīye dhyāne upekṣako viharatītyucyate |
Therefore in this contemplator, in the first and second *dhyāna*s, equanimity is not yet; for which reason in the third *dhyāna* 'he abides in equanimity' is said.
sbh4.2:3.13
sa upekṣakaḥ saṁstathā saṁstathopasthitasmṛtirviharati |
He, abiding in equanimity, abides thus with mindfulness established.
sbh4.2:3.14
yathā yathā te prītisahagatāḥ saṁjñāmanasikārāḥ samudācaranti |
As perception-and-attention conjoined-with-rapture occur —
sbh4.2:3.15
sa cet punarabhāvitatvāt tṛtīyasya dhyānasya smṛtisaṁpramoṣāt kadācitkarhicit citte prītisahagatāḥ saṁjñāmanasikārāḥ samudācaranti |
if, due to non-cultivation of the third *dhyāna*, by lapse-of-mindfulness, occasionally perception-and-attention conjoined-with-rapture occur in the mind —
sbh4.2:3.16
tāṁstān laghu laghveva prajñayā pratividhyati |
those, those, lightly, lightly indeed, by wisdom he penetrates.
Laghu laghv eva prajñayā pratividhyati — 'lightly, lightly indeed, by wisdom he penetrates.' The mechanism of third jhāna: prīti-traces still arise but are met with quick wisdom rather than effortful suppression. The two stabilizing factors are smṛti (mindfulness) and saṁprajanya (clear-knowing).
sbh4.2:3.17
samyageva prajānāti |
He properly understands.
sbh4.2:3.18
utpannotpannāṁśca nādhivāsayati |
As they arise, arise, he does not endorse them.
sbh4.2:3.19
prajahāti vinodayati |
He abandons, dispels;
sbh4.2:3.20
vyantīkaroti, cittamadhyupekṣate |
pushes out; equanimously regards the mind.
sbh4.2:3.21
tenāha smṛtaḥ saṁprajānanniti |
Therefore: 'Mindful and clearly-knowing.'
sbh4.2:4.1
tasya tasmin samaye evamupekṣakasya viharatā smṛtasya saṁprajanyasya āsevanānvayād bahulīkārānvayāt prītisahagataṁ prahīyate |
By that equanimity, by frequent practice, the rapture-conjoined factors are abandoned.
sbh4.2:4.2
taccittauddhatyakaraṁ, niḥprītikaṁ, śāntaṁ, praśāntaṁ cetasi veditamutpadyate |
The mind-agitating-maker — the rapture-less, calm, fully-calmed equanimity — arises in the heart, as known.
sbh4.2:4.3
prītiprātidvandvyena tasmin samaye rūpakāyena, manaḥkāyena vedita sukhaṁ ca praśrabdhisukhaṁ pratisamvedayate |
By counter-action to rapture, at that time, in the form-body and the mind-body, happiness and *praśrabdhi*-happiness are experienced.
sbh4.2:4.4
tṛtīyācca dhyānāt |
From the third *dhyāna* below,
sbh4.2:4.5
adhastad rūpaṁ sukhaṁ nāsti nāpi nirantarā upekṣā tṛtīyāddhyānādūrdhvaṁ yadapyupekṣopalabhyate |
that form-happiness does not exist; nor does without-interval equanimity. From the third *dhyāna* upward, although equanimity is found,
sbh4.2:4.6
na tu sukhaṁ |
happiness is not.
sbh4.2:4.7
tatrādhaḥ sukhopekṣābhāvādūrdhvaṁ ca sukhābhāvāt |
Below, sense-pleasure-equanimity is absent; above, happiness is absent.
The third jhāna is the unique zone where calm-equanimity meets felt happiness. Below it, you get happiness without continuous equanimity; above it, you get equanimity without happiness. Why classical texts sometimes call the third jhāna the most pleasant attainment — it is structurally pleasant in a way no other attainment is.
sbh4.2:4.8
idaṁ tadāyatanaṁ yaduta tṛtīyaṁ dhyānaṁ yat tadāryā ācakṣate |
This is that base — the third *dhyāna* — which the noble ones declare.
sbh4.2:4.9
yatpratilambhavihāriṇaṁ pudgalamadhikṛtya smṛtimān sukhavihārī tṛtīyaṁ dhyānamupasaṁpadya viharatīti āryāḥ punaḥ vṛddhāśca vṛddhaśrāvakāśca |
In dependence on this, the attaining-and-abiding person who is mindful and the happy-abider attains and abides in the third *dhyāna* — so the noble ones, the elders, the elder disciples say.
sbh4.2:5.1
tatrātulyajātīyatvāt |
Among the noble, there is no equality of category.
sbh4.2:5.2
pratipakṣasya sukhasya prahāṇapratipakṣānākhyātaḥ kṣo 'nākhyātaḥ |
Of the antidote to *sukha*, the abandonment-antidote is unannounced.
sbh4.2:5.3
yadeva tatpratipakṣakṛtaṁ sukhaprahāṇaṁ tadevākhyātaṁ |
What is, by counter-action to that, *sukha-prahāṇa* — that is announced.
sbh4.2:5.4
kaḥ punarasau pratipakṣaḥ |
What further is that antidote?
sbh4.2:5.5
yaduta upekṣā smṛtisamprajanyañca |
It is equanimity-mindfulness-clear-knowing.
sbh4.2:5.6
tasya ca niṣevaṇābhyāsāt tṛtīyadhyānāccalito yatra tṛtīyadhyānabhūmisukhaṁ tat prajahāti |
By practicing this, dropping out from the third *dhyāna*, where there is third-*dhyāna*-plane *sukha*, that he abandons.
sbh4.2:5.7
tenāha | sukhasya ca prahāṇāt pūrvameva ca saumanasyadaurmanasyayorastaṅgamāt |
Therefore: 'By abandoning *sukha*, and previously by abandoning *duḥkha*, by the disappearance of *saumanasya* and *daurmanasya*.'
sbh4.2:5.8
tatra caturthadhyānasamāpattikāle tasmin samaye sa dhyāyī sukhaduḥkhavyatikramamanuprāpnoti |
In the fourth-*dhyāna*-attainment-time, that contemplator attains the transcendence of pleasure-and-pain.
sbh4.2:5.9
tena yaśca pūrvaprahīṇaṁ, yaścaitarhi prahīyate |
Of what was previously abandoned, of what is now abandoned,
sbh4.2:5.10
tasya saṁkalanaṁ kurvannevamāha |
making the summation, he says:
sbh4.2:5.11
sukhasya ca prahāṇāt, duḥkhasya ca prahāṇāt, pūrvameva ca saumanasyadaurmanasyayorastaṅgamāt |
'From the abandoning of pleasure, from the abandoning of pain, from the previous disappearance of joy and dejection.'
sbh4.2:5.12
tanna caturthadhyānasamāpattikāle sukhasya ca prahāṇād dvitīyadhyānasamāpattikāle duḥkhasya, tṛtīyadhyānasamāpattikāle saumanasyasyāstaṅgamāt, prathamadhyānasamāpattikāle daurmanasyasya, asti tāvat sukhaduḥkhayoḥ prahāṇād aduḥkhāsukhaivāsya vedanā, na viśiṣṭā bhavati |
Not in the fourth-*dhyāna*-attainment-time alone is *sukha* abandoned; in second-*dhyāna*-attainment-time *duḥkha* is; in third-*dhyāna*-attainment-time *saumanasya* sets; in first-*dhyāna*-attainment-time *daurmanasya* is set. There is at least, by the abandoning of pleasure-and-pain, neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling for him; not specially-marked.
sbh4.2:5.13
tenāha | aduḥkhāsukhā tasmin samaye prathamaṁ dhyānamupādāya sarve adhobhūmikāḥ apakṣālāḥ prahīṇā bhavanti |
Therefore: 'Neither-painful-nor-pleasant' — at that time, taking the first *dhyāna* as the starting reference, all the lower-plane defilements are abandoned.
sbh4.2:5.14
tadyathā vitarkavicārāḥ, prīti, āśvāsapraśvāsāḥ |
Namely: *vitarka-vicāra*, *prīti*, in-and-out-breaths.
Asanga explicitly lists breath (āśvāsa-praśvāsa) among what is abandoned at the fourth jhāna. The breath itself ceases. A structural marker: if the breath has not become very subtle to the point of apparent cessation, fourth jhāna proper has not been entered.
sbh4.2:5.15
teṣāṁ ca prahāṇād yā tatropekṣā |
By the abandoning of these, the equanimity there arising,
sbh4.2:5.16
smṛtiśca sā pariśuddhā bhavati |
and the mindfulness — that becomes purified,
sbh4.2:5.17
paryavadātā, yenāsya etaccittaṁ caturthadhyānasamāpannasyāniñjyaṁ santiṣṭhate |
fully clarified. By which his mind, having attained the fourth *dhyāna*, stands without movement —
Aniñjya — without-movement, unperturbed. Sarveñjitāyatana — sphere of all-non-perturbation. The structural feature that makes fourth jhāna the launchpad for the formless attainments and the abhijñā. Fourth jhāna is a gateway state, not a destination.
sbh4.2:5.18
sarveñjitāyatanaṁ |
sphere of all-non-perturbation.
sbh4.2:5.19
tenāha | upekṣāsmṛtipariśuddhamiti |
Therefore: 'By the purity of equanimity-and-mindfulness.'
sbh4.2:6.1
tatrākāśādhimokṣasya varṇasaṁjñā nīlapītalohitāvadātādipratisaṁyuktatāmasātāmasātayā nirvirāgatayā ca samatikrānto bhavati |
Of one whose mind is resolved on space, the form-perception — connected with blue-yellow-red-white etc. — is transcended by indifference-toward and dispassion-toward.
sbh4.2:6.2
tenāha | rūpasaṁjñānāṁ samatikramādanābhāsagamanahetoryā anekavidhā bahunānāprakārā varṇapracayahetukā āvaraṇasaṁjñā sā yā sā vigatā bhavati |
Therefore: 'From the transcendence of form-perceptions' — for the cause of the mind proceeding to the limit. The obstruction-perceptions of various kinds, many-and-varied, caused by the accumulation-of-color, those go away.
sbh4.2:6.3
tenāha | pratighasaṁjñānāmastaṅgamāt, tāsāṁ vā punarvigamahetoryā aupacayikī saṁjñā teṣvavaśiṣṭeṣu viśiṣṭeṣu saṁghāteṣu pravṛttāstadyathā bhojanapānavastrālaṅkāragṛhodyānavanasenāparvatādisaṁjñāḥ |
Therefore: 'From the disappearance of obstruction-perceptions.' Or, by the cause-of-disappearance of those, the abundance-perception proceeding in the remaining superior aggregations — namely, perceptions of food, drink, clothing, ornaments, house, garden, forest, army, mountain, etc.
sbh4.2:6.4
teṣu sarveṇa sarvamābhogo 'pyasya na pravartate |
— in those, all and every directed-effort no longer proceeds.
sbh4.2:6.5
tenāha | nānātvasaṁjñānāmamanasikārāt |
Therefore: 'From non-attention to manifold-perceptions.'
The transition from form-realm (4th jhāna) to formless (ākāśānantyāyatana) requires releasing three perception-categories simultaneously: form-perceptions (color), obstruction-perceptions (resistance/objects-as-blocks), and manifold-perceptions (the world of things). The release-mechanism: vairāgya for the first two, amanasi-kāra for the third.
sbh4.2:6.6
sa evaṁ rūpapratighānānātvasaṁjñā bhāvayitvā anantākāreṇa ākāśādhimukto bhavati |
Having thus cultivated form-obstruction-manifold-perception, he becomes resolved on space by the infinity-aspect.
sbh4.2:6.7
tenāha | anantamākāśamaśaṁsasāmantakamatikramya prayoganiṣṭhān manasikārāducca-prayoganiṣṭhāphalaṁ maulaṁ samāpadyate |
Therefore: 'Infinite space' — taking the immediate-vicinity, transcending — from the practice-culminating attention to the high-practice-culminating-fruit, he attains the basic attainment.
sbh4.2:6.8
tenāha | ākāśānantyāyatanamupasaṁpadya viharati |
Therefore: 'Having attained, he abides in the infinity-of-space-base.'
sbh4.2:7.1
tasya yāvanmaulaṁ na samāpadyate |
Until the basic attainment is not attained,
sbh4.2:7.2
tasyākāśamālambanaṁ samāpannasya punastacca tadanye ca skandhāḥ svabhūmikāḥ sāmantake punaradhobhūmikā api skandhāḥ |
for the attainer of the space-as-object stage, that and the other aggregates are own-plane; the immediate-vicinity ones are also lower-plane aggregates.
sbh4.2:7.3
samayena vijñānenānantamākāśamadhimucyate |
By a moment of consciousness, infinite space is resolved-on.
sbh4.2:7.4
tadeva vijñānam anantākārākāśādhimokṣikaṁ |
That very consciousness, with the infinite-aspect of space-resolve —
The most subtle of the formless transitions. The consciousness that has been resolving on space-as-infinite is itself infinite (because its object is infinite). The practitioner shifts the resolve to that consciousness. Formless attainments are recursive applications of the same cognitive operation, not increasingly abstract objects.
sbh4.2:7.5
vijñānānantyāyatanaṁ samāpattukāmaḥ |
wishing to attain the *vijñānānantyāyatana* —
sbh4.2:7.6
ākāśānantyāyatanasaṁjñāṁ vyāvartya |
having turned away the *ākāśānantyāyatana*-perception,
sbh4.2:7.7
tadeva vijñānam anantākāreṇādhimucyate |
that very consciousness is resolved-on with the infinite-aspect.
sbh4.2:7.8
sasāmantake maulamākāśānantyāyatanaṁ samatikramyate |
With its immediate-vicinity, the basic *ākāśānantyāyatana* is transcended.
sbh4.2:7.9
tenāha | sarvaśa ākāśānantyāyatanaṁ samatikramyānantaṁ vijñānamiti |
Therefore: 'Transcending the *ākāśānantyāyatana* completely, infinite consciousness.'
sbh4.2:7.10
sa vijñānānantyāyatanamiti sāmantakaṁ samatikramya yāvat prayoganiṣṭhān manasikarāt maulaprayoganiṣṭhāphalaṁ samāpadyate |
He, transcending the immediate-vicinity of *vijñānānantyāyatana*, from the practice-culminating attention to the basic-practice-culminating-fruit, attains.
sbh4.2:7.11
tenāha | vijñānānantyāyatanamupasaṁpadya viharatīti |
Therefore: 'Having attained, he abides in the *vijñānānantyāyatana*.'
sbh4.2:8.1
sa vijñānānantyāyatanāducchalito vijñānāt pareṇālambanaṁ samanveṣamāṇo na punarlabhate |
He, dropping out from the *vijñānānantyāyatana*, searching for an object beyond consciousness, does not find one —
sbh4.2:8.2
kiñcana pratisaṁyuktaṁ rūpi vā, arūpi vā |
neither connected with form, nor formless.
sbh4.2:8.3
sa tadālambanamalabhamānaḥ sasāmāntakam maulaṁ vijñānānantyāyatanaṁ samatikramya nāsti kiñcidanyadālambanamadhimucyate |
Not finding that object, transcending the *vijñānānantyāyatana*-with-its-immediate-vicinity, he resolves: 'There is nothing else as an object.'
The mechanism for moving to ākiñcanyāyatana is via negativa. The practitioner searches for an object beyond consciousness — and fails to find one. The failure to find is itself the resolution. 'Nothingness' is not a metaphysical claim about emptiness or void — it is the contemplative recognition that no further object is available.
sbh4.2:8.4
so akiñcanasaṁjñādhimukta eva bhavati |
He becomes resolved on the perception of nothingness.
sbh4.2:8.5
sa tasya saṁjñādhimokṣasya bahulīkārānvayādākiñcanyāyatanasāmantakaṁ samatikramya yāvat prayoganiṣṭhān manasikārān maulaṁ prayoganiṣṭhāphale samāpadyate |
By the abundance of this perception-resolve, transcending the *ākiñcanyāyatana*-immediate-vicinity, from the practice-culminating attention, he attains the basic practice-culminating-fruit.
sbh4.2:8.6
tenāha | sarvaśo vijñānānantyāyatanaṁ samatikramya nāsti kiñciditi ākiñcanyāyatanamupasaṁspṛśya saṁpadya viharatīti |
Therefore: 'Transcending the *vijñānānantyāyatana* completely, there is nothing, the *ākiñcanyāyatana* — having attained, he abides.'
sbh4.2:9.1
tenāha | saṁjñī yaduta ākiñcanyāyatanāducchalitaḥ |
Therefore: 'Perceiving' — namely, dropping out from the *ākiñcanyāyatana*,
sbh4.2:9.2
ākiñcanyasaṁjñāyāmaudārikasaṁjñī ādīnavasaṁjñī ākiñcanyāyatanasaṁjñāṁ vyāvartayati |
gross-perceiving, fault-perceiving the perception-of-nothingness, he turns away the *ākiñcanyāyatana*-perception.
sbh4.2:9.3
tena pūrvamākiñcanyāyatanasamāpattikāle akiñcanasaṁjñā samatikrāntā, etarhyakiñcanasaṁjñā samatikrāntā bhavati |
By that previous *ākiñcanyāyatana*-attainment-time, the perception-of-nothingness was transcended; now the *non*-perception-of-nothingness has come to be transcended.
sbh4.2:9.4
tenāha saṁjñī yaduta kiñcanasaṁjñāyā vā, akiñcanasaṁjñāyā vā, akiñcanasaṁjñā vā, na ca punaḥ sarveṇa sarvaṁ sāsya saṁjñā niruddhā bhavati |
Therefore: 'Perceiving — namely, perception-of-nothingness or non-perception-of-nothingness or non-perception-of-nothingness.' Not, however, that all and every perception of his is ceased.
sbh4.2:9.5
tadyathā āsaṁjñike vā, nirodhasamāpattau vā, nānyatra sūkṣmā sā saṁjñā nimittālambane pravartate |
As in the non-perception-attainment or the cessation-attainment — but here, a subtle perception proceeds, with sign-as-object.
Naiva-saṁjñā-nāsaṁjñāyatana — perception is not ceased (that would be one of the non-conscious attainments below). It is reduced to sūkṣmā saṁjñā nimittālambane pravartate — a subtle perception proceeding with sign-as-object. There is barely-perception. Hence 'neither perception nor non-perception.' This is the structural endpoint of the laukika path (bhavāgra).
sbh4.2:9.6
naiva saṁjñā nāsaṁjñā |
'Neither perception nor non-perception.'
sbh4.2:10.1
tatra dve acittike samāpattī asaṁjñāsamāpattirnirodhasamāpattiśca |
There are two non-conscious attainments: the non-perception attainment and the cessation attainment.
sbh4.2:10.2
tatrāsaṁjñāsamāpattiṁ saṁjñāvimukhena manaskāreṇa pṛthagjana eva samāpadyate, nirodhasamāpattiṁ punarārya eva |
The non-perception attainment is entered by an ordinary person using perception-turned-away attention; the cessation attainment, however, is entered only by a noble one.
Two attainments in which consciousness itself ceases. Asaṁjñā-samāpatti is reachable by ordinary persons (pṛthag-jana) — serious meditators without supramundane attainment. Nirodha-samāpatti requires ākiñcanya-vairāgya plus stream-entry-or-higher status — an attainment of the noble path, not just of meditative skill. The latter is the closest mundane experience of nirvāṇa.
sbh4.2:10.3
tatra dvābhyāṁ manaskārābhyāmanayoḥ samāpattyoḥ samāpattipraveśo bhavati |
By two attentions, entry into these two attainments occurs:
sbh4.2:10.4
tadyathā saṁjñāvimukhena manaskāreṇāsaṁjñāsamāpatteḥ, naivasaṁjñānāsaṁjñā ucchalitenālambanasannirūddhena ca manaskāreṇa nirodhasamāpatteḥ |
namely, into the non-perception attainment by perception-turned-away attention; into the cessation attainment by attention dropping-out from *naivasaṁjñā-nāsaṁjñā* and resolving-on-cessation-of-object.
sbh4.2:11.1
tatra dhyānasanniśrayeṇa pañcānāmabhijñānāmabhinirhāro bhavati |
By the basis of *dhyāna*, the projection of the five higher knowledges occurs.
sbh4.2:11.2
kathaṁ ca punarbhavati |
How does it occur?
sbh4.2:11.3
yathāpi taddhyāyī lābhī bhavati |
Just as the contemplator becomes an attainer
sbh4.2:11.4
pariśuddhasya dhyānasya |
of pure *dhyāna*.
sbh4.2:11.5
sa tatpariśuddhaṁ dhyānaṁ niśritya yo anenābhijñādhipateyo dharmaḥ śruto bhavatyudgṛhītaḥ, paryavāptaḥ, yaduta ṛddhiviṣayamārabhya, pūrvenivāsadivyaśrotracyutyupapādacetaḥparyāyaṁ vā tameva manasi kurvan samāhitabhūmikena manaskāreṇārthapratisaṁvedī ca bhavati |
Relying on that pure *dhyāna*: whatever dharma he has heard, grasped, fully obtained — bearing on the higher-knowledge-predominant, namely the range-of-supernormal-power, recollection-of-past-abodes, divine-ear, divine-eye, knowledge-of-others'-minds — that very dharma he attends to with concentrated-plane-attention. He becomes meaning-knower
The five higher knowledges: range of supernormal powers, divine ear, knowing minds of others, recollection of past lives, divine eye (passing-and-arising of beings). A sixth, āsrava-kṣaya (destruction of outflows), is supramundane and not part of this laukika-path treatment. These are far outside the working territory of contemporary lay practice.
sbh4.2:11.6
dharmapratisaṁvedī ca |
and dharma-knower.
sbh4.2:11.7
tasyārthapratisaṁvedino dharmaḥ pratisaṁvedinastathā tathā cittānyabhisaṁskurvato bahulīkārānvayād bhavati |
Of that meaning-knowing, dharma-knowing practitioner, making the mind so-and-so, by the practice of frequent-cultivation —
sbh4.2:11.8
sa kālo bhavati samayo yadasya bhāvanāphalāḥ pañcābhijñā utpadyante |
the time, the moment, becomes when his fruits-of-projection, the five higher knowledges, arise.
