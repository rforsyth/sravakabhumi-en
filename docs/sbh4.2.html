Higher Jhāna and Formless Transitions

Śrāvakabhūmi · Sanskrit (Shukla 1973) ↔ English (1 rendering)
← All topicsView English only →

The seven manaskāras of sbh4.1 generalized through the higher transitions, governed by the audārika-lakṣaṇa (gross-characteristic) pattern: each higher attainment is reached by recognizing the grossness of the lower and the peace of the higher. Seven transitions: 1st→2nd, 2nd→3rd, 3rd→4th jhāna; 4th jhāna→ākāśānantyāyatana, ākāśa→vijñāna, vijñāna→ākiñcanya, ākiñcanya→naivasaṁjñā-nāsaṁjñāyatana. Plus the two non-conscious attainments (asaṁjñā- and nirodha-samāpatti) and the five abhijñā (super-knowledges) with their twelve perceptions.

The gross-characteristic in all these lower planes — up through the ākiñcanyāyatana — is, in summary, twofold:
sbh4.2:1.2

the greater-painful, non-calm abiding-quality [vihāritā] of the lower-and-lower planes,
sbh4.2:1.3
alpāyuṣkatarā ca |
and the lesser-life-span-quality [alpāyuṣkatarā].
Twofold criteria of grossness at every level: (1) the abiding-quality (vihāritā) of lower planes — both more painful AND less calm-abiding (one criterion with two aspects), and (2) the lesser life-span (alpāyuṣkatarā) — beings reborn in higher planes live longer cosmologically. The Sanskrit dvividha framing at 1.1 and 1.4 confirms the twofold reading; the two qualifiers in 1.2 both modify vihāritā, not list two separate criteria. For meditative purposes, the life-span criterion connects felt brevity-of-states at lower planes (states keep dropping away) with felt persistence-of-states at higher planes.

The English renders the three-epithet sequence; the Sanskrit JSON preserves the MS reading per the emendation policy.

The fourth-dhyāna formula is being explained retrospectively. The local quote at 5.7 appears to omit duḥkha, but the summation at 5.11 gives the full stock sequence: abandoning pleasure and pain, with prior disappearance of joy and dejection. Asanga explicitly lists breath (āśvāsa-praśvāsa) among what is abandoned at the fourth jhāna. The breath itself ceases. A structural marker: if the breath has not become very subtle to the point of apparent cessation, fourth jhāna proper has not been entered.
