The Seven Attentions

Śrāvakabhūmi · English working translation
← All topicsView side-by-side ↔

The canonical sapta-manaskāra (seven attentions) framework for the desire-realm-to-first-jhāna transition. The seven: lakṣaṇa-pratisaṁvedī (recognizing the characteristic), ādhimokṣika (resolved), prāvivekya (giving rise to seclusion), rati-saṁgrāhaka (gathering joy), mīmāṁsā (investigative), prayoga-niṣṭha (culminating practice), prayoga-niṣṭhā-phala (the fruit of culminating practice). Two-paths framing (laukika / lokottara) distinguishes the worldly mundane-path traveler from the supramundane.

Of the practitioner who has obtained attention, who has thus entered into restricted abandonment-and-delight, two paths upward become available — none other. What two? Namely: the mundane and the supramundane. This beginning practitioner, with-attention, thinks: 'Shall I go by the mundane path, or by the supramundane?' — and cultivates that very attention. Inasmuch as he cultivates, the *praśrabdhi* and one-pointedness-of-mind, by the passing of those days and nights, go to expansion, growth, abundance. When his attention has become stable in view, harsh, and the object-resolve proceeds relaxed, proceeds thus, and the *śamatha-vipaśyanā* signs have been grasped — then, wishing to go by the mundane path, he begins the practice there; or by the supramundane path.

How many persons, in this very life, go by the mundane path alone, not by the supramundane? It is said: Four. Namely: the wholly outsider; even within the dharma, the dull-faculty practitioner; one previously practicing only *śamatha* who is, moreover, of unripe wholesome roots; a bodhisattva wishing to attain *bodhi* in the future, not in this very life. These four persons go by the mundane-path-vehicle in this very life.

The mundane-path travel itself is twofold: that of the completely-bound ordinary persons, and that of the partially-bound, the trainees. What is it? Seeing the grossness of sense-pleasures and the defilement-edge of the first *dhyāna* attainment-and-rebirth — that is the going-to-dispassion-toward-sense-pleasure. Up through — dispassion toward the *ākiñcanyāyatana* is to be known thus. Likewise the unconscious attainment. Supported by the *dhyāna* attainment, there is the projection of five of the six consciousnesses.

The practitioner engaged in dispassion toward sense-pleasure attains *kāma-vairāgya* through seven attentions. He attains *kāma-vairāgya*. What seven attentions? It is said: The characteristic-knowing, the resolve-born, the seclusion, the delight-gathering; the examination attention; the practice-culminating, and the fruit-of-practice-culmination.

What is the characteristic-knowing attention? It is said: The attention by which the practitioner comprehends the gross characteristic of sense-pleasures, and the calm characteristic of the first *dhyāna*. How does he comprehend the gross characteristic? It is said: Investigating the six investigation-subjects of sense-pleasures: meaning, specific characteristic, general characteristic, side, time, and reasoning.

Investigating the gross meaning: 'These sense-pleasures are with-fault, with-many-troubles, with-many-afflictions, with-many-disturbances.' The much-fault-character of these sense-pleasures, up to much-disturbance-character — this is the gross meaning.

Investigating substance: 'There exists, inwardly, sense-desire toward sense-pleasures.'

Investigating the specific characteristic: 'These are the defilement-sense-pleasures (*kleśa-kāma*); these are the substance-sense-pleasures (*vastu-kāma*). They are in turn happiness-resting, suffering-resting, or neither-suffering-nor-happiness-resting. The happiness-resting are bound to *kāma-rāga*, bound to perception-and-mind-perversion. The suffering-resting are bound to hatred, anger, resentment. The neither-suffering-nor-happiness-resting are bound to concealment, malice, deception, fraud, shamelessness, lack of remorse, and view-perversion. Thus these sense-pleasures are followed by tainted feelings and by their counter-defilements.' Thus he investigates the specific characteristic of sense-pleasures.

How does he investigate the general characteristic? 'All these sense-pleasures, together with the suffering-of-birth, the suffering-of-aging, up through the suffering-of-thwarted-desire, are equally bound and bound-together. Even those sense-pleasure-enjoyers who proceed in great sense-pleasure-abundance — they too are not free from the *dharmatā* of birth-and-the-rest. Their abundance is only momentary.' Thus he investigates the general characteristic.

How does he investigate the side? 'These sense-pleasures fall on the dark side. They are like a skeleton, like a piece of meat, like a grass-torch; like a coal-pit; like a venomous snake; like a dream; like borrowed ornaments; like fruit on a tree. Even beings seeking sense-pleasures experience the suffering of the seeking. They experience the suffering of guarding, of loss-of-affection, of dissatisfaction, of non-autonomy, of misconduct. So also for one indulging in sense-pleasures. Five faults have been spoken of by the Blessed One: 'Sense-pleasures give little gratification, much suffering, much fault.' 'To one indulging in sense-pleasures, there is no measure of satisfaction or sufficiency. By this enumeration, sense-pleasures are reproached by the Buddhas, by the Buddha-disciples, by the well-gone ones, by the noble persons.' 'To one indulging in sense-pleasures, the fetters go to accumulation.' 'There is no evil, unwholesome action that I say is not-to-be-done [by such a one].' 'These sense-pleasures are dissatisfaction-makers, common, causes of unrighteous and unequal conduct; increasers of sense-craving, to be avoided by the noble, swift-going to sense-objects, dependent on conditions, places of heedlessness, hollow, impermanent, vain, falsely-pretending, illusion-like, child-prattle.' Whether sense-pleasures of this very life, or of the next life, whether divine or human — beginning with Māra, this is Māra's range, this is Māra's pasture, where these manifold evil, unwholesome mental dharmas come about: namely covetousness, ill-will, agitation — or whatever else is obstructive [to one] for the noble disciple training. By many enumerations these sense-pleasures fall on the dark side. By this much, in this manner, he investigates the side.

'Across past, future, and present times — these sense-pleasures are impermanent in every time, unstable in every time. Thus they are with-many-troubles, with-many-afflictions, with-many-faults.' Thus he investigates time.

How does he investigate reasoning? 'With great endeavor, with great seeking, with great labor, by various assorted craftsmanship-and-occupations, sense-pleasures are gathered. They are generated, accumulated. Once gathered well and generated well, they go — up through the external possession-substances: mother, father, son, wife, female slave, male slave, worker, servant, friend, minister, kinsman, blood-relation. Or, taken as the inward body of form, gross, made of the four great elements, accumulated by gruel and barley — by the dharma of constant smearing, washing, rubbing, breaking, cutting, scattering, destroying — they tend to the counter-action of just the suffering arising-and-arising. Food is the counter-action of the suffering of hunger. Clothing is the counter-action of the suffering of cold and heat, and for covering up shame. Bedding is the counter-action of the suffering of sleep-fatigue. Walking-and-standing is the counter-action of the suffering of posture. And for the counter-action of the suffering of illness, medicine for the sick — these sense-pleasures are pure counter-actions to suffering.' 'These are not to be enjoyed with attachment, nor with clinging — except by one ill, weakened, suffering, taking medicine simply for the appeasement of illness. So this is trustworthy testimony.' 'So these sense-pleasures are gross thus and thus.' 'Direct knowing-and-seeing has also arisen in me.' 'There is also this inferential rule.' It is also the nature of sense-pleasures — beginningless, established-dharma, unthinkable-dharma. It is not to be thought. It is not to be discriminated. Thus he investigates reasoning.

Having thus comprehended the gross-characteristic of sense-pleasures by the six investigation-subjects, he comprehends the calm-characteristic in the first *dhyāna*: 'There is not at all this gross-character in the first *dhyāna* that there is in the desire-realm.' By this, he comprehends the gross-characteristic of the desire-realm and the calm-characteristic of the first *dhyāna*. This is called the characteristic-knowing attention. This attention is to be known as mixed with hearing-and-thinking.

Having thus fully understood sense-pleasures and properly investigated the first *dhyāna* by the gross-and-calm-characteristic, he transcends hearing and thinking, and resolves purely by the cultivation-aspect alone. He cultivates *śamatha-vipaśyanā* with that very sign as object. As, by cultivation, he resolves on that gross-and-calm character again and again — thus arises the resolve-born attention.

Through familiarization, cultivation, and frequent practice of that, for the first time the path arises for the abandoning of defilements. When the path for the abandoning of defilements has arisen — that very arising of the Blessed Lord's attention is called the seclusion attention.

For the first time, by abandoning the desire-realm defilements and other things to be abandoned, and by the dissipation of the corresponding subtle obstructions — beyond that, he becomes one delighting-in-abandonment, delighting-in-seclusion. There, seeing the benefit of abandonment, touched by limited seclusion-born rapture-and-happiness, from time to time he gladdens the mind by inspiring attention, rouses it. By uplifting attention he arouses it. All for the dissipation of sloth-and-torpor and agitation. This is called the delight-gathering attention.

Of him thus delighting-in-abandonment, delighting-in-cultivation, properly engaged — with the establishment-of-the-wholesome-side, the defilement-and-action-obsession bound to sense-pleasures does not occur, whether walking or sitting. It occurs to him: 'Am I comprehending the existing sense-desire toward sense-pleasures, or am I only wishing to examine whether it persists as non-existent?' He attends to one or another inspiring fair sign. Because of the non-abandoned-ness of all the latent tendency — even attending to that sign, his mind goes to inclination-toward-indulgence, propensity-toward-indulgence, leaning-toward-indulgence. There is no genuine recoil or counter-going. It occurs to him: 'My mind is not properly dispassionate, not liberated from sense-pleasures. My mind is restrained by formations like water held by a dam, restrained by *dharmatā*. Let me, by greater measure, abide more delighting-in-abandonment for the complete abandoning of that latent tendency.' [Delighting-in-]cultivation. This is called the examination attention.

By a greater measure, he abides delighting-in-abandonment, delighting-in-cultivation, joined with *śamatha-vipaśyanā*. He examines his progress again and again. Of him cultivating the antidote, time after time examining the abandoned-and-not-abandoned, the mind disengages from all desire-realm defilements — by time-bound yoga. But the seed is not yet completely uprooted. At that time, having reached the limit of preliminary-*dhyāna*-practice path, the antidote to all defilements arises. This is called the practice-culminating attention.

And, conditioned on that very attention [the practice-culminating attention] as the cause, he attains the first *dhyāna*. The attention conjoined with the basic-first-*dhyāna* is called the fruit-of-practice-culmination attention.

Abiding in the seclusion-attention and the delight-gathering-attention, by the seclusion-born rapture-and-happiness, he saturates the body. Sometimes by some particular conditions, by the manifesting of a thinned aspect, in the time of the practice-culminating attention there is non-delight. Sometimes again, by the manifesting of greater *dhyāna*-amplitude, in the fruit-of-practice-culmination attention proceeding, nothing of his arises as a problem. The rememberable from every body — namely, by the seclusion-born rapture-and-happiness — at that time, with sense-pleasures secluded, with evil unwholesome dharmas secluded, with thought, with examination, with seclusion-born rapture-and-happiness, the first *dhyāna* with five factors — having attained, he abides. Having stood in the fruit of cultivating-the-counter-action-to-the-desire-realm, he attains *kāma-vairāgya*.

By the characteristic-knowing attention, he properly knows what is to be abandoned, and his mind sets the determination toward the abandoning of what is to be abandoned and the attaining of what is to be attained. By the resolve-born attention, he properly begins the practice for abandoning and attaining. By the seclusion attention, he abandons the strong-knowing-and-calming defilements. By the delight-gathering attention, he abandons the middling-grade defilement-modes. By the examination attention, he establishes the mind in non-pride-of-attainment. By the practice-culminating attention, he abandons the mild defilement-modes. By the fruit-of-practice-culmination attention, of these defilement-modes that have been cultivated and well-cultivated, he experiences the cultivation-fruit.

Furthermore: the characteristic-knowing attention and the resolve-born attention — this is called the conforming attention, also conjoined with the criticism-counter-action. The seclusion attention and the practice-culminating attention — this is the counter-action attention, conjoined with the abandonment-counter-action. The delight-gathering attention is both counter-action and inspiring. The examination attention is the review attention. Thus it is said. In this manner, of the seven attentions, four functional attentions are to be known as outstanding: namely, conforming, counter-action, inspiring, and review.

As is the first-*dhyāna* attainment with the seven attentions, so the second, third, fourth *dhyāna* attainments, and the *ākāśa-vijñāna-ākiñcanya-naivasaṁjñā-nāsaṁjñā-āyatana* attainments — with the seven attentions.

Among them, by which attention he comprehends the gross-characteristic in *vitarka*s, and the calm-characteristic in the without-thought second *dhyāna* — that is the characteristic-knowing attention for second-*dhyāna* attainment. There [in the first jhāna] the *dhyāna*-attainer — the first-*dhyāna*-obtainer sees grossness in *vitarka*s. That verbal mentation, pertaining to the concentrated plane, is also of grave-object-nature. By its first-falling-onto-the-object character, it is gross mental-talking. Following on that, subtle mental-talking proceeds with distracting-action — these are *vitarka* and *vicāra*. These *vitarka-vicāra*s are mental-factor-experiences arising in the mind, co-arising, conjoined, single-object-functioning. Thus they arise inwardly. Not at all does this gross-characteristic exist in the without-*vitarka* second *dhyāna*. Therefore the second is calm.
