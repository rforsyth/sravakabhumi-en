Avatārabhūmi: The Stage of Descent

Śrāvakabhūmi · Sanskrit (Shukla 1973) ↔ English (1 rendering)
← All topicsView English only →

The second sub-bhūmi of Yogasthāna I. Avatārabhūmi describes the descent (avatāra) into the path — the transition from being merely fit (the gotrabhūmi end-state) into actively practicing. Six person-types map the journey from latent fitness to arhatship; eight marks identify what a descended practitioner looks like in life.

II. The Stage of Descent
The avatārabhūmi opens by enumerating the six person-types it will define: bhavya (merely fit), the three grades of wholesome roots (mild, middle, strong), niṣṭhā-prāyogika (applied to the ultimate), and niṣṭhā-gata (arrived at the ultimate). Sections 1.4–1.27 define each in turn.
sbh1.2:1.1
yaiḥ ṣaḍbhirbhavyo mṛdukuśalamūlasamanvāgato madhyakuśalamūlasamanvāgataḥ |
By these six [person-types]: the merely fit person, one endowed with mild wholesome roots, one endowed with middle wholesome roots,
The opening enumeration is six person-types/stages, not six independent factors: merely fit, mild/middle/strong wholesome roots, applied to the ultimate, and arrived at the ultimate. The wording is kept aligned with the structural overview and the definitions that follow.
sbh1.2:1.2

endowed with strong wholesome roots,
sbh1.2:1.3

one applied to the ultimate, and one arrived at the ultimate.

He who, on the basis of being established in the lineage, first of all gains faith in the dharma-vinaya proclaimed by the Tathāgata,

He who, established in the lineage, first gains faith in the dharma-vinaya proclaimed by the Tathāgata,
sbh1.2:1.9

yāvat dṛṣṭi-ṛjū-kṛtā — the Sanskrit yāvat signals 'as elaborated above, up to the point of ...'. Standard Buddhist-text shorthand: the full description from the bhavya entry above is implied, repeated in summary by the trailing milestone (here, 'making the view upright').

sbh1.2:1.17

What is the person applied to the ultimate?
sbh1.2:1.18
yaḥ pudgalaścaramamātmabhāvaṁ pratilabhyāsravakṣayāya samyagavavādānuśāsanīṁ, saddharmaśravaṇaṁ vā pratilabhya samyageva prayujyate |
He who, having attained [his] final individual existence, having received the right admonition-and-instruction for the destruction of the fluxes — or having heard the true dharma — rightly applies himself,
ātmabhāva-pratilambha — 'attainment of [the final] individual existence.' Refers to the body-and-mind continuum that becomes the substrate for the path's culmination. The 'final' here means carama- (last in the saṁsāric series for this person), not chronologically late in life.
sbh1.2:1.19
na cādhmāyi yī sarveṇa sarvaṁ sarvathā pratipadyate |
and, no longer wavering, in every way and altogether practices,
sbh1.2:1.20
āsravakṣayamanuprāpnoti |
[Damaged/Conjectural]: but does not attain the destruction of the fluxes —
The root text as extracted prints āsrava-kṣayam anuprāpnoti ('attains the destruction of the fluxes'), but that would make the niṣṭhā-prāyogika an arhat and collapse the contrast with the immediately following niṣṭhā-gata. The recapitulation at 2.9–2.13 explicitly says this person realizes stream-entry, once-returning, or non-returning but not arhatship. The English therefore restores the required negative sense and marks the line [Damaged/Conjectural]; Sanskrit JSON is left unchanged.
sbh1.2:1.21

and does not yet arrive at the ultimate. This is called the person applied to the ultimate.
sbh1.2:1.22

sbh1.2:1.23

He who, rightly impelled, rightly instructed —
sbh1.2:1.24

namely for the destruction of the fluxes — practices accordingly,
sbh1.2:1.25

sbh1.2:1.26
kṛtakṛtyo bhavati paramaśītībhāvaprāptaḥ |
He has done his task, attained supreme calming.
paramaśītī-bhāva-prāpta — literally 'attained the supreme state of becoming-cool.' Coolness (śīta) is the standard Buddhist metaphor for nirvāṇa, contrasting with the burning of the kleśas. Rendered 'supreme calming' for English readability; 'supreme cooling' or 'highest peace' would also work.
sbh1.2:1.27

This is called the person arrived at the ultimate.

sbh1.2:4.1
tatrāvatīrṇānāṁ pudgalānāṁ kiṁ parimāṇaniyatastulyaśca sarveṣāṁ kālo bhavati |
For persons who have descended, is the time required by all of them — for final nirvāṇa — of fixed measure and equal across all?
Restructured as a single question across 4.1–4.3 — the source uses three short |-separated clauses (asking about fixed measure, equal duration, and certainty respectively) that together form one question whose answer is given in 4.4. Punctuation here makes that visible.

sbh1.2:4.14
avatīrṇasya pudgalasya katamāni liṁgāni |
What are the marks of a descended person?
sbh1.2:4.15
iha gotrasthaḥ pudgalaḥ avatīrṇamātra eva yadā janmāntaraparivarttenāpi smṛtisaṁpramoṣaṁ pratilabhate |
Here, a person established in the lineage who has just descended — even when, through the transition to another birth, he experiences loss of memory;
Sections 4.15–4.25 describe the descended person's distinctive first mark through a two-part contrast: 4.16–4.19 — rejection of an ill-stated dharma even when praised; 4.20 — simile for the un-descended state, where one cannot connect with even the well-stated dharma (like a honey-creature thrown into vinegar); 4.21–4.24 — the descended person's positive embrace of the well-stated dharma (like a honey-creature in honey). The contrast is the mark — what distinguishes the descended from the un-descended is precisely this changed orientation toward dharma.
sbh1.2:4.16
āśāstari dharmavinaye vā sati samvidyamāne a pi durākhyāte dharmavinaye svākhyāte a pyanekaparyāyeṇa durākhyātasya dharmavinayasya varṇaṁ stutimānuśansaṁ śaṁsaṁ śrutvā nāvatarati |
and even when, with a Teacher and the [true] dharma-vinaya present and available, an ill-stated dharma-vinaya is praised in many ways — hearing the praise, eulogy, and commendation of that ill-stated dharma-vinaya rather than the well-stated one, he does not descend [into it];
sbh1.2:4.17
na pravrajati |
he does not go forth [in it];
sbh1.2:4.18
pravrajito a pyavatīrṇo laghu laghveva pratyudāvarttakhe |
and even if he has gone forth and descended [there], he turns back lightly, very lightly;
sbh1.2:4.19
prakṛtyaiva cāsya tatrārocakaḥ saṁtiṣṭhate |
and by nature distaste for it remains in him.
sbh1.2:4.20
madhuni jātasyeva ca prāṇakasya śuktiprakṣiptasya kāmopabhogino vā karde nde ṇa, syandanikāyāmvā prakṣiptasya yathāpi tatpūrvakeṇaiva hetubalādhānena svākhyātasya vā punardharmavinayasya naiva varṇa ṁ stutimānuśansaṁ śaṁsaṁ śrṛṇoti vā kaṇḍati vā |
Just as a creature born in honey, when thrown into vinegar — or a sensualist when cast into mire or a foul drain [cannot thrive there] — just so, by the prior force of conditioning, [the un-descended person] neither hears the praise, eulogy, and commendation of the well-stated dharma-vinaya, nor longs for it.
svākhyātasya … dharmavinayasya — 'of the well-stated dharma-vinaya.' Counterintuitive in context (the descended person is supposed to embrace the well-stated), but coherent if read as describing the un-descended state to which the simile compares: the un-descended person, like a honey-creature thrown into vinegar, cannot survive in the well-stated dharma until descent has occurred. 4.21 then turns to the post-descent positive case.
sbh1.2:4.21

sbh1.2:4.22

sbh1.2:4.23

and by nature delight in it remains in him —
sbh1.2:4.24

like a honey-creature in honey, or a sensualist in refined sense-pleasure,
sbh1.2:4.25

by the prior force of conditioning.
sbh1.2:4.26

Endowed with which marks, he is reckoned ‘descended.’
sbh1.2:13.5
api punaretāni gotrasthānāmavatīrṇānāñca pudgalānāṁ ānumānikāni liṅgāni veditavyāni |
Moreover, these are the inferential marks of those established in the lineage and of descended persons, to be understood.
sbh1.2:13.6
buddhā eva tu bhagavantaḥ paramapārami tā prāptāśca śrāvakāstāyinaḥ |
Only the blessed Buddhas, who have attained the supreme perfection, and the disciples who are protectors,
sbh1.2:13.7
tatra pratyakṣadarśinaḥ |
are direct seers there.
sbh1.2:13.8
suviśuddhena jñānadarśanena pratyanubhavanti |
By perfectly purified knowing-and-vision they experience them directly.
sbh1.2:13.9
yaduta gotraṁ cāvatārañca |
Namely, lineage and descent.
sbh1.2:14.1
avatīrṇāḥ pudgalāḥ katame |
What are descended persons?
sbh1.2:14.2
astyavatīrṇaḥ pudgalaḥ |
There is a descended person.
sbh1.2:14.3
avatīrṇa eva, na paripacyamāno, na paripakvo, na niṣkrāntaḥ |
Only descended, not yet maturing, not matured, not yet gone out.
sbh1.2:15.1
asti paripakvo na niṣkrāntaḥ |
There is the matured but not gone out.
sbh1.2:15.2
asti niṣkrānto na paripakva eṣāṁ ca pūrvavadvibhāgo veditavyaḥ |
There is the gone-out but not matured. Their division is to be understood as before.
sbh1.2:16.1
ye a pi tadanye mṛdvindriyā dayaḥ pudgalāḥ |
Those other [person-categories] — the persons of mild faculty and so on,
sbh1.2:16.2
gotrabhūmau nirdiṣṭāḥ |
set forth in the gotrabhūmi —
sbh1.2:16.3
teṣāmihāpi yathāyogaṁ vibhāgo veditavyaḥ |
their division here too is to be understood accordingly.
sbh1.2:16.4
tatra yaścāyamavatārasya svabhāvaḥ, yacca vyavasthānaṁ, yāni cemānyavatīrṇānāṁ liṅgāni |
The own-nature of descent, its delineation, the marks of the descended,
sbh1.2:16.5
ye ceme avatīrṇāṁ pudgalāḥ tatsarvamabhisaṁkṣipyāvatārabhūmirityucyate |
and who the descended persons are — all this gathered into one is called the avatārabhūmi.
sbh1.2:17.1
uddānam |
Summary verse.
sbh1.2:18.1
svabhāvastad vyavasthānaṁ liṅgaṁ pudgala eva ca |
Own-nature, the delineation, marks, and the person itself,
sbh1.2:18.2
avatārabhūmivijñeyā sarvametat samāsataḥ |
the stage of descent is to be known — all this in brief.
sbh1.2:19.1
śrāvakabhūmā va vatārabhūmiḥ samāptā |
In the Śrāvakabhūmi, the stage of descent is complete.
