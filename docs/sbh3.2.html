The Vipaśyanā Method

Śrāvakabhūmi · Sanskrit (Shukla 1973) ↔ AI-assisted English
← All topicsView English only →

Vipaśyanā is fourfold (1.3–1.6): the monk investigates, investigates closely, deliberates, and engages in thorough examination. ¶3–6 give it three gateways (following the sign alone, seeking, reviewing) and the six investigation-subjects as its object: meaning, entity, characteristic, side, time, and reasoning, the last by four reasonings (5.25–5.38). ¶7–28 run the six investigation-subjects through the five conduct-purifying objects; ¶29–39 treat the kinds of engagement, purification from obstacles, and the yoking of śamatha and vipaśyanā; ¶41–56 the cultivation of attention, its four kinds (41.3–41.4) and then the beginner's instruction: five signs to take up (46.4–46.5), worked through for aśubhā (46.6–54.1), maitrī (54.2–54.18), dependent origination (55.1–55.8), and the elements (55.9–55.16). The breath-mindfulness instruction is lost in the lacuna after 55.16.
