The Vipaśyanā Method

Śrāvakabhūmi · Sanskrit (Shukla 1973) ↔ English (1 rendering)
← All topicsView English only →

The fourfold vipaśyanā (insight) method — vicinoti (investigates), pravicinoti (investigates closely), parivitarkayati (deliberates), parimīmāṁsām āpadyate (engages in thorough examination). The chapter unfolds the framework through three gateways: sign-only, seeking, and reviewing; six investigation-subjects: meaning, substance, characteristic, side, time, and reasoning; and four reasonings: dependence, cause-and-effect, proof-establishing, and nature-of-things.

Vicinoti → pravicinoti → parivitarkayati → parimīmāṁsām āpadyate — the same four prajñā-function verbs that appear throughout the Śrāvakabhūmi. The progression moves from coarse to fine inquiry: general examination → close discrimination → deliberation → thorough critical examination. The accumulation conveys that vipaśyanā is sustained, multi-aspected investigation rather than a single act of looking.

What, then, is purification from obstacles (āvaraṇa-viśuddhi)?
Āvaraṇa-viśuddhi — purification from the four obstacles. The Yogācāra fourfold obstacle-typology (paritamanā, nivaraṇa, vitarka, ātmasaṁpragraha) maps to: dispiritedness with practice, the five hindrances, defiled discursive thought, and self-conceit-from-attainment. What, then, is manaskāra-bhāvanā (cultivation of attention)?
The four-fold manaskāra framework — cittasaṁtāpana (mind-heating), cittābhiṣyandana (mind-suffusing), praśrabdhi-janaka (serenity-producing), and jñāna-darśana-viśodhana (knowledge-and-vision-purifying) — is the Yogācāra operational protocol for getting an ādikarmika (beginner) into one-pointedness and delight-in-abandonment. Each manaskāra targets a specific phase: heating produces saṁvega; suffusing produces praharṣa; the serenity-producing transitions into śamatha; the knowledge-purifying transitions into vipaśyanā. The same fourfold reappears in sbh4.1 as the framework underlying the seven attentions.

Answer: the beginner, the one engaged for the first time in this work, having had the pervasive characteristic established for him with respect to one-pointedness and the purification from obstacles, avoids wrong engagement and trains in right engagement.
sbh3.2:41.3

What [is the description] when, from time to time, he stirs up the mind with respect to stirring-up-worthy dharmas? Thus his mind becomes heated — well-heated, agitated, well-agitated — namely, with respect to the outflow-stationed dharmas.
sbh3.2:42.2
sarvasaṁvejanīyāni sthānāni katamāni |
What are all the stirring-up-worthy places?
sbh3.2:42.3
āha | catvāri | tadyathā ātmavipattiḥ, paravipattiśca — vartamāne samavahite saṁmukhībhūte yoniśo manasikārānvayāt saṁvejanīyaṁ sthānaṁ bhavati |
Answer: four. Namely, ātma-vipatti (one's own misfortune) and para-vipatti (the misfortune of others) — in the present, when at hand, when face-to-face, [taken up] in consequence of wise attention, [each] becomes a stirring-up-worthy place.
The four saṁvejanīya-sthāna — places that generate urgency — pair across two axes: one's own / others', and misfortune at hand (present) / fortune past (gone). The wise-attention to misfortune face-to-face heats the mind; the wise-attention to past-fortune-now-perished also heats it (through impermanence). This is one of the tightest Yogācāra schemas for the practical generation of saṁvega.

Thus he generates joy — by relying on the basis of self-conception-of-distinctive-attainment, he gladdens the mind.

Heating the mind with respect to stirring-up-worthy dharmas, he turns the mind away from outflow-stationed dharmas, makes it inverse [to them], establishes it facing-away, separates it.

Gladdening, suffusing the mind with respect to gladdening-worthy dharmas, he turns the mind, with affection, toward dharmas born of renunciation and seclusion; he attaches [the mind] to them, delights [the mind] in them, conjoins [the mind] with them.
sbh3.2:44.5

Thus, by these two dharmas, his mind proceeds — turned away from all dark-side, turned toward all bright-side — namely, by saṁvega (urgency) and praharṣa (gladness).
sbh3.2:44.6

Since the mind, thus turned away from the dark side by the mind-heating attention and turned toward the bright side by the mind-suffusing attention, from time to time is inwardly applied — namely, by cetaḥ-śamatha with the serenity-producing attention —
sbh3.2:44.7

from time to time he discerns dharmas, examines, ponders, investigates — by the knowledge-and-vision-purifying attention.
sbh3.2:44.8
evam asya taccittaṁ kālena kālaṁ śamathavipaśyanāparigṛhītaṁ, sarvākārasarvaguṇahetūpakṛtaṁ, teṣāṁ teṣāṁ rātridivasānām atyayāt, kṣaṇalavamuhūrttānāṁ ca viśeṣāya paraiti |
Thus his mind, from time to time conjoined with śamatha-vipaśyanā, supported by causes of all modes and all qualities, after the passing of those nights and days, of moments, half-moments, hours, advances toward distinction.
sbh3.2:45.1
tadyathā jātarūparajataṁ dakṣeṇa karmāreṇa vā, karmārāntevāsinā vā, kālena kālaṁ yadā saṁtāpitaṁ ca bhavati, vigatamalakaṣāye bhāve, abhiṣyanditaṁ ca bhavati — tatra tatrālaṁkārakarmaṇā mṛdukarmaṇyatāyogenābhimukhīkṛtaṁ bhavati |
Just as raw-gold or silver, by a skilled smith or by a smith's apprentice, when from time to time it is heated, [its] state with stains and dross gone, and suffused — there, by the ornament-making work, made workable by the combined-soft work, [it is] made fit;
The gold-smith simile — one of the more famous Yogācāra meditation similes. The yogī is the smith; the mind is the raw metal; the four manaskāras together produce the workable mental state in which śamatha and vipaśyanā can be 'shaped' to the practitioner's purpose. The simile recurs in the Yogācārabhūmi and the Mahāyāna-sūtrālaṅkāra.
sbh3.2:45.2
tam enaṁ dakṣaḥ karmāro vā karmārāntevāsī vā tadupamena śilpajñānena karmāntavastunā yatreṣṭā alaṁkāravikṛtistatra pariṇamayati —
the skilled smith or the smith's apprentice, with the comparable craft-knowledge, with that material of his craft, transforms it where the desired ornament-variety is required —
sbh3.2:45.3
evam eva yoginā yadā taccittam abhidhyādimalakaṣāye vimukhībhāvenodvejitaṁ ca bhavati, kliṣṭadaurmanasyavimukhībhāvena cābhipramoditaṁ bhavati,
just so, when the yogī's mind has been agitated by being turned away from the stains and dross of covetousness and the rest, and gladdened by being turned away from defiled-dejection,
sbh3.2:45.4
tam enaṁ yogī yatra yatra niyojayati — śamathapakṣe vā vipaśyanāpakṣe vā — tatra tatra sūpaśliṣṭaṁ ca bhavati, sulagnaṁ cāvikalaṁ cāvikampyaṁ ca |
then wherever the yogī directs it — to the śamatha-side or the vipaśyanā-side — there [the mind] becomes well-attached, well-clinging, undivided, unshaking;
sbh3.2:45.5
yathābhipretārthasaṁpattaye ca paraiti |
and it advances toward the perfecting of the intended purpose.
sbh3.2:46.1
tatra katham ādikarmikaḥ tatprathamakarmiko manaskārabhāvanāyāṁ viniyujyate |
There, how is the beginner, the one in his first attempt at this work, instructed in manaskāra-bhāvanā?
sbh3.2:46.2
yathāyaṁ viniyujyamānaḥ pratipadyamānaśca spṛśati tatprathamataḥ prahāṇābhiratiṁ cittasyaikāgratāṁ |
[The way is] that he, being instructed and engaging, first touches prahāṇa-rati (delight in abandonment) and one-pointedness of mind.
sbh3.2:46.3

Here, the yogajña (knower of yoga) initially admonishes the ādikarmika engaged in yoga thus:
sbh3.2:46.4
'ehi tvaṁ, bhadramukha, trīṇi nimittodgrāhakāni kāraṇāni niśritya — yad uta dṛṣṭamvā śrutamvā, cintānumānādhipateyaṁ vā parikalpaṁ — pañca nimittānyudgṛhṇīṣva:
'Come, dear one — relying on the three causes that take up signs (namely, what is seen or heard, or what is governed by thought and inference, [or] imagined), take up five signs:
The five nimitta-grahaṇa sequence: (1) saṁvejanīya (urgency), (2) prasadanīya (gladdening), (3) ādīnava (danger), (4) āloka (luminous), (5) vasturūpaṇa (substance-image). The yogajña (teacher) leads the beginner through each in order. Each nimitta is an internal object the practitioner cultivates to ground a specific manaskāra and prepare for śamatha-vipaśyanā. The lust-conducted student (here) is led specifically into the aśubhā practice; later paragraphs cover the maitrī-, idaṁ-pratyayatā-, dhātu-, and ānāpāna-conducted variants.
sbh3.2:46.5
saṁvejanīyaṁ, prasadanīyam, ādīnavanimittam, ālokanimittaṁ, vasturūpaṇānimittaṁ ca |
the saṁvejanīya (urgency-generating), the prasadanīya (gladdening), the ādīnava (danger), the āloka (luminous), and the vasturūpaṇa (substance-image) signs.
sbh3.2:46.6
sa cetsa yogaprayukta ādikarmiko rāgacarito bhavatyaśubhāvineyaḥ — kathaṁ sa pañcānāṁ nimittānām udgrahaṇāyāvabodhyate |
If that yoga-engaged ādikarmika is of lust-conduct, suited to be led by aśubhā — how is he to be awakened to the taking-up of the five signs?
sbh3.2:46.7
evam avabodhyate — 'ehi tvaṁ, bhadramukha | yaṁ yam eva grāmamvā nigamamvopaniśritya viharasi | sa cedanyatra grāme nigame vānyatamaṁ puruṣamvā striyamvā ābādhikaṁ śṛṇoṣi — duḥkhitaṁ bāḍhaglānaṁ, mṛtamvā kālagataṁ, puruṣamvā striyamvā |
He is to be awakened thus: 'Come, dear one. Whatever village or town you dwell in dependent on: if you hear of someone, man or woman, in another village or town who is afflicted — pained, gravely ill, or dead — man or woman;
sbh3.2:46.8
api tu tasya puruṣasya vā striyā vānyatamānyatamaṁ mitrāmātyajñātisālohitaṁ, paracakrakṛtamvā tadgrāmaparyāpannasya janakāyasya bhojanavyasanaṁ, agnidāhakṛtamvā, udakāpaharaṇakṛtamvā, kuvihitapraviṇāśakṛtamvā, kuprayuktakarmāntapralujyanākṛtamvā, apriyadāyādādhigamakṛtamvā, kulāṅgāra-vipraṇāśakṛtamvā |
or [news] of any of that man's or woman's friends, ministers, relatives, kin — done by an enemy-host, [news of] eating-misfortune of the people of that village, fire-burning, water-carrying-off, [news of] destruction by poor management, ruin from misengaged transactions, [news of] hostile-heir-arrival, fire-destruction-of-the-house;
sbh3.2:46.9
no cecchṛṇoṣi — api tu pratyakṣaṁ paśyasi |
or if you do not hear [of it] but see [it] directly.
sbh3.2:46.10
no vānyasmin grāmanigame, no ca tasminneva grāmanigame — na pareṣām, anya-rairapi tu ātmanaiva spṛṣṭo bhavasi — śārīrikābhirvedanābhirduḥkhābhistīvrābhir' iti vistareṇa pūrvavat |
Either in another village-town, or in this very village-town — [either of others, or, on others' part, even] of yourself — you are touched, by bodily pains, by sharp painful feelings,' in detail as before.
sbh3.2:46.11
'sarvaṁ dṛṣṭvā śrutvā caivaṁ cittaṁ saṁvejaya — duḥkho batāyaṁ saṁsāraḥ, kṛcchra ātmabhāvapratilabdho yatremā evaṁrūpā ātmanaśca pareṣāṁ ca vipattaya upalabhyante |
'Having seen, heard all this, stir up the mind: ‘Painful indeed is this saṁsāra, hard-won this individual-existence-attainment, in which such forms of misfortune — one's own and others' — are met with.
sbh3.2:46.12
yaduta ārogyavipattirapi, jātivipattirapi, bhogavipattirapi, vyādhirvyādhidharmatā ca, maraṇaṁ maraṇadharmatā ca | api caikeṣāṁ śīlavipattirapi, dṛṣṭivipattirapi yato nidānaṁ sattvā dṛṣṭe ca dharme duḥkhavihāriṇo bhavanti | saṁparāye ca durgatigāminaḥ |
Namely: misfortune of health, of birth, of wealth, sickness and the nature-of-sickness; death, the nature-of-death. Furthermore, for some, even misfortune of virtue, of view — by which-cause beings here-and-now become pain-dwellers, and in the future are bound for unfortunate destinies.
sbh3.2:46.13
yāśca saṁpattayo dṛṣṭadharmasukhavihārāya, abhisaṁparāye ca sugatigamanāya — tā apyanityāḥ, tāsām apyanityatā prajñāyate |
And whatever fortunes [there are] for here-and-now happy-dwelling and for fortunate destinies in the future — these too are impermanent; their impermanence is to be discerned.
sbh3.2:46.14
vipattiścet saṁmukhībhūtā, vimukhībhūtā tasminsamaye saṁpattiḥ | asaṁmukhībhūtāyām api vipattau durlabhā saṁpattirvināśadharmiṇī ca |'
If misfortune is face-to-face, fortune at that time is turned-away. Even when misfortune is not face-to-face, fortune is hard to obtain and of perishable nature.’'
sbh3.2:46.15
evaṁ ca punaścittamudvejayitvā sādhu ca suṣṭhu ca yoniśaḥ pradadhatsva —
And thus, having agitated the mind, wisely and well, you should apply [yourself] wisely:
sbh3.2:46.16
'anāśvāsyam etatsthānam aviśvāsyaṁ | yatsaṁsāre me saṁsarataḥ, aparinirvṛtasyāvimuktacetasaḥ — etā vipattisaṁpattayo, na me saṁmukhībhāvaṁ, vimukhībhāvaṁ ca gaccheyuḥ |
'This place is unreliable, untrustworthy. Going round in saṁsāra, not finally-extinguished, with mind not yet liberated — these fortunes-and-misfortunes might not [always] turn-away-from me, or face-me.
sbh3.2:46.17
na vā ato nidānaṁ me duḥkham utpadyate — tīvraṁ, kharaṁ, kaṭukam, anālāpyam, alabhyam etatsthānaṁ — tasmādetatsarvārtham adhipatiṁ kṛtvā prahāṇaratiratena me bhavitavyam apramattena |
Or from this cause, suffering does not [necessarily] arise for me — sharp, harsh, bitter, indescribable, unobtainable [is] this place. Therefore making all this adhipati, I must be one delighting in abandonment, heedful.
sbh3.2:46.18
evaṁ bahulavihāriṇo me apyevāsyānarthasyākriyā syād' iti yoniśaḥ pradadhatsva — evaṁ tvaṁ saṁvejanīyaṁ nimittam udgṛhya, punaḥ prasadanīyaṁ nimittam udgṛhṇīṣva |
Thus dwelling abundantly [in this practice], the not-doing of this harm of mine would be [the result].' Thus wisely apply yourself — thus, having taken up the urgency-generating sign, take up next the gladdening sign.
sbh3.2:47.1
evaṁ ca punarudgṛhṇīṣva | ātmanaḥ śīlāni pratyavekṣasva |
And again, take it up thus — examine your own virtues:
sbh3.2:47.2
'kiṁ pariśuddhāni me śīlānyapariśuddhāni vā — yo me smṛtisaṁpramoṣādvā, anādarādvā, kleśapracuratayā vā, avyutpannato vā asti kaścicchikṣāvyatikramaḥ |
'Are my precepts pure, or impure? Is there any transgression of training — through forgetfulness, lack of respect, abundance of defilement, or by being uninstructed?
sbh3.2:47.3
vyatikrānte vā me, mayā śikṣāṁ yathādharmaṁ pratikṛtya, adhyāśayena ca punarakaraṇāya cittamutpāditaṁ |
Or, having transgressed, have I made amends as the dharma directs, and with [right] intention generated the mental resolve not to do [it] again?
sbh3.2:47.4
kaccinme kartavyaṁ kṛtam, akartavyañca na kṛtaṁ — samāsataḥ | kaccidadhyāśayasaṁpanno asmi, prayogasaṁpannaśca — yaduta śikṣāpadeṣu |'
Have I done what should be done; have I left undone what should not be done, in brief? Am I one of accomplished resolve and accomplished engagement — namely, in the trainings?'
sbh3.2:47.5
evaṁ na te pratyavekṣamāṇena, sa cetpariśuddhaḥ śīlaskandhaḥ, na punaste cetanā karaṇīyā: 'kaccinme vipratisāra utpadyeta' — api tu dharmataiveyaṁ — yad evaṁ viśuddhaśīlasyāvipratisāra utpadyate |
Thus he should not [generate the volition]: 'May regret arise in me!' For when one is of pure virtue, by the nature of things, non-regret arises.
The 'volition-less arising' principle — central to Yogācāra meditation psychology. Non-regret, joy, gladness, and mind-delight should not be willed into being; they arise by the nature of things (dharmatā) once the prior condition (pure virtue → non-regret → joy → gladness → mind-delight) is in place. The chain is: pure śīla → avipratisāra → prāmodya → praharṣa → prīti-manaskatā. Each step is dharmatā-arising, not effortful production.
sbh3.2:47.6
evaṁ cāvipratisāriṇā na cetanā karaṇīyā: 'kaccinme prāmodyam utpadyeta' — api tu dharmataiveyaṁ yad avipratisāriṇaḥ prāmodyam utpadyate |
Likewise, for one without regret, the volition should not be made: 'May joy arise in me!' For by the nature of things, joy arises for the non-regretting one.
sbh3.2:47.7
anena tāvad ekena prāmodyādhiṣṭhānena dvayāvipratisārapūrvakaṁ prāmodyam utpādayitavyam |
By this one base of gladdening, joy preceded by the twofold non-regret is to be generated.
sbh3.2:47.8
utpādya pareṇa saṁpraharṣādhiṣṭhānena mānasaṁ saṁpraharṣaya |
Having generated [joy], by another base of further gladdening — gladden the mind.
sbh3.2:47.9
sa cetpunarbhavasi pūrveṇāparaṁ parīttasyāpi viśeṣādhigame — prītirjanayitavyā: 'bhavyo aham asmi, pratibalaḥ | evaṁ pariśuddhaśīlo bhagavataḥ śikṣāsu supratiṣṭhitaḥ |
If, then, you have, after a previous [attainment], become [one with] even a limited further distinctive attainment, joy is to be generated: 'I am capable, able. Thus, of pure virtue, well-established in the trainings of the Blessed One —
sbh3.2:47.10
dṛṣṭe dharme prāptasya prāptaye, anadhigatasyādhigamāya, asākṣātkṛtasya sākṣātkriyāyai' — anenāpyadhiṣṭhānena mānasaṁ saṁpraharṣaya |
in this very life, for the attainment of what was not yet attained, the attainment of the unrealized, the realization of the not-yet-realized.' By this base too, gladden the mind.
sbh3.2:47.11
sa cetpunarlābhī bhavati pūrveṇāparaṁ paracittasyāpi viśeṣādhigamasya — sa tvaṁ tam adhipatiṁ kṛtvā pareṣāṁ ca paripūrṇe viśeṣādhigame, yaduta tathāgate, tathāgataśrāvakeṣu vā, ātmanaścottariviśeṣādhigamasaṁpratyayajāto mānasaṁ saṁpraharṣaya iti |
And if you have, after a previous [attainment], also become a gainer of distinctive attainment in others' minds — then, making that adhipati, and [recognizing] the complete distinctive attainment in others (namely, the Tathāgata and the Tathāgata's disciples), with confidence in your own further distinctive attainment, gladden the mind.
sbh3.2:47.12
ya ebhirākārairmānasaḥ — sa praharṣa iti |
Whatever mental [delight] arises from these modes — this is praharṣa (gladness).
sbh3.2:47.13
ya ebhirākāraiḥ sa pūrvapramuditasyaitarhi prītimanaskatetyucyate |
Whatever [delight arises] from these modes — this is called prīti-manaskatā (mind-delightedness) of the previously-gladdened one.
sbh3.2:48.1
evaṁ prasadanīyaṁ nimittam udgrāhayatyudgrāhayitvā hi punaḥ samanuśāsti —
Thus having taken up the gladdening-sign, [the teacher] again admonishes:
sbh3.2:48.2
'ehi tvaṁ, bhadramukha — saṁvejanīyena nimittena saṁtāpitacittaḥ, prasadanīyena cittenābhiṣyanditacittaḥ — prahāyābhidhyādaurmanasyaṁ loke bahulaṁ vihariṣyasi |
'Come, dear one. With mind heated by the urgency-generating sign, with mind suffused by the gladdening-sign — having abandoned covetousness and dejection in the world, you shall abide abundantly.
sbh3.2:48.3
yatra ca yatrālambane prayokṣyase — śamathapakṣe vā vipaśyanāpakṣe vā — tatra tatrālambane cittaṁ sthitaṁ bhaviṣyati |
Wherever you engage in the object — on the śamatha-side or the vipaśyanā-side — there, on that object, the mind shall stand.
sbh3.2:48.4
adhyātmaṁ susaṁsthitaṁ, kāyacittapraśrabdhicittaikāgratāśca pratilapsyase |'
Inwardly well-established, you shall attain body-serenity, mind-serenity, mind-one-pointedness.'
sbh3.2:48.5
evaṁ kṛṣṇapakṣavimukhībhūtaḥ śuklapakṣābhimukhībhūtasya yaduta saṁvegābhiṣyandanatayā — sarvaṁ punarasyādīnavanimittam udgṛhṇīṣva — yaduta nimittebhyo vipakṣebhyaścopakleśebhyaśca |
Thus, for one turned away from the dark side and turned toward the bright side — namely, through urgency and suffusion — take up next the danger-sign (ādīnava-nimitta): from the signs, from the opposed-side, and from the defilements.
The threefold sign-system the yogī presents to the ādikarmika: (1) saṁvejanīya (urgency) was the aśubhā application of the four saṁvejanīya-sthāna; (2) prasadanīya (gladdening) was the recollection of Three Jewels + śīla + ātmano-adhigama-saṁbhāvanā; (3) ādīnava (danger) is the three-fold analysis of how signs / vitarka / defilements harm the mind. Following these, the āloka-nimitta (luminous-sign) is given in paragraph 49 and the vasturūpaṇa-nimitta (substance-image) implicitly governs the entire engagement with the meditation object.
sbh3.2:48.6
tatra nimittāni rūpanimittādīni daśa, vitarkāḥ kāmavitarkādayo 'ṣṭau, upakleśāḥ kāmacchandādayaḥ pañca |
There: signs are the ten beginning with form-sign; vitarkas are the eight beginning with sense-desire-vitarka; defilements are the five beginning with sense-desire.
sbh3.2:48.7
evaṁ ca punasteṣv ādīnavam udgṛhṇīṣva — 'itīmāni nimittāni vyāpārakārakāṇi cittasya | itīme vitarkā unmuktasaṁkṣobhakārakāś cittasya | itīme upakleśā anupaśamakārakāś cittasya |
And of these, take up the danger thus: 'These signs are bustle-makers for the mind. These vitarkas are agitation-makers, loosing the mind. These defilements are non-calmness-makers for the mind.
sbh3.2:48.8
yaśca cittasya vyāpāro nimittakṛtaḥ | yaścon-muktasaṁkṣobho vitarkakṛtaḥ | yaścānupakleśa upakleśakṛtaḥ — duḥkhāvihāra eṣa cittasya |
Whatever bustle of the mind made by signs, whatever loosing-agitation made by vitarka, whatever non-pacification made by defilement — this is the painful-dwelling of the mind.
sbh3.2:48.9
tasmād ime nimittavitarkopakleśāḥ duḥkhā anāryā anarthopasaṁhitāś cittavikṣepasaṁkṣobhakarāḥ' iti |
Therefore these signs, vitarkas, defilements are painful, ignoble, harm-bringing, mind-scattering and agitating.'
sbh3.2:48.10
evaṁ ādīnavanimittam udgṛhya cittaikāgratāyāṁ, cittasthitau, cittāvikṣepe ṣaḍbhirākārair nimittam udgṛhāṇa — yaduta nimittasaṁjñayā nirnimitte, avyāpārasaṁjñayā, nirvikalpasaṁjñayā, nirvikalpe cānautsukyāsaṁkṣobhasaṁjñayā, upaśamasaṁjñayā, upaśamena niṣparidāha-nairvṛtya-aśubhasaṁjñayā |
Thus having taken up the danger-sign, [practicing] mental one-pointedness, mental abiding, mental non-scattering, take up the sign through six modes: by sign-perception with respect to the signless, by non-bustle perception, by conception-less perception, in the conception-less also by non-eagerness, non-agitation perception, by upaśama (calm) perception, [and] by upaśama-induced, burning-free, peace-of-aśubhā perception.
sbh3.2:49.1
evaṁ nimittam udgṛhya punaraparaṁ cālokanimittam udgṛhāṇa — yaduta pradīpādvā, agniskandhaprabhāsādvā, sūryamaṇḍalādvā, candramaṇḍalādvā nimittam udgṛhya, śmaśānādyupasaṁkramya, vinīlakādvā nimittam udgṛhāṇa,
Thus having taken up the [danger] sign, take up next the āloka-nimitta (luminous-sign): from a lamp, from the brightness of a fire-mass, from the sun-disk, from the moon-disk — having taken up the sign; or approaching a charnel-ground, take up the sign from a livid corpse,
sbh3.2:49.2

sbh3.2:49.3

Having taken up [the sign], approach the sitting-place — go to a forest, the foot of a tree, or an empty hut; sit on a couch, a seat, or a grass-mat.

And there, let the awareness-of-distraction-and-non-distraction be present — by which awareness, in whichever signs, vitarkas, defilements [arise], you know distraction; and in mental one-pointedness, accompanied by the cultivation of the six perceptions, [know] non-distraction.

And there, attentive to distraction-and-non-distraction, be such that your continuum and mental flow — bound to one-pointedness, bound inwardly to cetaḥ-śamatha — proceeds successively, signless, conception-less, calm.

If, due to forgetfulness, when the mind has reached śamatha, signs, vitarkas, defilements appear (through the fault of non-practice), [they] show pleasure, make themselves [your] object —
sbh3.2:49.9

in those that arise and arise, non-attention from non-mindfulness should be made — namely, making the previously-seen [tendency] adhipati; that object is then vividly-realized by the recollection-non-attention, and is established as untrusted, gone to non-appearance.
sbh3.2:49.10

Dear one, this is the subtle object — hard for [you] to penetrate. For your penetration, sharp desire and exertion are to be made.
sbh3.2:50.1

This object the Blessed One signified [in the following discourse]:
sbh3.2:50.2

'Bhikkhus, [crying] ‘Country-beauty! Country-beauty!’ — a great crowd of people would assemble.
The janapadakalyāṇī (country-beauty) parable — a canonical Pali parallel exists at SN 47.20 Janapadakalyāṇī Sutta. Asaṅga gives the yogācāra allegorical reading: country-beauty = defilement-obsessions; great gathering = ten signs; oil-vessel = śamatha-bound mind; sword-raised executioner = the danger-sign just taken up. The parable enacts the satyābhyavaṣṭabdha-prayogatā (anchored-engagement) introduced at the start of the 9-fold prayoga framework.
sbh3.2:50.3
atha puruṣa āgacched abālajātīyaḥ | taṁ kaścidevaṁ vadet:
Then a man would come, not of foolish kind. To him someone would say:

‘Sir man, [carry] this oil-vessel full to the brim, not to be sprinkled, between [yourself] and the assembled crowd. That country-beauty [is to be seen] between, with the great gathering — be careful!

'No, no, Bhadanta! Why so? Because by that man the sword-raised execution-man — behind, behind, following — has been seen.

He would think: ‘If I drop even a single drop from this oil-vessel onto the ground, then this sword-raised execution-man will cut down my head at the root!’
sbh3.2:50.9

Rather, that man — not attending to the country-beauty or the great gathering — would carry just that oil-vessel with the whole mind brought to bear, and would [carry] it rightly.'

'Just so, bhikkhus — those who, having venerated the four foundations of mindfulness, cultivate them, having esteemed them, brought the whole mind to bear — these are my disciples.'

There, 'country-beauty' is the [Buddha's] name for dharmas standing as the obscuring-by-defilements beginning with sense-desire.
sbh3.2:50.12

'The supreme dance-song-music' is the name for dharmas standing as agitation-by-vitarka-proliferation.
sbh3.2:50.13

'The great gathering' is the name for the ten signs beginning with form-sign.
sbh3.2:50.14

'The man not of foolish kind' is the name for the yogācāra.
sbh3.2:50.15

'The oil-vessel' is the name for the mind bound up in śamatha — by virtue of its anointing of body-and-mind with serenity.
sbh3.2:50.16
'utkṣiptāsiko vadhakapuruṣa' iti nimittavitarkopakleśeṣu pūrvodgṛhītasyādīnasya etadadhivacanaṁ |
'The sword-raised execution-man' is the name for the danger previously taken up with respect to signs, vitarkas, defilements.
sbh3.2:50.17

'Carries [it] venerated, and does not drop even one drop onto the ground' — this is the name for the śamatha-path held by the awareness of distraction-and-non-distraction — by which one continually causes the entire mind-stream, mind-flow to proceed, signless, conception-less, calm, by the power of energy, in succession.

And he does not produce even one mind taking sign-as-object or vitarka-or-defilement-as-object.

Thus the yogī admonishes the ādikarmika engaged in śamatha:

'So long, dear one, as your mind — engaged in the śamatha-path, accompanied by mindfulness-and-clear-comprehension, accompanied by delight — is held by [proper] means, so long should you cultivate the śamatha-path itself.

If, due to the fault of non-practice, [the mind] does not delight, and the object is with means — then, having risen from the conception-less object, make [the mind] bound up in mindfulness on a conception-laden object.

That very aśubhā-sign you previously took up — that very thing, attend to it from the start by a vipaśyanā that follows only the sign — namely, the livid-corpse, or the pus-laden, up through the skeleton.

And thus engaged, from the first resolve on one livid-corpse, up through one skeleton.
sbh3.2:51.6

The multiplication-sequence from one corpse to immeasurable corpses — a Yogācāra elaboration of the Pali aśubhā meditation. Each step of the resolve (adhimokṣa) extends the imagined object outward until 'all directions and intermediate-directions' are filled without gap. The result-sign is a luminous (prabhāsvara) resolve in which real-attention (bhūta-manaskāra) follows. Same multiplication-pattern is used for maitrī (54.6) and other practices.

of which there is no space, not even the size of the tip of a staff [unfilled].

As with the livid-corpses, so up through the skeletons — all in this way: relying on resolve-attention, descend to real-attention.

And descend again thus: 'As many of these livid-corpses I have resolved on, up through skeletons — but immeasurably more for me, beginning from the prior limit, in those-and-those rebirths, deaths, and re-takings of birth, the livid-corpses produced from the dead, the time-gone, up through skeletons produced —
sbh3.2:51.10

of those returning, whose prior limit is not discerned: if anyone were to collect them, and the collected ones not perish, not putrefy — there is no earth-region where they would have room.
sbh3.2:51.11

[Even] those of a single kalpa, up through skeletons — if anyone were a gatherer, the heap of the gathered would be like a vast mountain-side.
sbh3.2:51.12

Just as for the prior limit, so beginning from the prior limit also the further limit — up through ‘I shall make an end of suffering!’ Thus indeed you should engage.'
sbh3.2:51.13

Relying on attention, you shall descend into real-attention.
sbh3.2:51.14

Having calmed the mind inwardly, calm and release [the object]. Then, afterwards, the immeasurable livid-corpses, immeasurable up through skeletons — inwardly, by mental contraction, [the practitioner] vividly-realizes [them], establishing [them] in [a state] gone to non-appearance.

And he does not release those signs, nor does he conceptualize the conception-laden ones — otherwise, [he] establishes [the mind] only on that object as signless, conception-less, calm.

'Dear one, that āloka-nimitta you previously took up — that, too, attend to in śamatha-side engagement and vipaśyanā-side engagement. With mind accompanied by light, accompanied by brightness, luminous, free of darkness, cultivate śamatha-vipaśyanā.

If from the very beginning it is clear, abundant in appearance — it shall by greater measure go to clearer-and-clearer and more-abundant-in-appearance.'

You, with the urgency-sign well taken up, the gladdening-sign, the śamatha-sign, the vipaśyanā-sign, the āloka-sign well taken up, from time to time inwardly calming the mind, from time to time discerning dharmas — by a vipaśyanā that follows only the sign, descend into the foundations of mindfulness.
sbh3.2:52.6
yad utāśubhāprayogamevādhipatiṁ kṛtvā, evaṁ ca punarvicinvan bahirdhā ṣaṭtriṁśato dravyāṇi kāyāt keśādi-prasāvaparyantāntaṁ nimittam udgṛhya, adhyātmamapy etāni sarvāṇy aśucidravyāṇy adhimucyādhyātmaṁ cittaṁ saṁśamaya —

The 36 aśuci-dravya — the 36 unattractive substances of the body, taken as the aśubhā basis. From this base, the four foundations of mindfulness are established in turn: (1) body-as-body inwardly (own body, 52.7); (2) body-as-body externally (others, 52.8-9); (3) inwardly-and-externally (52.10-11); (4) feelings/mind/dharmas through the four formless aggregates analyzed in the three modes (śamatha/distraction/vipaśyanā) (52.12-19). This is the practical-instruction synthesis of aśubhā (the carita-viśodhana practice at sbh2.2) with the four foundations of mindfulness (the framework developed at sbh2.0 ¶¶68-88).

This shall be for you the inward body-as-body contemplation — namely, beginning from one's own inward body.

The friend-enemy-neutral system in maitrī is the standard Yogācāra elaboration of the Pali brahmavihāra. The three parties model the practitioner's reactive structure: friends evoke easy maitrī (no obstacle); enemies evoke the obstacle of resistance; neutrals evoke the obstacle of indifference. Cultivating equal maitrī across the three trains the mind to produce maitrī regardless of object-class — the prerequisite for the immeasurable-extension.

These beings are perverted — through the cause of perversion, in here-and-now and in saṁparāya, in feelings, in the becoming-of-individual-existence, they crave; and craving, having done birth-rooted actions, in the future [with] karma-and-defilement as cause [.......] solely engender pain —
sbh3.2:55.3
evaṁ nimittam udgṛhyādhyātmam adhimucyate: 'ayamapi kevalo duḥkhaskandha evam eva saṁbhūta' iti |
thus having taken up the sign, he resolves inwardly: 'This too is simply the pain-aggregate, just so come into being.'
sbh3.2:55.4
ye cātmabhāvā nānantāḥ paryantāḥ pūrvāntam ārabhya yeṣām ādireva na prajñāyate, te 'pyevaṁbhūtāḥ — eṣām api sattvānām atītānāgatapratyutpannāḥ sarva evātmabhāvā duḥkhaskandhasaṁgṛhītā evam evābhinirvṛttāḥ | āyatyāṁ notpadyante |
And [as for] those individual existences — innumerable, limitless, beginning from the prior limit, whose beginning is not discerned — they too are thus. The past-future-present individual existences of these beings — all are gathered under the pain-aggregate, just so come into being. They do not arise in the future.
sbh3.2:55.5
sa khalvayam idaṁpratyayatāpratītyasamutpādamanaskāraḥ sarvabhūtamanaskāra eva, nāstyādhimokṣikaḥ — yadi na punarātmano vartamānān skandhān pratītyasamutpannān manasi karoti |
And this idaṁ-pratyayatā pratītyasamutpāda attention is itself simply real-attention — there is no [other] adhimokṣa-attention — provided he does not attend to his own present skandhas as dependently-arisen.
sbh3.2:55.6
tadādhyātmaṁ kāye yāvad dharmeṣu dharmānudarśī viharati |
[When he does,] there inwardly in the body, up through the dharmas, he abides as a dharma-contemplator.
sbh3.2:55.7
yadā ca punaḥ pareṣāṁ vartamānān skandhān pratītyasamutpannān manasi karoti, tadādhyātmabahirdhā kāye yāvad dharmeṣu dharmānudarśī viharati |
And when he attends to others' present skandhas as dependently-arisen, then inwardly-and-externally in the body, up through the dharmas, he abides as a dharma-contemplator.
sbh3.2:55.8
yadātmanaśca pareṣāṁ cātītānāgatān skandhān pratītyasamutpannān manasi karoti, tadādhyātmabahirdhā kāye yāvad dharmeṣu dharmānudarśī viharati | śeṣaṁ pūrvavat |
When he attends to his own and others' past-future skandhas as dependently-arisen, then inwardly-and-externally in the body, up through the dharmas, he abides as a dharma-contemplator. The rest as before.
sbh3.2:55.9
tatra dhātuprabhedaprayogaprayukta ādikarmiko bahirdhā pṛthivīkāṭhinyanimittam udgṛhya — tadyathā bhū-parvata-tṛṇa-vana-śarkara-kaṭhilla-maṇi-mukti-vaiḍūrya-śilā-pravālādikebhyaścādhyātmaṁ kāṭhinyam adhimucyate |
There, the ādikarmika engaged in dhātuprabheda-engagement — having taken up externally the earth-hardness-sign — namely, from earth, mountain, grass, forest, gravel, clods, jewels, pearls, lapis-lazuli, rock, coral, and so on — resolves on hardness inwardly.
sbh3.2:55.10
bahirdhā apsv abdhātor nimittam udgṛhya — tadyathā nadī-prasravaṇa-taḍāga-kūpādibhyaḥ; tathā mahato agniskandhasya abdhau vā ādityakiraṇasaṁtāpitā bhūrāviṣṭebhyo vā sarvebhyaḥ; udārāgnisaṁpratāpitebhyo vā prasrayebhyaśca — no bahirdhā vāyuskandhāt pūrvadakṣiṇa-paścimottarebhyo vāyubhyo yāvadvāyumaṇḍalebhyaḥ;
Having taken up externally the water-element-sign — namely, from river, spring, pond, well, and the rest; likewise from a great mass of fire, [present] in water, or from earth heated by sun-rays, or from all greatly fire-heated [places], or from rivulets; not externally from the wind-mass, from east-south-west-north winds up through wind-disks;
sbh3.2:55.11
ye deśā astyādes tāraṇīyā vāyugatena sacchidrāḥ, suśirāḥ, sāvakāśāḥ — tasmād ākāśadhātor nimittam udgṛhṇātyadhyātmam abdhātuṁ, tejodhātuṁ, vāyudhātum, ākāśadhātum adhimucyate, śrutacintādhipateyena ca parikalpitena |
those places fit for traversal that are wind-affected, with holes, hollow, with space — therefrom he takes up the space-element-sign — and inwardly resolves on water-element, fire-element, wind-element, space-element, by the imagined [object] dominated by hearing-and-reflection.
sbh3.2:55.12
evaṁ vijñānadhātor nimittam udgṛhṇāti — cakṣur ādhyātmikam āyatanam aparibhinnaṁ cedbhavati, rūpam ābhāsagataṁ, na ca tajjo manaskāraḥ pratyupasthito bhavati, na tajjasya cakṣurvijñānasya prādurbhāvo bhavati — viparyayād bhavati | evaṁ yāvanmanodharmān manovijñānaṁ veditavyam |
Thus he takes up the consciousness-element-sign — when the inward sense-base of the eye is unimpaired, form having reached appearance, and the attention born of that is present, then the arising of eye-consciousness from that occurs; in the opposite [case, it does not]. Likewise up through mental dharmas, mind-consciousness is to be known.
sbh3.2:55.13
evaṁ nimittam udgṛhyāpyeṣāṁ sarveṣāṁ vijñānānām asmin kāye cāturmahābhūtike bījaṁ, dhātur, gotraṁ, prakṛtir ityadhimucyate |
Thus, having taken up the sign of all these consciousnesses, [he resolves] in this fourfold-great-element body: 'The seed, element, lineage, nature [is this body]' — thus he resolves.
sbh3.2:55.14
tānyetāni catvāri mahābhūtāni tatprathamato aṅgapratyaṅgagatato arthaṁ vināpyadhimucyate; tataḥ paścāt sūkṣmatarāvayava-prabhedān adhimucyate; evaṁ yāvad agatāyanapraviṣṭas truṭi-samatayā; evaṁ yāvac chanaiḥ śanaiḥ paramāṇuśo 'dhimucyate |
These four great elements, first, even apart from the limb-and-sub-limb meaning, he resolves; then afterwards, the subtler component-divisions; up through reached the limit of [analyzability], with the equality of split; up through gradually, atom-by-atom, he resolves.
sbh3.2:55.15
sa ekaikam aṅgāvayavapramāṇa-paramāṇu-saṁcaya-sanniviṣṭam adhimucyate; kaḥ punarvādaḥ sarvakāyamayam |
He resolves [the body] as a collection of atoms equal in measure to each limb-sub-limb. What need to mention the whole body?
sbh3.2:55.16
ayaṁ dhātuprabhedaprayuktasya cārthaprabhedaparyantaḥ rūpiṇāṁ tāvad-dhātūnām, ākāśadhātoḥ punaḥ — yatpunarasya tasmin prayoge śamathavipaśyanābhāvanāyāṁ vikṣepāvikṣepaparijñāvadhānam — idam asya saṁprajanyasya smṛtisamatāyāś ca |
This is, for the dhātuprabheda-engaged one, the limit of meaning-distinction — for the form-element-elements, but for the space-element too — and the awareness of distraction-and-non-distraction in this engagement with śamatha-vipaśyanā cultivation: this is his [practice of] saṁprajanya and mindfulness-equality.
sbh3.2:56.1
yatpunaḥ saṁveganimittaṁ, prasadanīyaṁ ca nimittaṁ sūdgṛhītaṁ bhavati — idam asyābhidhyādaurmanasya-vinayasya |
And again, that the urgency-sign and the gladdening-sign are well taken up — this is for him the [practice of] abhidhyā-daurmanasya-vinaya (training-down-of-covetousness-and-dejection).
sbh3.2:56.2
tasyaivam ātāpino viharato yāvad dvitīyaloke abhidhyādaurmanasyaṁ pūrvam eva samyakprayogasamārambhakāle |
For him thus dwelling ardent, up through the second world's covetousness-and-dejection [is overcome] — already at the time of beginning right-engagement.
sbh3.2:56.3
sūkṣmacittapraśrabdhir durupalabhyā pravarttate — yā tatra śamathamvā bhāvayato, vipaśyanāmvā prasvasthacittatā, prasvasthakāyatā, cittakāyakarmaṇyatā | iyam atra kāyacittapraśrabdhiḥ |
A subtle mind-serenity, hard to obtain, proceeds — whichever inward calm-of-mind, calm-of-body, mind-body-workability is there for one cultivating either śamatha or vipaśyanā. This here is kāya-citta-praśrabdhi.
sbh3.2:56.4
tasya saiva sūkṣmā cittaikāgratā cittakāyapraśrabdhiścābhivardhamānā audārikāṁ sūpalakṣyāṁ cittaikāgratāṁ kāyapraśrabdhim āvahati — yad uta hetupāraṁparyādānayogena |
His very subtle mind-one-pointedness and mind-body-serenity, increasing, brings about a gross, easily-observable mind-one-pointedness and body-serenity — namely, by the method of complete-conformity of cause-pāramitā.
sbh3.2:56.5
na tasya, na cirasyedānīm audārikī cittakāyapraśrabdhiścittaikāgratā ca sūpalakṣyotpatsyatīti — yāvad asyāḥ pūrvanimittaṁ pūrva-nirgaurava-pratibhāsam utpadyate — na caitad bādhalakṣaṇam |
Not long from now, the gross mind-body-serenity and mind-one-pointedness, easily-observable, will arise — when, prior to that, a non-respect-appearance, a prior sign of it, arises. And this is not a hindrance-characteristic.
sbh3.2:56.6
tasyānantarotpādād yat prahāṇaratir-vibandhakāri-kleśapakṣyaṁ cittadauṣṭhulyaṁ tat prahīyate, tatpratipakṣeṇa ca cittakarmaṇyatā cittapraśrabdhir utpadyate |
From its proximate arising — whatever mental-dauṣṭhulya, hindering of delight-in-abandonment, on the defilement-side — is abandoned. By its antidote, mind-workability and mind-serenity arise.
sbh3.2:56.7
tasyotpādāt kāyapraśrabdhyutpādānukūlāni vāyūr-dhva-muktāni mahābhūtāni kāye 'vakramanti | teṣām avakramaṇahetoryat kāyadauṣṭhulyaṁ tad vigacchati |
By its arising, the great-elements favorable to body-serenity-arising — released upward by wind — descend into the body. By the cause of their descent, the body-dauṣṭhulya departs.
sbh3.2:56.8
prahāṇaratirivaddakṛtakleśāpakṣyakāyapraśrabdhyā ca tatpratipakṣikayā sarvakāyaḥ pūryate | syāt ............ dhyāti |
By delight-in-abandonment-like, [body-]produced, defilement-side-free body-serenity — its antidote — the whole body is filled. [Description corrupted; partial reading]
sbh3.2:56.9
tataḥ prathamopanipāte cittauḍḍilyaṁ, cittadauṣṭhulyaṁ, cittasumanaskāraprāmodyasahagatālambanasābhirāmatā ca cittasya tasmin samaye khyāti |
Then, at the first attainment, mind-effervescence, [diminution of] mind-dauṣṭhulya, and accompanied-by-glad-attention-and-pre-joy delight-in-object — these become manifest in the mind at that time.
sbh3.2:56.10
tasyordhvaṁ yo 'sau tatprathamopanipātī praśrabdhivegaḥ — sa śanaiḥ śanaiḥ pariślathataro bhavati |
Upward of that, whatever first-attainment serenity-surge there is — that gradually becomes more slack.
sbh3.2:56.11
chāyevānugatā praśrabdhiḥ kāye ca pravarttate | yac ca tad auddhatyaṁ cetasas tad apyavahīyate |
Shadow-like, accompanying serenity proceeds in the body. And whatever agitation of the mind there is — that too subsides.
sbh3.2:56.12
praśāntākāracittasālambane śamatho yat-tac-cayas-saḥ pravarttate |
On the calm-mode mind-object — śamatha — that accumulation proceeds.
sbh3.2:56.13
tata ūrdhvam ayaṁ yogī ādikarmikaḥ samanaskāro bhavati — 'samanaskāra' iti ca saṁkhyāṁ gacchati |
Above that, this beginner-yogī becomes samanaskāra (one-with-attention) — 'samanaskāra' he is reckoned.
Samanaskāra — the ādikarmika-yogī who has obtained attention. This is the structural milestone that the entire ādikarmika-tutorial has been preparing for. The English keeps the Sanskrit term because of its technical specificity: sa-manaskāra literally 'one-possessing-attention,' marking the transition from pre-attention preparation into the concentrated-stage proper. The signs at 56.15-22 are the diagnostic markers — limited but observable body-serenity, swift-onset śamatha, weakened defilement-arising, alms-round attention preserved. From this point the practitioner is ready for the seven attentions of sbh4.1.
sbh3.2:56.14
tatkasya hetoḥ — rūpārthānurodhena samāhitabhūmiko manaskāraḥ parīttastaprathamataḥ pratilabdho bhavati | tenocyate samanaskāra iti |
Why so? Because, in conformity with the form-meaning, concentrated-stage attention, limited [in scope] from the first, has been attained. Hence he is called samanaskāra.
sbh3.2:56.15
tasyāsya samanaskārasya ādikarmikasyemāni liṅgāni bhavanti — parīttam anena rūpāvacaraṁ cittaṁ pratilabdhaṁ bhavati |
Of this samanaskāra ādikarmika, these are the signs (liṅgāni): a limited form-realm-pertaining mind has been attained by him.
sbh3.2:56.16
parīttā kāyapraśrabdhiś cittapraśrabdhiś cittaikāgratā — bhavyo bhavati pratibalaḥ |
Limited [are his] body-serenity, mind-serenity, mind-one-pointedness — he becomes capable, able.
sbh3.2:56.17
kleśaviśodhanālambanaḥ prayoge 'sya, snigvāmā cāsya cittasantatiḥ pravarttate | śamathopagūḍhāccaritaṁ tadānena viśodhitaṁ bhavati |
His engagement [is] with the defilement-purifying object, his mind-continuum proceeds with affection. The conduct that was hidden-by-śamatha is thereby purified.
sbh3.2:56.18
sa cedrañjanīye viṣaye carati — na tīvraṁ rāgaparyavasthānam utpādayati | alpamātrakeṇāvaramātrakeṇa ca, pratipakṣasanniśrayeṇa ābhogamātrakeṇāśakto atiprativiśodhayituṁ |
If he ranges in a lust-arousing object, he does not generate intense lust-obsession. By a minimal, limited [effort], with [defilement-]antidote-support and merely by intention, the [obsession] is impossible to overcome [further].
sbh3.2:56.19
yathā rañjanīye, evaṁ dveṣanīye, mohanīye, mānasthānīye, vitarkasthānīye veditavyam |
As with lust-arousing, so with aversion-arousing, delusion-arousing, conceit-stationed, vitarka-stationed [objects] — to be known.
sbh3.2:56.20
niṣaṇṇasya cāsya pratisaṁlayane cittaṁ pratidadhatas tvaritatvaritaṁ cittaṁ praśrabhyate, kāyaśca |
And when he sits in seclusion, his mind set [on the object], swiftly, swiftly the mind becomes serene, and the body [too].
sbh3.2:56.21
kāyadauṣṭhulyāni ca nātyarthaṁ bādhante | na cātyarthaṁ nivaraṇasamudācāro bhavati | na cātyarthamutkaṇṭhā-ratiparitamanāsahagatāḥ saṁjñāmanasikārāḥ samudācaranti |
Body-dauṣṭhulyas do not afflict [him] excessively. There is no excessive hindrance-flow. There is no excessive longing-and-displeasure-accompanied perception-or-attention-flow.
sbh3.2:56.22
vyutthitasyāpi manaso 'pi carataḥ — praśrabdhi-mātrā kācic citte, kāye, citte cānugatā bhavati ityevaṁbhāgīyāni samanaskārasya ādikarmikasya liṅgāni nimittānyavadātāni veditavyāni ||
Even of one risen [from concentration], even of one moving about, some measure of serenity in the mind, in the body, accompanied by [the residue], is present. — Such kinds of signs of the samanaskāra ādikarmika, clear marks, are to be known.
