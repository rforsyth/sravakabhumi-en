The Vipaśyanā Method

Śrāvakabhūmi · Sanskrit (Shukla 1973) ↔ English (1 rendering)
← All topicsView English only →

The fourfold vipaśyanā (insight) method — vicinoti (investigates), pravicinoti (thoroughly investigates), parivitarkayati (reflects upon), parimīmāṁsām āpadyate (engages in full reflection). The chapter unfolds the framework through three gateways (savikalpa-pratibimba, nirvikalpa-pratibimba, vastu-paryantatā), six investigation-subjects (meaning, things, characteristics, sides, time, reasoning), and four reasonings (dependence, function, demonstration, real nature). Five illustrations apply the method to specific objects: aśubhā, maitrī, idaṁ-pratyayatā, dhātu-prabheda, ānāpānasmṛti — the first two are translated; the remaining three are present in Sanskrit but not yet rendered in English.

Vicinoti → pravicinoti → parivitarkayati → parimīmāṁsām āpadyate — the same four prajñā-function verbs that appear throughout the Śrāvakabhūmi. The progression moves from coarse to fine inquiry: general examination → close discrimination → deliberation → thorough critical examination. The accumulation conveys that vipaśyanā is sustained, multi-aspected investigation rather than a single act of looking.

ye tāvadatītamadhvānamupādāya sukhakāmāḥ sattvāḥ te atītāḥ, teṣāṁ kiṁ punaḥ sukhopasaṁhāraṁ kariṣyāmaḥ |
'Those happiness-desiring beings, taking past time — they have passed away. To whom shall we now bring happiness?'
sbh3.2:24.3
ye punarvartamānāḥ sattvāste vartamānamadhvānamupādāya |
'But those beings of present time — taking present time,'
sbh3.2:24.4
yāvadanāgatādadhvano nityakālaṁ sukhino bhavantvityevaṁ maitryāḥ kālaṁ paryeṣate |
'taking future time — let them be at all times happy.' Thus he investigates the time of lovingkindness.
sbh3.2:25.1
sa punaḥ pravicinoti |
He investigates closely:
sbh3.2:25.2
nāsti kaścidātmā vā, sattvo vā ya eṣa sukhakāmo vā syāt |
'There is no self, no being, who would be the desirer-of-happiness,'
sbh3.2:25.3
yasya vā sukhamupasaṁhriyate |
'or to whom happiness would be brought.'
sbh3.2:25.4
api tu skandhamātrametat saṁskāramātrakametad yatraiṣā saṁjñā saṁjñaptirvyavahāraḥ |
'Rather, this is mere aggregate, mere accumulation of formations, wherein this notion, designation, convention is applied.'
sbh3.2:25.5
te punaḥ saṁskārāḥ karmakleśahetukā ityevamapekṣāyuktyā maitrīm paryeṣate |
'And these formations are caused by *karma* and *kleśa*.' Thus by dependence-reasoning he investigates lovingkindness.
sbh3.2:25.6
prasiddhadharmatā khalveṣā acintyadharmatā yan maitrīvyāpādabhāvanā prahāṇāya saṁvartata ityevaṁ dharmatāyuktyā maitrīm paryeṣate |
'It is an established nature-of-things, an unthinkable nature-of-things, that lovingkindness-cultivation conduces to the abandonment of ill-will.' Thus by nature-of-things reasoning he investigates lovingkindness.
The reasoning-investigation collapses maitrī into anātman. Even lovingkindness practice is to be done with full recognition that there is no self sending the maitrī and no self receiving it — only aggregates conditioned by karma and kleśa. The Yogācāra distinctive: maitrī is not at odds with emptiness; the deepest maitrī is maitrī informed by anātman.
