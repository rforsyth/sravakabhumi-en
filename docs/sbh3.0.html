Approaching the Teacher

Śrāvakabhūmi · Sanskrit (Shukla 1973) ↔ English (1 rendering)
← All topicsView English only →

The opening preamble of Yogasthāna III — the practitioner-teacher relationship that must be in place before the nine-stage śamatha of sbh3.1 can begin. Covers the qualifications of a kalyāṇamitra who actually knows the yoga (yoga-jña) and the four roles such a teacher plays; the teacher's examination of the practitioner by lineage / faculty / conduct; the daily routine the disciple takes up under instruction; and the kāya-viveka / citta-viveka (bodily and mental withdrawal) that prepare the ground for the dhyānas. Closes with a fourfold definition of samādhi that grounds the cittasthiti analysis at sbh3.1.

Kṣaya-vastu — literally 'the destruction-substance' or 'object of cessation.' Rendered as 'cessation event' to capture the practice-experiential sense: in meditation, the cessation event is the moment when the mind becomes truly still and habitual subconscious-to-conscious cognitive activity drops away.
