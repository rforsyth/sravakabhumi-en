The conduct-purifying object for the person of delusion-conduct (2.1–2.3), and the shortest of the five. ¶1 defines it: in the three times, mere formation, mere dharma, mere thing, mere cause, mere effect, consistent with the reasonings; dharmas brought forth by dharmas alone, with no agent or experiencer (1.2–1.7). The practice on this object is set out in sbh3.2 (¶26 and ¶55).

What is this-conditionality dependent origination?

In the three times, it is: mere formation, mere dharma, mere thing, mere cause, mere effect — consistent with reasoning, namely, by dependence-reasoning, by cause-and-effect reasoning, and by proof-establishing reasoning. [Lacuna] It is the bringing forth of dharmas by dharmas alone, and the absence of an agent or experiencer. This is called the this-conditionality-dependent-origination object.

Attending to this object, the person of delusion-conduct in whom delusion predominates abandons delusion, thins it, and purifies the mind from delusion-conduct.
